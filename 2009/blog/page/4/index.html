RECAP: Slim’s in San Francisco

April 27, 2010

Three years ago, Blue Scholars, Common Market, and Gabriel Teodros got together and did the first (and last) official MassLine tour.  When we made our San Francisco stop on that tour, the show was with Bambu at a venue called Pier 23.

I remember this show especially because it was 21+ and there were a handful of youth from the East Bay that had come out to see us and weren’t able to get in.  Instead of joining the festivities within the venue, the liquor laws of California forced them to watch the show from about 100 feet away behind a fence at a really awkward angle.

This was unacceptable.  The dedication these young folks exhibited had to be acknowledged and we needed to reciprocate it somehow!  Later that nigh,t we decided to call all our community contacts in the Bay area and find a venue where we could put on a last-minute daytime show before we went to L.A. that would be accessible to those of under-21 persuasion. Thankfully, we found a community center on Misson St. and put the word out to the little homies to come thru.

20 people showed up. It was the folks from the night before along with a bunch of Filipino kids from the Mission. The energy in that brightly lit building was unforgettable. Some of them joined us onstage, some of them rapped – including Rudy, the community center coordinator. We packed up afterwards, gave our daps and said our goodbyes, took the picture below, and continued with the rest of the tour.

Image not recovered

Fast-forward to Tuesday April 13, 2010:  I’m standing at the bar at Slim’s on 11th Street in SF catching up with an old high school friend of mine… and guess who walks up and taps me on the shoulder? It was a few of the East Bay youth that were at the community center show back in ’07, except now they were all grown up and had made it out to Slim’s to say wassup to Geo and I once again.

“Yo! Remember us from the center?!” That moment definitely made my night.

The same energy and feel that these East Bay cats displayed was echoed by everybody in the crowd that night at Slim’s.  At one point, I got a little too hype during the set and jumped to hang from a pole near the front of the stage.  My apologies to the people whose drinks I knocked over, fingers I stepped on, and faces I kicked.

She’s right.  I didn’t realize it until the Twizzler informed me.  Since then, we smoothed it out (and I think I’ve been forgiven) because everybody involved in that stunt considers the damage as a necessary risk of standing at the front of a Blue Scholars show.  I’m making an official promise on the blog to orJAAASmic and her friends to buy them whatever drinks they like next time we see each other in the Bay.  =)

-Sabzi

Macklemore Reviews Grynch’s ‘My Volvo’ Video

April 26, 2010

Blue Scholars has been in the media lately for hating on Space, but to tell you the truth, EVERYONE’S hating on Futuristic sh*t…Well, kind of.

Grynch released his video ‘My Volvo’ last week, a song that anyone who grew up driving their parent’s car can relate to. Drawing on memories of where the car has been, emotional experiences (“I cried in that car”), and car trouble (the video starts with the engine not turning over)–it’s an instant classic for Sabzi especially because the first car he drove was a Volvo.

Our boy Macklemore took it upon himself to carry on the tradition of local media and review this song for what it really was.  Thankfully, he had the courage to read between lines of Grynch’s song, interpret it, and draw conclusions so bold that lesser individuals might even call said conclusions baseless and uncalled for.  Is this irresponsible?  Nah.

” ‘Come back down to earth’ Grynch seems to be saying ‘and I’ll give you a ride to Ballard High School, where there are no aliens. It’s better here.’”

If anybody has been paying attention to the media in Seattle, they know that it is the duty of those who write reviews (that the whole Town apparently reads) to 1) make assumptions, 2) avoid actually speaking to the artist they’re reviewing, and 3) state their opinion as hard fact regardless of how misleading and divisive it may be.  Some may call this careless, foolhardy, or downright unprofessional.  Not us.  We consider it extremely intelligent and arguably heroic.  Kudos to you Macklemore, kudos.

The best part is the asterisk at the bottom which reads, “*Grynch didn’t say any of this, but it makes for a better story.” Indeed!

Mack’s opinionated and subversive response to “Space Hate” is something that we can appreciate.  Read his review at BengalYucky.com and check out Grynch’s video below.

Co-Founder of Contrast Mag Surfs to ‘HI-808′

April 8, 2010

In case you’re not aware, Hawaii is the 50th state of the United States. Some people often forget this, caught up in the massiveness of the ‘mainland’; making visits to places such as Hawaii with romanticized views of easy-living/vacation-getaway/day-time nap business.

This is why we’re lucky to have publications such as Contrast Magazine, who believe that “the natural allure of Hawaii attracts many diverse people, making these islands an international breeding ground for modern culture.” Publishing content that explores art, design, culture, and music, Co-Founder & pro-surfer Jason Shibata shot this video on the South Shore of Oahu using the GoProHD camera with ‘HI-808′ as the track.  Crazy image quality – check the video (below) as Jason catches some tubes.

We’re also featured in the latest Travel issue – Contrast 003: Goin’ Places.  Look for a copy or request it from your neighborhood newsstand.

Shout outs to our North F@$!in’ Shore homie Zen who expands our minds regularly, and all the In4mants crew once again.  Miss you guys!

2 of 2: Oregon Recap – PORTLAND

April 8, 2010

Two pre-show missions: 1. Stop by the Adidas store and 2. Hit thrift shops on Hawthorne.

On the first mission, we got a tour from the marketing homie from Seattle, Mark Shin. We got a glimpse of some upcoming releases, tried on Dwight Howard’s shoes, and tried to get a sponsorship for Macklemore’s homie Raven Bowie for these shoes.

On the second mission, we hit up a vintage store and engaged in the time honored masculine tradition of taking pictures together in an old-school photo booth.

As for the show, I gotta say that it was the most athletic crowd we’ve ever played for in Portland. Several medicinal brownies were thrown onstage and it all ended with 1/4 of the crowd taking the party onstage.

Big thanks to Taylor Lemons for sending a few mobile phone shots  (not bad, we can almost feel the condensation on that lens), and to Adam Bussing.  He said, “We wanted to give you our special cupcakes, but we couldn’t bring them into the show without messing up our (expertly) hand-crafted frosting design…. So we brought brownies instead.” Were those your brownies thrown onstage?

Photos 2-5 are from Taylor Lemons, and medicinal cupcake photo is courtesy of Adam Bussing.  See you again real soon, Portland!
