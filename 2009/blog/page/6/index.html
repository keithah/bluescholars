NEW MUSIC: “The Town (Sabzi Remix)” by Macklemore

March 26, 2010

Image not recovered — TowwnWEB

Shortly after I met Macklemore at Evergreen State College sometime in 2003, he sent me about 67 messages on MySpace (a new thing back then) asking for beats for the debut album he was working on.  Like the buster I was in 2003, I pretty much ignored most of his messages.  Ok, all of his messages.

The 20 or so of you that were paying attention to the music us SEA rap doods were making back then might have picked up on this lack of communication between Ben and I when he shouted out how difficult it was to get a hold of me in the lyrics to his infamous “MySpace Song.”  Yea, I know in the song he said “Blue Scholars” but I know he was talking about me.

Maybe it was because I didn’t want to work with the guy.  Maybe it was because I was afraid that the MC I was already working with would get jealous and take a baseball bat to my car while I was in the barbershop getting my hair cut.  Maybe it was because I didn’t want him, or anybody for that matter, to discover that I had only made 14 beats and I’d put them all out already and there was nothing left for me to give.  Or maybe, just maybe, it was my Seattle-bred, passive-aggressive way of telling Macklemore “Not now, but one day…”

Well Macklemore, that day has come.  You are becoming far too popular for me to ignore and it’s better that I try and associate myself with your inevitable success as an artist before the opportunity escapes me.  I have remixed “The Town” and used my Rollei to take a picture of one our favorite burger spots to function as the artwork for its internet release.

Certainly, I chose this photo because that place is a Town staple and we love those burgers.  More importantly, however, it’s the name of the restaurant (without the “s”) that reminds me of my behavior towards you in 2003.

I vow to make a living amends to you from here on, Macklemore.  Enshallah I will have the opportunity to cook many more beats for you to rock over.  Love ya buddy. - Sabzi

New Music: GORDON VOIDWELL “WHITE FRIENDS” (SEFEED POOST REMIX)

March 25, 2010

Image not recovered — whitefriends

I first met Gordon Voidwell at an event called Minorityfest in this dive bar in Brooklyn.  They performed shortly before I went up to DJ and their set was incredible.  I remember frequent vogue-like posing and certainly no shortage of random sound effects.

Later in the evening during my set, I played Brotha Lynch Hung’s “Rest In Piss” and the drummer from GV’s band went apeshit and nearly spilled his drink while he sang along to all the words.  Not long after that I discovered that this drummer was originally from Seattle and then everything made sense.  I knew at that moment that I’d be getting along with these folks just fine.

“Gordon is glamorous, but gritty. He’s ghetto, but scholarly. He’s retro, but futuristic. The idea behind Gordon Voidwell is to take various concepts/ideas that presume to be binary opposites and deconstruct them, proving them to be more alike than different. That is the idea behind both the sound and the general movement I align myself with. We are all complex people – each and every one of us. I’m championing complexity rather than simplicity.” -GV

Yup.  I feel that.  Since these days we’re all getting ready to perform together in Seattle, I figured we may as well start with a classic Seattle-ish theme of discussion:  White Friends.

Let’s see what Gordon Voidwell has to say about it:

“White Friends is a song about privilege.  Privilege is a complex thing, but in short, I’ve got a lot of white friends and I love them all.  They’ve shaped my ideas of friendship in a lot of ways. At the same time, I reached a point in my life – in my late teens – where I began to realize how their privilege and wealth shapes their reality/view of the world.  Sometimes, I simply can’t relate. I think some black kids who went to private schools like the one I attended would lash out and just try to alienate those kids.  Me, I think it’s important to just share my experience, knowing damn well that they understand what I’m talking about.  It’s cool, they dig it, they’ve got money and are endowed with special privilege so they don’t get upset by very much. Haha (I’m joking, kinda).” -GV

Now that’s interesting!  Anyway, I did the beat for this remix. Download the ‘White Friends (SEFEED POOST remix)’, below.  And if you were lucky enough to cop a ticket, he’ll be performing with us for the first night of our DOUBLE FEATURE event (SOLD OUT), Friday March 26th.  OR, you can catch us at the Encore Show on Sunday March 28th, tickets still available here. — Saba

7 Questions with Das Racist

March 25, 2010

Image not recovered — DR2

We’re one day away from a weekend of sold out back-to-back shows, with an added Encore Show on Sunday, March 28th, and needless to say – we’re hype.  Get your tickets for Sunday so you don’t miss out.

Those of you who were lucky enough to score tickets to the Saturday show, welcome our friends Das Racist.  Comprised of Queens, NY native Himanshu Suri, San Francisco-born Victor Vazquez, and complimented with their hype man Ashok Kondabolu (known as Dap), their musical references defy classification.  Made famous by their cut ‘Pizza Hut Taco Bell’, their music continues to reflect the groups fun nature and unpretentious  lyricism, while commenting on rap music as they play their role within the game that they wholeheartedly embrace.

In light of the DOUBLE FEATURE, we’ve asked them 7 Questions and they answer with their views on film & the future.   Also, check out the Das Racist track ‘Who’s That Brown (remix)‘ by our one and only Sabzi.

1. What’s the last movie you watched?

Victor: The Carter (2009)
Dap: Standard Operating Procedure (2008)
Himanshu: Aar Paar (1954)

2. A short review?

V: The best part is probably when this British interviewer says, “I think your grill is even more impressive than Birdman’s.” Something about how she says it cracks me up.
D: It was a fairly horrifying movie, especially trying to imagine rebuilding your life after being incarcerated and tortured. Judd Appatow’s least funny movie to date.
H: The first Guru Dutt film to utilize his direction style and poor man in a hard world theme. Feels like this movie had to be made for 57′s Pyaasa or 59′s Kaagaz Ke Phool to be as dope as they were. Also, my favorite part is when the British interviewer says, “I think your grill is even more impressive than Birdman’s” to Guru Dutt.

3. What did you think the year 2010 was going to be like 20 years ago?

V: When I was six, I thought that in the future I would say swear words whenever I wanted, drink coffee, smoke cigarettes, drink beer, drive a car and have a dog.  I don’t have a dog or a car and I don’t really smoke cigarettes but the rest is accurate.
D: When I was 4/5 years old I would go through the Staples catalog and circle the things I thought I would want to own when I was older. This was from the front of the catalog to the back, so there’s still a lot of things I’ve yet to acquire. So, better than this!
H: I never really cared about 2010 or anything in between Y2K and 2012 but i guess I wanted flying cars and teleportation like everyone else.

4. How did you meet Blue Scholars?

V: I met Sabzi through Dap.  Sabzi DJs for us now and co‐produced a track on our mixtape, Shut Up, Dude, out this month.
D: I met Sabzi through brother/comedian Hari Kondabolu. I’ve only spoken with Geo once or twice briefly. I’m hoping to exchange many more words with him when we come out there.
H: I met Sabzi through Dap as well. I’ve exchanged several words with him already but as I enjoy his company I’m hoping to exchange many more words with him when we come out there. Geo too.

5. Describe your stage performance in three words.

V: Life is meaningless?
D: Jumping up/down.
H: Life IS meaningless.

6. If you had to pick a film genre to classify your music, what would it be?

V: Brownsploitation
D: Spaghetti Western (lots of standoffs/staredowns).
H: Damn, I was gonna say Brownsploitation (Dap and I actually made a terrible 3 minute long Brownsploitation film once). But umm, it would be like brownsploitation meets a 1950s hindi Guru Dutt film meets Judd Appatow with Megan Fox’s ass, Fatima Bhutto’s brain, and Rashida Jones’ everything. All Rashida Jones everything.

7. Protagonist or Antagonist?

V: Tupacalypse
D: Man’s best friend.
H: “An alright guy”
