PHOTOS: Anchorage, part 1

- Geo photos

Image not recovered

Last week in Alaska was my very first trip out there. Sabzi said it was like an “extreme Washington,” which is a fitting description for all the familiar geographic scenery (trees, mountains, water), but times 100 and with only a fraction of the people. Crazy culture shock from playing the Capitol Hill Block Party in Seattle just a couple days before the trip, and being in New York for a week prior to that.

Image not recovered

Image not recovered

Image not recovered

A good dude named Mike, originally from Bremerton, picked us up from the airport and showed us around downtown Anchorage. Apparently, he’s a big Mariners fan, along with a grip of Alaska folks who share with us the unfortunate experience of rooting for the teams we have (or no longer have). He said that since he’s moved to Alaska in ’73, glaciers that used to creep on his backyard have receded several miles inland. These folks actually see global warming.

We stayed at the very wood-cabined out Captain Cook hotel, which felt like the arrival scene in Hot Tub Time Machine.

After grubbing and perusing downtown, I saw a trolley posted up on 4th Ave. with the word “FREE” prominently painted on it. So I hopped on, having no idea where it would take me. It took us down to a river where people fished, gutted their catch on the spot, and sold it to whoever wanted it.
