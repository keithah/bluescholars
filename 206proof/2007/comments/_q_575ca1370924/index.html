    Mars Hill Church to buy troubled Belltown nightspot for downtown campus
    By AUBREY COHEN
    P-I REPORTER

    A contentious Belltown nightclub is on its way to becoming a church.

    Mars Hill Church agreed to pay $3.95 million for the current site of Tabella Restaurant and Lounge, at 2333 Western Ave. Tim Gaydos, Mars Hill's downtown campus pastor, said the sale is scheduled to close Thursday and church leaders hope to open the branch for services early next year, after renovating the building.

    Tabella came to the forefront of a long-standing debate in July about downtown-area nightclubs, when a woman was shot in the leg on Western Avenue and witnesses connected the incident to a fight inside the club. The Washington State Liquor Control Board rejected Mayor Greg Nickels' subsequent request to have Tabella's license revoked.

    But it did move to yank the club's license Oct. 12, citing five alleged violations -- one for serving a minor, two for serving intoxicated people and two for disorderly conduct by employees -- stemming from a Seattle police sting that concluded in September.

    "If you're asking questions about Tabella, I don't have any answers for you," owner Kauser Pasha said Monday evening.

    Pasha said police unfairly targeted his club in September, saying, "the mayor is making the police do dirty jobs and making them look bad."

    David Osgood, the attorney who represented Pasha during the recent Seattle police nightclub sting, heard about the pending sale through the media and said he'd known for several weeks there were potential buyers. Osgood said he did not believe the sale was directly caused by the Oct. 12 actions by the Liquor Control Board.

    Pasha is still in the 20-day window to appeal the alleged violations, but it is unclear if he has, Osgood said.

    Edwin Mirsky, the Ewing & Clark brokerage agent representing the seller, confirmed the pending sale to Mars Hill, but said the owner of the site did not want to comment. Rocket Enterprises Inc. bought the site in March for $3 million, according to county records. Tabella had been there since 2005.

    Mars Hill leaders, who have wanted to open a downtown branch since starting the church 11 years ago, have looked more seriously in the past six months and were delighted to find the Tabella building, Gaydos said.

    "It's almost exactly what we're looking for -- very industrial, urban, arty," he said.

    At a time when many churches are leaving the city for suburban properties, he said, "We want to go upstream."

    Gaydos said he spent a lot of time at nightclubs before becoming a Christian and sees a connection between clubs and churches.

    "I was looking for happiness in a club and satisfaction in a club," he said. "I think the people who come to our downtown campus are looking for the same things. We have a different answer."

    Mars Hill Church has campuses in Ballard, Shoreline, West Seattle and Wedgwood and is looking for an Eastside site.

    Belltown Community Council President Zander Batchelder welcomed the news that the church would replace Tabella.

    "I know it will be a relief to the neighbors," he said, adding that the demise of Tabella was also "a wake-up call for the nightclubs."
