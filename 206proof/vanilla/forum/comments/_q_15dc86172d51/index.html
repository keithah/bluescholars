    I just landed a job mid-recession with no experience in the field I'm currently in. I am by no means a career counselor but nonetheless I have collected a fair amount of wisdom from various folk as well as from my own experiences. Here is my advice for landing a job:

    1. Make sure your resume doesn't LOOK shitty. I recently got the opportunity to sift through a bunch of peoples' resumes and I realized that I didn't want to look at a single one of them because they all ran together. If you don't want to be one of the pack, BE different. There are no rules about what employers like to see and don't see. The all black, one page resume is out. For example, here is my resume.

    Everything is properly kerned and leaded for maximum reading comfort and the Helvetica Neue gives it a fresh, polished look that sets it apart from all the Calibri, Times and Arial resumes. When an employer opens it up, they will want to read it moreso than the cramped style that job counselors always recommend. It is different in that I feel it is better composed than most resumes. The plus signs also subconsciously denote positives as they are reading through it. (MTK also owes his job to my resume making skills :P)

    2. Don't be uptight or overly professional in your cover letter. It might seem like the right move but employers are people too. Talk to them like they're human, not some robot checking off keywords in your letter. I got a job offer out of the blue because I wrote a passionate enough appeal to a random employer who wasn't even hiring at the time but wanted to pick me up because they saw that I really meant every word of what I was saying. Don't be afraid to be corny either. I love designing and developing websites, and so in my letter I said exactly that. For those that don't know me, I graduated from UW in 2008 with a Chemistry degree yet landed a job in media 3 weeks ago and got offered another 1 week ago in web/creative.

    3. Write WELL. Review and revise your resume and cover letter over and over and over and over and over and over and over and over and over and over again. I went through at least 16 revisions before I found what I felt to be the perfect balance. The whole point of the resume/cover letter is to get to the interview stage. It is really the only part of the process that you have full control over, so make sure that it's perfect.

    4. That said, when you get to the interview. Go above and beyond. If you show up with just yourself, even if you dress nice, you're just one of the pack. Do something extra. In fact, if applicable, start doing the job before you even go to the interview. Research the company, research their competition, figure out ways that you could take the position they're looking for, and make it better. For example, I rolled into my interview with Q13 Fox with a laptop, a presentation, and a proposal. My manager told me later that he got about 80 resumes and did 8 interviews before he chose me. Every other candidate was more qualified than I was for the position. Why did I get the job? I was the only one to bring a laptop and actually show them how I would go about doing the job, before I got the job. He saw that my dedication was beyond what the others were offering and hired me 2 days after interviews concluded.

    5. Be yourself. Your personality is probably 80% of why you'll land a job. If you come with some fake canned answers to the interview, prepare to get shut out. If you can't answer a question, tell them why you can't answer it. Just don't try to politician your way out of it. It doesn't matter that you didn't answer every question, it's just that you were truthful in presenting yourself. Trust me on this, it matters. You'll most likely be nervous, yes, but don't be afraid to loosen up and crack jokes with your interviewer. Remember, at the end of the day, they're people just like you. They don't want to see you unemployed any more than you do.

    6. Don't be desperate. Be appreciative if you get the chance to interview, yes, but don't be the guy/girl that makes it obvious they're willing to go for whatever. Employers want to find a good fit for the job, not someone that is trying to force themselves to fit because it's their last rope. Be natural in all conversation/e-mails you might send to recruiters/employers etc. It will pay off.
