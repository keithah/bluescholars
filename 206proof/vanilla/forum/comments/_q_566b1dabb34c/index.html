    To say that Sub Pop pulled our deal before we got to put out a "real record" is re-writing history. (I would have liked to see anyone tell Kutfather that his record was not a "real record.") It is true that distributing hip-hop through ADA was a big challenge, but Sub Pop was extremely supportive of us (financially and otherwise) and there's no way we could have put out all the records we did – Kut, the first Jake One record, Eclipse, Third Degree, Walkman Rotation (mixed by J-Rocc), to name just a few – without Sub Pop backing us up. By the time they dropped us, we wanted to be dropped (I think I may have even asked) because the vinyl distribution had gotten too frustrating, but I still feel indebted to Sub Pop for their early support – they gave us all kinds of opportunities. We learned from them and they probably learned something from us.

    About the street team stuff – the truth is that stuff like that wasn't Sub Pop's responsibility as part of the deal. We did explain to them about street promotions, and they were supportive in printing posters, stickers, etc. for us. It was up to us to get it out there and we did to the extent it was possible for us as a hip-hop label from Seattle, learning how to do it as we did it, and before the internet took off as a connector and promotional tool the way it is now.

    I understand the desire to speculate but there are behind-the-scenes mechanics to all of this stuff.
