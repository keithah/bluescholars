    sabarillo:it is the opinion of a small few


    A small, pretty damn vocal few if you ask me.

    Now I hardly consider myself a Northwest Hip Hop veteran or a scenester who knows his way around the block (or pretends to know), but I'll weigh in my $0.02 anyway. As a fan and future DJ myself, I simply can't deny that I owe the Blue Scholars a lot. Before I heard their music, I didn't even know a scene existed out here. Four years later, I live on Beacon Hill and collect vinyl during my spare time. Shit happens, right? I know this will sound extremely corny, but everybody remembers their cherry pop or their "first time", and that way Saba and Geo will always feel special as musicians to me. They served as a gateway drug to a whole world of 2-0-sickness that I haven't even begun to explore yet. Had they not produced such great music two or three years ago, I probably wouldn't have moved onto other (read: "harder") styles like Dyme Def, Cancer Rising, the Sportn' Life camp, Grynch, etc., so I credit them with much more than just dropping science on the sociopolitical tip.

    That said, I'll always love Mass Line since artists like Geo, Ra and Gabe speak about issues that I honestly want to hear about, and do so poetically the way real emcees should. Not everybody will like that psuedo-Marxist, vaguely Mao-inspired sound (just like not everybody likes that "harder" sound), but Hip Hop needs diversity every which way to grow. Mos Def once said that if every rapper on eMpTyV sounded like him, he'd complain the same way these so-called "backpackers" do about the violence and misogyny. So above all, fuck self-censorship. Hip Hop needs variety, not monotony; conscious, gangsta, underground, crunk, hyphy, backpacker, crossover, whatever.

    Perhaps Seattle music journalists' dickriding Mass Line prevents other talented acts from "breaking through", so to speak, but I'd also hope that the Mass Liners' immense popularity inspires other youngsters like myself to really start exploring the local scene and supporting their favorite artists. I do street team work with Mass Line, and if I can just remember to call DeVon Manier one of these damn days, I'll soon start promoting the Sportn' Life camp as well. It's all gravy to me, since both of y'all spit the truth your own ways. One approach might appeal more to privileged college kids rocking Che t-shirts and dreadlocks, while the other will attract the folks who've really experienced "the struggle" and know a thing or two about hard times. As soon as all the haters (on all sides) realize that embracing one particular style or sub-genre doesn't necessarily mean rejecting another, Seattle Hip Hop can really start to blossom. But so long as we bitch and moan like this, we only look like dumbasses to Guru and others who periodically visit our town (and our boards).I'll bring the fire till you're soakin' in your seat...
