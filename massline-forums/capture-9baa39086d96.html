ehh I would say no, just on a count of the fact that it's a true story and he may have even met up with the guy and intensely studied his character like he did with the Hurricane
when I was watching it, I could see hints of Training Day in their as well as the "businessman" traits, but I would say in a way that fits the character of an intelligent organized black gangster in the 60's and 70's who ruled a drug empire

and of course, being a humane being, their will always be aspects of ones own personality that show up in a character, but after watching it, I would honestly say he did the best that can be expected

but beyond that, I would say that the overall importance of this Film is that portrail of a strong and powerful black figure in American history whose victory over the white power structure (morally aimed or not) has gone uncredited

to elaborate on my feeling about this movie in particular: when people talk about Italian gangsters, the image that usually comes to mind is "The Godfather" or "Good Fellas"; well organized, code of honor among criminals, etc. but when people talk about black Gangsters, the image you get is "Menace to Society" and "New Jack City"; a bunch of crazy stupid assholes killing each other for spare change or because they offered you a BJ for some crack.

but with "American Gangster", here's this Intelligent Black Kingpin who totally ruled the drug game all over New York and part of New Jersey, and eluded the cops for nearly a decade

I mean don't get me wrong. he was no Malcolm X. he didn't become some amazing spiritual/moral leader. but even as a criminal he showed a level of intelligence and mastery that is historically not attributed to a black man by pop-culture, unless his name Martin Luther King or he's being portrayed by Denzel Washington in a movie
