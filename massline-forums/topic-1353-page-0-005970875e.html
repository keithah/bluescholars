Peace fam,
 
Most folks have followed the discussions concerning the FCC Payola Settlement, Kube 93, and independent media. (For more info on settlement you can check out www.reclaimthemedia.com or www.daveyd.com)
 
206 Zulu is hosting a town hall gathering Monday March 26th from 7-9pm at Langston Hughes Cultural Arts Center to develop a community statement on the issue.

The point of this meeting is to build on the platform those local independent outlets and various activists have founded over the years to create a community statement of solidarity with a two tiered campaign we will launch here in Seattle and will be working with existing efforts and groups such as Hip Hop Congress, and Universal Zulu Nation, and National Hip Hop Political Convention networks to connect to the broader movement. This will accomplish the objectives of 1) increasing strength in numbers 2) increasing opportunities for independent media for content exchanges and cross-promotions nationally 3) increase opportunity for regional artists through strengthening independent distribution networks.

 The backdrop to this campaign will be three documents which I can email anyone who hasn't already seen them: 1) Universal Zulu Nation's National Bring Back the Balance Campaign 10 Point Program developed in 2006 2) Seattle's Statement on Radio developed in 2004 3) Youth Media Council's "Is KMEL The People's Station?" developed in 2002.

 The first two documents are broader statements on what are ideals in terms of just media, one from a national body, UZN which was, of course, the very first Hip Hop institution, and the second is a similar, more detailed analysis from a local collective. The third is a detailed analysis and list of demands for KMEL (this is the Bay Area station that pioneered the format for KUBE 93 and other watered-down ass clear channel stations across the country). Organizing efforts around "Is KMEL the People's Station?" was a huge force in usherin' in the success of the Bay Area's independent Hyphie Movement.

This means there is a broad body of interests we intend to represent with this statement. Many regional artists want airplay and equal access to media sources, some activists will primarily be concerned with images perpetuated by mainstream media and lack of diversity in programming, independent media makers want the broader community to acknowledge their efforts in providing alternatives to independent media, and still others want the body of artists in the community to learn how to utilize the indy media that does exist.

 In this discussion, our diverse concerns are not mutually exclusive, and it's not outta "kumbaya" but more outta practicality that we stick together on this one. Since we will need sign on and buy-in from labels, artists, organizations, indy media outlets, etc. for this to be effective, we need everyone's input in developing this statement. I understand that some folks will not be able to make Monday's meeting, but if you hit me up I'll make sure you're in the loop.
