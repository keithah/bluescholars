Selah officials support Minuteman meeting
By ROD ANTONE
YAKIMA HERALD-REPUBLIC


A day after Selah Civic Center administrators denied meeting space to the controversial Minuteman Civil Defense Corps, city officials hope the center's board will change its mind.

Mayor Bob Jones said Thursday he was concerned about violating the group's constitutional rights, and after consulting with city attorney Bob Noe, urged the Selah Civic Center Association to reconsider its decision.

"There is not sufficient evidence anywhere to support the denial of the meeting," agreed Selah City Supervisor Frank Sweet.

The Eastern Washington chapter of the Minuteman group had planned to hold weekly meetings at the center starting in November, but on Wednesday received a letter of denial from the civic center's board that included a $75 refund for the meeting room.

Civic center activities coordinator Cassie Deatherage explained the board's decision in the letter, which was addressed to Kathy Dameron, the wife of Minuteman chapter leader Bob Dameron:

"We are a small community and we do not have the resources to provide protection for our city residents, the Minutemen or possible protesters," Deatherage wrote. "The board feels safety for everyone is an issue and we cannot allow the possibility of an incident.

"I hope you understand our concerns and you will still have time to find another location."

The denial was based on a recommendation from Selah police Chief Rick Gutierrez, who was concerned that protests and confrontations -- which have accompanied Minuteman gatherings in other parts of the country -- could flare in Selah. Should protesters and Minutemen clash, Gutierrez warned, his department might not have enough officers to respond adequately.

But Mayor Jones disagreed, saying he saw no reason why Minutemen and their critics couldn't express their political views peacefully.

"Both sides are expected to behave," Jones said. "If people on either side get out of line, the chief will deal with them accordingly."

Protesters opposing the Minuteman organization want civic center association members to uphold the original decision -- and they hope Selah residents will come forth to voice their opinions.

"We would ask the residents of Selah, 'Is this who they are?'<2009>" said Maria Cuebas of Aguilas De Norte, an immigrant advocacy group. "Is this who they want to be known for, as a community that supports a white supremacist organization?"

Dameron, however, said his group was not founded on racism, but a desire to protect the nation's borders from illegal activity, such as crossings by undocumented immigrants. While some immigrant-rights groups see the Minutemen's actions as racist and potentially threatening, Dameron said members have strict rules against taking any action beyond reporting their observations to the U.S. Border Patrol.

"I hate racism and always will," Dameron said. "This country is a melting pot and it's built from every race on Earth, and all of them have equal rights."

In light of the controversy, though, Dameron said that even if the civic center board reconsiders its den, his group will likely scale back its meeting to monthly instead of weekly.

"Once a month is more than enough because I don't want to detract from law enforcement from doing their job if they have to show up because of the protesters," he said. "We want to help law enforcement, not hinder it."

If the center's board gives the go-ahead, the Minutemen will meet in Selah on Nov. 5, Dameron said.

And if that's the case, Cuebas expects her group and others to be there that day as well -- although she assures that their goal is not confrontation, but education.

"We recognize this is their constitutional right to meet," she said. "We also have the right to a peaceful protest."
