Title: Working Radio
Post by: Fuzed on May 10, 2007, 08:44:48 am
I've had a few emails asking me about CMJ and radio so thought I'd just share the info with everyone.

Radio is a great tool - non-commercial stations like KEXP are key to promoting your album. Satellite and web radio are also becoming increasingly important.  The non-commercial stations and college stations report to a chart called CMJ - also a monthly magazine -www.cmj.com (http://www.cmj.com). But of course getting all the stations to play your album is difficult, so there are companies you can hire that do it for you. They mail out your albums to all the stations, have relationships with the music directors and important dj's for your genre of music, and give you reports and feedback. They will also hook up promotion and interviews at the stations when you tour. For Massline, we've been using Terrorbird - http://www.terrorbird.com/ who have been doing an amazing job. Highly recommended to anyone releasing a hip hop album. They also have a great service that send out digital EPs to radio, which saves on the cost of mailing the cds.

It costs about $3,000 to really work an album to the whole CMJ panel, so you have to make sure it's going to be effective for your release. That means that if you get radio support in a market you need to follow it up by getting albums into stores, playing shows in that market, getting press, doing street marketing, etc.

as always, if you have any questions about working your album, distribution, etc, let me know.

I did college radio from 1998 to 2003.  College radio is where it's at if you want to break music.  It has slipped in it's importance since the late 90's though.  During that time, major labels broke their artists at college first and then commercial mixshow would follow up.  Now isnt' really the case.  A lot of majors are skipping over the college aspect and going for commercial becasue they dont' want to waste their time and money at the college level.  Big mistake.  Right now, you really only have CMJ and rapattacklives.com as true supporters of college radio.  At one time you had CMJ, Gavin, Urban Network which later became The Network Magazine, Insomiac Magazine and others.  College radio will always be the first to break artists and independent labels/distrubtors/companies such as Spectre, Brick Records, Rhymesayers, Def Jux, Fondation Media still make it focal point to sevice college radi, so support and yeah, if you have the oppurtunity to work a project at college radio. 

Spectre worked the Blue Scholars record and took it to number one on the charts of rapattacklives.com.
