hmm, this is a very interesting topic because i think the very same thoughts have plagued my mind when attending hip hop shows. when i look at the people around me during a show, i see mostly white and asian folk.. reciting the lyrics, pumping their fists.. i saw this while Immortal Technique was up on stage, verbally confronting the new laws against immigration and the history of Mexican Americans. it does seem often at times that the artist-fan relationship seem to contradict each other when the artist stands for something that the fan isn't willing to carry out. but man, what can you do? an artist with a strong political view has something to say, their form of medium: music. and fans are willing to listen. that's all there is to it. the speaker and the listener. we can't promise as listeners to go out and preach the political-economical views of our favorite artists, all we can do is nod our head, pump our fist, and agree with 'em. cus we FEEL what they're saying. but for me, my faith stays with the artist to spread his own word....

and that's when something like this happens. listeners who gather in agreement that Blue Scholars has something good to say.
