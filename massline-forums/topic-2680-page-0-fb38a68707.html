Hip-hop happening

Blue Scholars raised eyebrows earlier this year, signing a distribution deal with nationally known Rawkus Records. This smart, socially conscious hip-hop duo has a huge customer base in the Northwest, recently selling out two concerts at the Showbox.

But could they sell on a national level? The Scholars' "Bayani," released June 12, came in at No. 38 on the Billboard rap album charts, and cracked the Top 10 on CMJ, iTunes hip-hop chart and Amazon hip-hop charts. Nothing close to 50 Cent or Eminem, but still pretty impressive.

The next Blue Scholars: The Saturday Knights.

Saturday night (of course), the Saturday Knights were doing what they do best � grabbing a crowd from the first song, then charming, humoring, grooving and, most of all, rocking it. This quartet � the standard two MCs and a DJ, plus a guitarist � played a set at Capitol Hill's Havana, then unveiled the video to "45."

With its mix of hooks and faux gangsterism ("I'm on the dance floor / and the DJ dropped my 45 / I was so hype that I dropped my .45 and everyone just ran, ran, ran"), this might be the first Seattle hip-hop hit since Sir Mix-A-Lot was talking about butt sizes. It's a party jam, not unlike the recent Shop Boyz chart-topper "Party Like a Rockstar."

While there may never be another hip-hop act out of Seattle with Sir Mix-size sales, several Seattle acts are talented enough to get national attention, including: Blue Scholars' 2Mass Line comrades Common Market and Gabriel Teodros, Renton boast-rhymers Dyme Def (250,000 MySpace plays) and Sportn' Life Records' acts smooth D. Black � check him at the Capitol Hill Block Party and Bumbershoot � and wild-style Fatal Lucciauno.

The Vera Project has a strong showcase of local hip-hop this week, with Dyme Def, Grynch, Macklemore and others. The Vera is now in Seattle Center; doors open at 7:30 p.m. Friday; $6 (www.theveraproject.org).

John Richards, KEXP: Richards, who plays local acts on his 6-to-10 morning show, picks the Blakes, whom he co-manages; the Blue Scholars; and Cave Singers.

Said Richards via e-mail: "Blue Scholars have a chance � the hip-hop environment is changing, and their message is perfect timing. The Cave Singers are just GREAT, and with Matador behind them, who knows!"

DJ B-Mello and DJ Hyphen, KUBE: Seattle hip-hop station KUBE 93.3 has been playing more local artists than ever, and these two station DJs like this new Seattle wave.

B-Mello e-mailed his might-make-it-big list:

� Dyme Def

� Fatal Lucciauno

� Blue Scholars

B-Mello also spins on KEXP's "Street Sounds" Sunday night hip-hop show, where Fatal Lucciauno "really impressed me with his freestyle a few weeks ago."
