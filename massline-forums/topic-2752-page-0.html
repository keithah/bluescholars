one of my best friends is a huge wrestling fan and has been ever since he was a youngin'.  he basicly grew up watching these guys.  they're like family to him.  check out his blog about this.




***UPDATE*** I just wanted to let everyone know, I of course have heard what happened in the Chris Benoit situation. Apparently, Benoit snapped over the last weekend, killing his wife and child, then himself. I'm really at a loss for words, as every report and account of meeting Chris from fans was that he was a genuine human being who loved those who loved him. He was quiet, yet polite. Under no circumstances would anyone consider him the candidate to perform such a henious act, and this should be considered a testament to how quickly the mind can break-down and turn against you. Many attribute Benoit's actions to "Roid Rage"; an act of temporary insanity caused by abusing the performance enhancers. This entire revelation adds to my ultimate thanks for the events that removed me from "The Business" as it were, because although I'm a fan of pro wrestling, the business as a whole destroys many, many lives. To be the best, you have to look like a Superhero; muscles where people normally don't have muscles, like a work of art. The means to create these bodies just don't come fast enough or dramatic enough naturally, so steroids are used instead, leading to many situations such as what happened to the Benoit family. I'm going to leave the tribute up, if only out of respect to who Chris Benoit was, and to what his TRUE legacy meant to me. I deeply regret any negative feelings this may cause; I don't mean to "worship" a murderer, but I do firmly believe that Chris Benoit the person wouldn't EVER do such things if he weren't under the influence, and as someone who's done some incredibly dumb things myself while under the influence of alcohol, I can sympathize to an extent. Thank you for reading.     

     I don't want to do this, because I don't want to believe yet another one of my heroes has passed. I don't want to do this, because I don't think my words do his legacy justice, but as a fan and as someone who loves to write, I believe I owe it to his memory.

      For those that haven't heard, or may have and didn't really care at the time for whatever reason, Chris Benoit, known by his nicknames "The Crippler" and "The Rabid Wolverine", was found dead tonight alongside his wife Nancy and his son Daniel in their Atlanta home. I'm not going to speculate on the situation, it's not my job, and no other details have been released as of this writing. This isn't a forum of speculation, this is a tribute.

      Tonight, one of my best friends, Lou DePaul, sent me a text informing me of Benoit's death. I couldn't believe what I was reading, and immediately started tearing-up. I rushed back to the laptop and tried going to 411mania, a wrestling news site, but it was so backed-up I couldn't log into it. I then went to wwe.com, and right there in front of my eyes, "Chris Benoit Dead, 1967-2007". Pure, overwhelming shock. More and more frequently, my heroes are dying in greater numbers. I can only hope to help make you understand what made this man so special to me...

      Anyone who knows me, knows I'm a fan of professional wrestling, and what's more, it was going to be my profession until I blew-out my knee. When I first decided I wanted to become a wrestler, one of the first things I started doing in preparation was to watch and STUDY matches, move for move, hold for hold, trying to figure out exactly how these guys did what they did. To be honest, much of what is seen on TV looks fake, because well, it is. The matches are scripted and many of the flashy moves that wrestlers do aren't physically possible to perform in a real fight; they're done to make matches more exciting.

      Chris Benoit was one of the few remaining wrestlers of this generation, who could make EVERYTHING he did in the ring look real. Every chop, head-butt, kick, suplex, everything...Benoit went into the ring with a serious, gonna-beat-some-ass look on his face every night, and made you FEEL the pain he was delivering his opponents. There are three guys I have studied more than anyone else when my goal was to be the best; Steve Austin, "Mr. Perfect" Curt Hennig, and Chris Benoit. I knew if I took away even a fraction of what those guys could do, I'd be set, and you'd better believe I've chopped more people in the chest in bars across the country because of "The Crippler". Hell, it was just recently brought to my attention that high school kids are prone to shout, "BENOIT!!!" and then chop their friends, most not even knowing why they're yelling "Benoit". I hope now they know.

      I first became fascinated by Chris Benoit when I first started watching wrestling again, back in 1997. Benoit was just beginning to catch fire in WCW, having his "Best of Seven" series of matches against Booker T, both men showing the entire world how great they were at their chosen craft. I remember vividly loving the hell out of the three-way program between Benoit, Booker, and Fit Finlay as they each traded the Television Title, providing constant entertainment.

      Over the course of the next few years, Chris Benoit never ceased to keep his fans on their toes. First, he shocked the world by defeating Sid Vicious for the WCW World Title, finally proving he was a worthy main event player, but then the very next night he and his friends Eddie Guerrero, Perry Saturn, and Dean Malenko jumped ship to the WWF for a chance to be even BETTER. And better Benoit would become, having dream matches against Rock, Austin, Jericho, Angle, Regal, and dozens of others. The greatest thing Chris will be remembered for, as a wrestler, was that he could quite literally have a good-to-great match against ANYONE. From a man of arguably equal skill such as Kurt Angle, to a 500-pound behemoth like Viscera, Benoit was such a master of the artform he knew how to bring out the very best in his opponents.

      The greatest memory I have of Chris Benoit, was watching "Wrestlemania 20" with a collection of my best friends at Frank's place. The two main events for that show were Eddie Guerrero defending his WWE Title against Kurt Angle, and Triple H defending his World Title against Shawn Michaels and Chris Benoit in a triple threat match. Nobody had really given Benoit a serious chance of winning; he had won the Royal Rumble to earn his shot at Triple H, but Michaels was thrown into the thing at the last second, possibly because management didn't have confidance in Benoit as Champion(remember, it's scripted, and it's a business: Whoever can make the company the most money, holds the top Title).

      The match was amazing, each of the three men working their absolute hardest to entertain their fans, both the thousands in attendance and the millions watching at home. Most of my friends watching the show with me were mostly-casual-at-best wrestling fans, zoning in and out of attention as the event transpired. However, ALL OF THEM in the room were glued to the TV, just like I was. In the final moments of the match, Benoit was taken out by Triple H and Michaels, driven through a table on the outside of the ring. The end, it seemed, had come for Benoit's chances. And sure enough, Michaels landed his super-kick to the face of "The Game", covered, and out of nowhere Benoit interrupted the count! The building, as well as the attendees at Frank's, exploded into cheers! Shawn was sent flying out of the ring, but Benoit turned right into Triple H! He went for the Pedigree...But Benoit reversed it into the Crossface! In the climax that seemed to stretch on forever, Triple H finally tapped-out to the hold.

      It was one of those rare, truly-special "Feel Good" moments that one really doesn't expect to find in the realm of professional wrestling. Chris Benoit, for years labeled as one of the hardest working men in the business, never really getting his shot at the top of the sport he was perhaps the best at, finally got his due. After EIGHTEEN YEARS wrestling in Japan, ECW, WCW, and finally in the WWF/E, Benoit won what is considered the biggest trophy in pro wrestling. Not only that, but he won it in the main event of the biggest show in the HISTORY of the sport, over two of the biggest stars in its history in Shawn Michaels and Triple H. And it wasn't over, either. As tears streamed from his eyes down his face, "The Crippler" held the World Title over his head, displaying his winnings to the fans who had supported him, cheered for him, begged for this moment to happen for the better part of two decades. And then, Benoit turned around to see his best friend in real life, as well as in the ring, Eddie Guerrero. Another rare, unscripted moment saw these two best friends, these two Champions, embrace and raise each other's arms in the middle of the ring in celebration, their respective moments had come on the same night, on the Grandest Stage of Them All. I was the tough guy, I was sitting in a room filled with my buddies and drinking beer, but you'd better believe I was crying. Couldn't help it, and I wasn't trying to hide it. There are moments that I have been utterly ashamed to be a wrestling fan, and there are those where I wish I could shout to the world, "SEE?!?! THIS IS WHY!!! THIS RIGHT HERE!!!" and that was one of them.

      The horribly unfortunate side of that scene, is that now both Eddie Guerrero and Chris Benoit are no longer with us. When Eddie died last year, I cried off-and-on for three days. Yes, it was an emotional time in my life altogether, but I was an Eddie fan for years, I had JUST seen him live in Anchorage, and I just wasn't ready to accept that he was never going to make me laugh or dazzle me with an amazing match ever again. The tribute show in Guerrero's honor, which prominently featured Benoit breaking-down and crying for his fallen brother, Benoit, a man who had never really shown emotion outside of anger in the ring, was now truly human in the eyes of his fans. It gave mortality to a man I, as well as many others, considered to be immortal.

      What I wasn't ready for, was realizing how very mortal Chris Benoit really was just one year later. I'm honored to have gotten the chance to see him live, on the same show in Anchorage I saw Eddie, and to have cheered him at my loudest as he won a Battle Royal, finally eliminating The Big Show. I am honored to be able to say I've been a Chris Benoit fan for ten years strong, never once stopped supporting his efforts. When I moved to Wisconsin, I brought four DVDs with me on the trip; "Waiting"(for a few cheap laughs), "Man on Fire"(I'd only seen it once before), "X-Men: The Last Stand"(same with this), and finally "Hard Knocks: The Chris Benoit Story". That's absolutely true. Whenever I needed to be entertained for fifteen or twenty minutes, I always wanted to have Chris Benoit's matches within reaching distance, and I will forever more have that DVD with me for that very same reason.

      There's so much more I could say, but it'll be said better by the friends and family of Chris Benoit in the coming weeks. My heart goes out to his family, specifically his parents who had already lived without their son since the age of 17, when he first became a wrestler in Japan, and now he's truly gone. My sympathies to his friends and peers, specifically Dean Malenko, Chavo Guerrero, Chris Jericho, William Regal, and Booker T.

      "Wrestling is my passion, it is my mistress" - Chris Benoit

      I love and respect the man and the legacy that was and is "The Crippler" Chris Benoit, for giving twenty years of his life to entertain millions of fans across the globe. I only hope that in the Afterlife, the Great Squared Circle in The Sky, at the very least Eddie Guerrero has his best friend back. Rest in peace.
