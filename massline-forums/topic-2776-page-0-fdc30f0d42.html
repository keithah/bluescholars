Rally to Call for Resignation of SPD Chief Gil Kerlikowske and replacement of OPA with truly independent police accountability system that serves the community

full description & details:

Rally for Police Accountability: Kerlikowske Must Resign!
Date: Thursday, June 28th
Time: 4 PM
Location: Seattle City Hall, 4th and James side

Rally organized/endorsed by Aaron Dixon, Seattle/King County NAACP, People's Coalition for Justice, October 22nd Coalition, the People's Alliance for Police Accountability, and others
Event to include: Music * Spoken Word * Speakers *

For more information: please contact KL Shannon at (206) 250-5412

Background
Since its creation nearly eight years ago, the Office of Professional Accountability (OPA) has been presented to the community as an independent oversight body that would enable community members to adequately address police misconduct and violence. However, the past several months - especially the wrongful arrest and failed frame-up of George "Troy Patterson by Officers Greg Neubert and Mike Tietjen and their subsequent exoneration by Seattle Police Department Chief Gil Kerlikowske -- have revealed that this "independence" is an illusion. The structure of the OPA can produce no other result.
 
At every level of the OPA's activities, the Chief of the SPD retains control and final decision-making authority over any outcome of any OPA investigation.  All initial investigations of complaints made by community members against police officers are carried out by other officers. All of the other participants in the process - the OPA Director, the OPA Auditor, and the three-member OPA Review Board - have no disciplinary powers, and are limited to making recommendations or suggesting changes in policy. The Seattle Police Officers Guild (SPOG) continually obstructs any attempts to give the OPARB any real power or authority, choosing its members' interests over the community it claims to serve.
 
While the attention Mr. Patterson's case has received has been exceptional, the investigation of his charges of misconduct against Officers Tietjen and Neubert is anything but. This case - especially the increasingly obvious interventions by Chief Kerlikowske in the OPA investigation -- highlights how flawed Seattle's official police accountability system is, how much institutional SPD power favors the police over the community it claims to serve, and how justice can only be assured by the collective power of the community itself. This case should not be suggested as a case where the "system worked." It was not the OPA, but the efforts of concerned community members and organizations like the Defender Association and Seattle/King County NAACP that uncovered these officers' lies and misconduct via videotape.
 
Every step of the way, no matter how substantial the evidence that Neubert and Tietjen are guilty of several offenses -- including framing a suspect, Chief Kerlikowske has supported these officers, justified their actions, and obstructed any attempts to make these officers truly accountable.
 
It is clear that the community cannot depend on the Chief or the OPA to produce accountability, but must do it ourselves, by demanding the status quo be replaced by a truly independent review board that empowers the community, not the SPD. The first step in the process is also clear: As the true head of the OPA, Chief Kerlikowske must resign immediately.
 
If you believe in ending police abuse and violence and making the police accountable to the people, please join us on Thursday, June 28th, in this first step of what will likely be a long struggle.
