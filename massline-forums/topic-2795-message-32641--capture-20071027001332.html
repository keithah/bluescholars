Art doesn't need to "involve" (or directly address) political or class issues to reflect them. All art reflects its environs, even that which doesn't claim any type of "message".

Most of the brash cartoons I watch on Adult Swim, along with the countless sexist and downright nasty humor pages I read online actually satirize the socioeconomic factors that cause racial, class and gender inequalities here in the United States (take Kevin Smith's work, as a prime example). So while they might appear crude, slapstick and otherwise "offensive", they all reflect the clashes that go on daily within this society. Certain art (and yes, pornography counts here) might even take a regressive stance and openly promote degrading women or some other "counter-revolutionary" shit, but it still mirrors the relationship paradigms we deal with on a constant basis as chains and cogs of a system that naturally pits human beings against each other.

So to answer your question, art doesn't need to "speak" pro or against any political or economic issues when it already reflects the social factors that influence the artist.
