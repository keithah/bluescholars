Mastering is the little tinkering you do after your final mix is finished.  They fix all the spaces inbetween the songs, EQ all the tracks and compress/boost the levels of all the songs to make them sound uniform.

Mixing is just that... mixing.  Get all the levels and tracks to sound right together.  You may need to compress your vocals, add reverb, distortion... all that ish.  Here's the thing DON'T PUT YOUR ISH IN THE RED!!!!  You may think your ish doesn't sound loud enough, but by pushing you tracks into the red is just going to make it sound like garbage later on.  Mastering at that point would most likely just make you tracks sound worse.

Not to diss, but I noticed a bit on "the long march" where Barry didn't cut out the little clip at the begining of one of the songs.  I don't remember what song it was, but you can hear the mic a split second before the beat starts.

I myself have been juggling many hats as I've been working on my LP.  (writing, producing, djing, recording and mixing)  But...  I'm going to pass off my mastering to someone else; because I'd rather have someone with the knoledge bust it out.  Plus HAVING A DIFFERENT SET OF EARS HELPS SOOOO MUCH.

Well... with programs like Cubase, Protools, Acid etc. you basically have everything in one box.  Later on you might need to get some plugins, but it's basically all there.  Your main weapon of choice will be your compressor.  Basically what it does is make it so all the peaks and low parts have a uniform sound.  (so you're not uber loud at one part and then quiet the next)  But be careful, if you compress the crap out of your vocals they'll have no "umph" to them.  Also another key is a high end filter or EQ.  You'll use this to get all the SSSSSSSSSSS's out of the vocals, so it doesn't sound like you drank a lot of booze.  But, once again don't go crazy; because you can actually make it worse if you over do it.

Your S's and P sounds are what are going to peak your levels the most.  A popper stopper will help with that, but wont necessairly get rid of everything when you're recording.

Hi Jawn, I'd be happy to give lessons, my only problem is I'm short on time, but feel free to call me at Jupiter Studios next week 206 6331363 and we can talk about what it is that is giving you problems.
  Most of what Dawhud says is right, it's basically good advice(especially the stuff about gain structure/clipping). You don't have to have amazing gear to make a great record. Sabzi has very basic, simple gear and makes great sounding music, he's very careful about picking good sounds and arranging them well, I know he doesn't use many plugins and doesn't even own a compressor. His tracks sounded good before I got involved. It does make huge difference mixing through a great analogue console, you can get separation, low end and bite that you definately can't get when staying in the computer.
  Daps, part of getting the drums so loud is just balancing and EQ, but it is partly due to paralell compression (bussing out to a separate compressor and then blending it back in with the mix). This can work very well but you have to be careful about using a compressor that doesn't introduce massive phase shift, which can cancel out the original signal in certain frequency bands. Trying to do this in software can be tricky due to the latency and phase shift of the plug-in that may be on the compressor buss, but it's worth fooling around with.
