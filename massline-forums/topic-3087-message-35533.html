Quality of the music comes first but I agree after that the sound quality is vital. You have to remember that when you put your music out there it is being judged next to everything else, and a lot of those CD's (especially majors) have had huge amounts of time and money ($200K+) spent on them. Now I don't believe that you need to spend an obscene amount of $ on a record ( I've consistently proved that you don't need to.) But there are good reasons why some records sound better/louder/more dynamic than others. You'd be surprised how much difference can be  made with a speedy mix and state of the art mastering, if the artist has their shit together it's much quicker and cheaper than you might think..

I do record, mix and Master. I don't normally charge by the song but by the hour or day. Label rates are quite high but I charge a lower rate for indie/self financed projects - $65 per hour or $600 for a 10 hour day. I know this is not cheap but for this you are getting a world class studio with $500K invested in it and 20 years of experience. Mixing a Hip-Hop record is much quicker than mxing a rock record, 1-2 hours per song, so a whole record can be mixed to a very high standard of kick ass in 2 days.
   I can Master most full length Lp's for under $500 including materials. If you are nervous about spending the $ then we could always do 1 song as a test so you can hear the difference it makes. (this is what I did for Sabzi then after that he was hooked!). what makes the mixing go smoothly (and ultimately saves you $),  is to have your tracks/sessions organised well without tons of out-takes and junk in them that has to be sorted/edited etc. I can advise on all the technical/cross platform stuff that needs to take place before a session if anyone needs it.
hope this helps,

If you don't have much experience the best way is to just pick up a simple recording program and a cheap audio interface and just record, record  and record. That's the best way to train your ears. Record yourself and anyone who will let you record them. You could go to school for it (I didn't, I have no formal training at all ), some courses are better than others, but the best way is to pick it up yourself and experiment. Don't get caught up in trying to make just a few tracks perfect- it will never happen, Don't beat yourself up if it doesn't turn out the way you want it to be, just start something new instead. Write as many things as you can, you'll learn more that way and you'll be more creative.
Martin
