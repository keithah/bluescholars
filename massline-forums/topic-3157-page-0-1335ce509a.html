Hey Massline Bloggers!�

I'm sure that many of you folks in the know are familiar with the (relatively) new and glitzy 2200 Westlake condominium building, located at, you guessed it: 2200 Westlake. You know - it's that building right off of Denny Way with the inconventiently located Whole Foods at the bottom. (BTW, who actually makes the annoying drive to shop there on a regular basis? Don't get me wrong, I love me some Whole Foods deli, but kudos to the bold corporate heads at WF who surmised that a high rise of over-caffeinated, well-to-do yuppies would single-handedly support an entire Whole Foods chain. But I digress...)

Anyway, the reason for this post is to bring to light a fellow blogger who seems to have an all-to-common stereotype of folks who live in the South End. It just so happens that he lives at 2200 Westlake and writes a regular blog about the plight of the rich and privileged (such as how the on-site fitness center doesn't always provide its patrons with hot towels). Now, admittedly, I live in Queen Anne, a neighborhood that I hold dear to my heart but is definitely known more for its affluence than its diversity. However, in my defense, this doesn't mean that I can't love and value the awesomely diverse, cultured, and vibrant area that is SOUTH SEATTLE (!).

Ya'll should take the time to scan the blog (www.2200life.blogspot.com), written by some dude named Chucky Cheese. Not everything in it is disrespectiful, but he definitely does his share of hating (see the brief but inflammatory comments he makes in the "I-5" post). Just another example of those who have, not understanding or caring about those who have not.

And make sure to read the posts in the "Comments". There's a particularly well-written one by some dude who feels the same way I (we?) do!! Support that guy!! Go South Seattle!!

One,

foxyben,

Thanks for your thoughts. Let me try to quantify what I was feeling when I first posted: I'm definitely aware of Seattle's general perception towards the South End. Every major city has a rough or "forgotten" neighborhood and I think the South End is Seattle's version. The last thing these neighborhoods need is someone from a position of privilege (read: rich and from the outside) reminding us of that existing perception because it generally comes with more negative connotations. The South End has gotten a bad rep, partially because local media outlets choose to play up all of the negative things that happen down there. THERE IS NOT ENOUGH POSITIVE NEWS ABOUT THE SOUTH END! I'm not claiming that the South End doesn't have issues (ALL neighborhoods have issues), what I am claiming is that we don't need someone in an ivory tower reminding their privileged condo buddies about the "poor schmucks" who "no one gives a damn about" in the South End. How can these words possibly be productive? I don't care where he claims he might be from, that does not magically immunize him from saying destructive things about a neighborhood that should be uplifted rather than put down. I'm not concerned with being politically correct, I'm only concerned about folks in Seattle having a fair and balanced perspective of a neighborhood that many choose to fear and forget rather than love and embrace.

And, I would respectfully take issue with your comment about the police and developers ignoring the South. On the contrary, I think the police are paying too much attention. Just take a look at the Massline main page to get an idea of how often the police stick their nose in innocent folks' business. I also work in the construction industry and know that many of our area's largest housing developers see the South End as fertile ground for development. In three years we'll have a state-of-the-art light rail system. Development is happening there.

- Azian1977
