nah man, good points no offense taken.  i've thought about the irony in that a bit my self since posting this stuff. 

i guess i'd clarify a bit by saying what i've said on a few other boards.  that i don't necessarily think this guy was wrong to resist an unjustfied police action, in an abstract philosophical sense at least.  my point was that in this instance his resistance was pointless and stupid.   

my point in posting the picture was that all the general public can letimately do about this kind of incident is organize into a body that can bring to bear practical and effective resistance of a type that has the potential to really force change in society.  to form organizations which can resist police brutallity in a manner that doesn't only result in a more severe beat down for the resister. 

my point is that resistance without a strategy guiding it is not only generally ineffective but usually counter productive, and is therefore ultimately not justified.  even when what is being resisted is also not justified.  the consequences of an action must be taken into concideration and if more damage will come as a result than benefit it should not be undertaken.

i don't agree with the police's actions here, and i don't really fault the kid for what you point out was probably a fear response.  my point is that this specific act of resistance was unwise and unjustified because it resulted only in greater hardship for the resister with no strategic benefit for him or his cause.

so i think you're pretty much right, except that i disagree that the black panthers didn't use their head.  they used their heads very much imo to come up with a way to prevent individual victims of police brutallity from having to fend for themselves as this college student had to.  my point is this kid's energy (and likely clean criminal record) could have been put to better use organizing as the panthers did to effect real resistance, than they were trying to pointlessly resist this specific arrest.
