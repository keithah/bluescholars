No merit and little merit are different things.  And rationalizations are a muf--ka.  Folks always want to give "value" and merit to what they like, especially if they are criticized for liking what seems to some to lack merit.  But I'm more interested in what kind of value something contributes.  My personal example would be Sean Price.  I think P rhymes about a lot of what I would call bullshit- shit talking, bragadoccio, whose the bigger badass.  He also talks a lot about street life, loving hip hop and being broke.  But I love dude for his flows.  I would love if he talked more about topics that I think have more substance and value, and I do enjoy his attitude, but I love his delivery, wordplay, and voice.  IMO this is his value, his merit, he contributes to the talent pool significantly.  This is also why I don't like a lot of other artists who talk about what I would call bullshit, 'cause they don't make up for it with having something else to contribute, i.e. and equal number of cuts that try and dissect the bullshit they talk about, or filthy skills on the mic, etc.  This is why I'm seen as salty by a lot of folks.  I want something for my dollar and my time other than a nice beat.  If you really enjoy Coke-rap for the content of it, I guess that's your perogative.  I'm not interested.  I want dope beats, but I also want dope lyrics, dope flows, and dope content.  Otherwise, I feel like the artist and the fans are just tools of the record industry.

Backpacker was originally a term for somebody that was really deep into hip hop.  It came around in the early to mid-90s.  It was also generally an East coast term.  It was used because a lot of MCs used mass transit since they didn't own their own rides, and therefore they always had a backpack to carry around their rhyme book(s) and any product they had to sell.  They were also frequently assumed to have dope skills. Backpackers, being from the East coast (yeah, yeah, I know not everybody was East coast, but that's where it originates), were usually very critical of West Coast music, and anything in general that didn't have that East coast grimey sound to some degree.  In the late '90s some folks started turning this on its head.  As hip hop gained popularity and a lot of folks started listening to more hip hop on the radio that was often dumbed down, these same Backpackers, around the country, would often attack "mainstream" or "commercial" hip hop as garbage.  So, a lot of folks started using backpacker as a dis, since those cats attacked their music, folks started attacking back.  So, then East coast flavored music with complicated flows and abstract or philosophically minded artists started being labeled backpack in a negative way, by those who prefered other styles of hip hop.  Backpack has since evolved to imply a narrow, elitist, self-important attitude and perspective on hip hop.
