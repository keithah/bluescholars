2007-03-11 | Sunday

Peace (it's Geo). I'm back in Seattle for the weekend having just completed the west coast leg of the Stop the Killings Tour. Thank you to all the Bayan-USA folks and tour organizers in San Francisco, Los Angeles/Long Beach and San Diego for hosting DJ daps1, Kiwi and I on our three-city excursion to raise awareness of the grave political situation in the Philippines and raise funds for the Permanent People's Tribunal later this month in The Hague, Netherlands. With the help of all those who attended and donated to the campaign, family members of victims and witnesses will be flown to the Tribunal on March 22-24 to testify against the Armed Forces of the Philippines and president Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo's puppet regime. You can read the full text of the press release here.

Thanks to all the folks we had the pleasure of performing with - there's an undeniable, underrated stream of Brown talent up and down the coast: Jern Eye (of Lunar Heights crew), Denizen Kane, The Committee, Foundation Funkollective, DJ Kuttin Kandi, Dannu & Key Kool (of the Visionaries), and Zandi de Jesus. The momentum is building. I can feel something on the verge.

This is what daps1 and I rolled to, maybe about four or five times:


Native Guns' Stray Bullets Vol. 2
: mixtape of the year so far. And not just because I dropped a verse on two tracks: "Agitation Propaganda" (over the Jake One-produced "Rock Ko Kane" beat) and a remix of "Said It." SB2 is the truth! Peep this crazy cameo lineup: Nump, Roscoe Umali, Ise Lyfe, The Pacifics, Skim, Nomi of Power Stuggle, Do D.A.T. and hella more.

AND I just recorded a cut for Bambu's upcoming album I Scream Bars for the Children, dropping on April 29th, which is also the 15th anniversary of the LA Uprising of 1992 (and they called it a riot? come on).

So now I head out to the Midwest and East Coast to resume my ambassador swagger. It's been a humbling and inspiring trip so far. Things seem to be coming full-circle - I've ran into folkers from B-town and Seattle in each city, as well as folks I've known since before Blue Scholars ever existed. Then I'll be back to close out the tour with a homecoming show on Friday, March 23 at the NEW VERA PROJECT!

More info about the Stop The Killings campaign and the Permanent People's Tribunal RIGHT HERE.

2007-03-05 | Monday

so, the news is public now folks. the following press release is going out today:

MASSLINE

 joins with Rawkus Records to release Blue Scholars new album "Bayani"

Sometimes the stars align and you get a front row seat to watch a cultural shift. You get to see it happen-you get to be a part of it. Rawkus Records was incredibly fortunate to have experienced one of those moments - we're even more fortunate to be in the midst of another.

In the Fall of 1995 Rawkus saw a truly exciting underground movement emerging in New York's hip hop scene. Hip hop had always had an underground, but it had been a minor league, where artists cultivated their skills and awaited their shot at a deal on a major. In response to this the underground, instead of chasing the labels demands, took control of their own scene and began making music without regard for commercial palatability or crossover potential. Of course all the labels ignored this movement and dismissed the artists. But Rawkus saw this as a tremendous opportunity and helped start a truly grassroots movement with three soon to be legendary artists signed to the label: Company Flow, Talib Kweli, and, of course, Mos Def. The success of these groups proved that the underground movement could sell records and compete with the majors. Moving forward, Rawkus is stronger than ever and promises to have many more classic albums in store for the fans - new signings include Kidz in the Hall, Hezekiah, Naledge, The Procussions and Guru's Jazzmatazz vol. 4. Rawkus' mission remains the same - to forever change the face of hip-hop by bringing the underground to the surface using the most artistic and culturally significant aspects of the underground. Rawkus sees Seattle group Blue Scholars (MC Geologic and DJ/ Producer Sabzi) and their label MASSLINE being a huge part of this new movement. The momentum behind this group and the scene they come out of in Seattle is undeniable. Armed with a purpose beyond creating music for music's sake, Blue Scholars take the classic form of the emcee/DJ duo perfected by groups such as Gangstarr, Pete Rock & CL Smooth, Eric B. & Rakim while carrying the essence of militant, yet personal struggle shown by hip-hop luminaries such as Public Enemy and KRS-One.

Blue Scholars have released one full-length album (Self-Titled, 2004) and a 8-song EP (The Long March, 2005) and have emerged as the leaders in politically radical hip hop coming out of the Northwest with soulful beats, poetic yet political rhymes and a reputation for breathtaking live performances. Since 2002, the duo has become renowned live show veterans, rocking over 200 shows with the likes of Kanye West, De La Soul, Immortal Technique, and supporting such acts as Zion I, One Be Lo, The Coup and Soul Position on tour. In June 2006, Blue Scholars joined forces with Common Market (emcee RA Scion and DJ Sabzi) and emcee Gabriel Teodros (of Abyssinian Creole) to launch MASSLINE, a new artist-run independent record label community activist group.

Blue Scholars second full-length release, "Bayani", is due out in June 12, 2007 on Rawkus Records in collaboration with MASSLINE. Be part of the next cultural shift.

big up to all the folks who've supported, this is the next step!
