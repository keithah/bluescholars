2007-07-12 | Thursday

not long ago, a group called Blue Scholars did a show in Portland. at Doug Fir to be exact. when we were down there, our homie Dustin from Coal Headwear, after givin us a handful of fresh hats to rock around the town, connected us with his old buddies at Upper Playground.



Upper Playground was a small clothing brand that started back in the late 90's. We actually met Dustin about 7 years ago when he attended some of the meetings of the now defunct student org, SHOW. some of you who were around hip hop shows back then might remember a couple events we did that had a big walrus looking logo on an oversize banner behind the stage. or UP Walrus stickers all over Terry Hall at UW. i wonder who put those there.





an artist by the name of Sam Flores, same guy that did that mural outside the Doug Fir, was instrumental in the development of the Upper Playground image. check out his toys. they're tight.



anyway, at the Upper Playground retail spot Geo and i gotta chance to check out the Will Barras exhibit at the Fifty24SF Gallery and got a big bag of good goodies from Stephen. he explained that the world of art in PDX was actually crackin more than in Seattle which was why they had chosen to open up the UP store there as their second location outside San Fransisco. i was disappointed to hear that, but then just more inspired to smash Portland in everything we do in Seattle. although all due respect to Cool Nutz.


(Eliza, Stephen, some guy, some other guy @ UP PDX)

any of the MassLiners who find themselves in the PDX area should stop by Upper Playground on 23 NW 5th Ave. they got a good selection of conservative--that is, not a lotta pinks and purples--artistic streetwear that does the job. big up ya'll!

saba



2007-07-09 | Monday

Mark your calendars MassLiners, there's an art show opening at BLVD gallery this Friday that you must see. "TIKKKI," a show of new works by Iosefatu Sua and Darvin Vida is a view of tikki culture from an authentic Pacific island perspective. The imagery for “TIKKKI” will revolve around the use of shameful imagery from Americas past wed with a Polynesian aesthetic to challenge and bring into sharp focus the disparity between reality and Tiki culture.

Ya'll will remember that Sua did the first Blue Scholars Artist Edition shirt, and Darvin Vida did all of the artwork for Gabriel Teodros' album "Lovework."

2007-07-07 | Saturday



after the show in NYC, the homie Phil Chang and i went around Brooklyn and the Lower East Side checkin out all the uber-cool fashionistas, streetwear aficionados, and other members of New York's high but not so high and just plain not so high (and some high) snobiety.



i'd just met Phil a couple days earlier at the Blue Scholars show--a show that i happened to be performing at--thru the other homie DanDiggity, our San Jose connect. Phil came out to interview us for The Royal Magazine and we ended up choppin it up about this and that and i decided to tag along with him when he went out to interview some NYC/Brooklyn clothing brands to see watt it do and the like.

-------DAY 1-------



the first stop was the office of Kilo International | Kilo GOODS | Rocksmith. our dood Ralph works at this spot and works with a magazine called Kicksclusive. in case you fools forgot, Kicksclusive is the crew that helped organize the NY show with hella help from the homegirl Lauren at Sony. some might remember Rocksmith for their Andy Warhol-esque ODB hoodie and shirt that caught hella attention a couple seasons back. i'm not sure how to describe the Rocksmith and Kilo GOODS gear... all i can say is that it makes me think of Miami, the 80's, hip-hop, cocaine (at least, like it used to be), and BMX bikes for some reason.



i gotta chance to meet all the good folks at the Kilo office including Kenshin, Lin Yee, and Erik who laced me with the the KOOL (Moe Dee) shirt you'll see in the later photos. big up Kilo! filth to you, my friends. filth to you.

after that i actually had to bounce to Queens to visit the other good folks at the Philippine Forum and unwittingly stumble upon Hari's old stomping ground, SAYA. but that's another story.

-------DAY 2-------



before we left for NYC i had met this dood Craig and a couple of his associates from Frank151 at the Doug Fir in Portland. we chatted briefly and i offered them some of our MassLine/BAYANI bandanas to take back as souvenirs. they all chose blue for some reason. east coast folks always choose blue and never black. for some reason, blue rags just aren't popular in the west, hmm...

anyway, Craig invited us out to check out Frank's Chop Shop on Essex when we got out there, so that was the next stop for Phil and i.



"The Chop Shop doubles (or, triples, rather) as a full-on old-school barber shop, a fitted hat retailer and the offices of stuntastic publication Frank 151." - Phil's Facebook.

there i met the homie Todd who generously laced me with the Limited SEATTLE LEGENDS New Era. Oooooh, Daps was about to be jealous.

i thought it ironic that i had to get out to New York to get this cap, but hey, all's well that ends well. *Seattle Trivia*: the image on the hat was crafted by our very own Iosefatu Sua whom ya'll might remember as the first Blue Scholars Limited Edition Handstyle T artist. keep an eye out, the Red/Black version is supposed to come out soon.



then we took some more pictures and bounced. (if you look close, you can see that i clearly didn't have time for a much needed haircut) thanks Frank 151! filth to you, my friends. filth to you.

-------The Reed Space-------

Phil and i then took the train deeper into the Lower East Side and stopped at a spot called Reed Space. this place had everything from clothes, to sneakers, to vinyl toys, to even artsy laptop bags. of course, none of their technology accessories could compare to my Pars Kid laptop sleeve i got from Electric Baby, but you can't hate on good folks for trying. at Reed Space we chatted briefly with the folkers there, including this one really sarcastic dood named Tim who had forsaken his San Francisco home to move to NYC for what he called "greener pastures." i told him he sucked for moving from the West Coast. then he made fun of my hat cuz it said "Seattle" and then asked about the Kool Moe Dee shirt. i said people in Seattle idolize Kool Moe Dee as the illest MC to come out before '91. he said "oh." haha, i shure clowned him. nobody in Seattle likes Kool Moe Dee.



-------SHQ-------

after Reed Space, we walked a couple of blocks to a place called Sugar Head Quarters. correct me if i'm wrong, but that spot turned out to be where the friendliest people in the Lower East Side stay at. as we walked in two doods dressed in nothing but Bape--Bape shoes, Bape shirts, Bape hats, and man... who knew they made Bape codpieces?--smiled really big at me. i thought it was an NYC rule that you weren't supposed to smile while wearing Bape, but oh well.



Phina and Carol expressed their disappointment for missing the Blue Scholars show the other night and so i had to pile on top of them about a 100 stickers to compensate.



this other dood Hugh who worked there and epitomized the essence of New York style told me about how much he disliked Hyphy music and thought it absolutely necessary that Cazal's be worn with Shell Toes. his knowledge of designer eyewear boggled my mind and i'm forever indebted to him for letting me try on several pairs of $500 glasses to pose like a poser for our pics.



they blessed me with a shirt and we headed out to DUMBO. all MassLiners who are in the NYC area should hit up the SHQ in the Lower East Side. filth to you my friends!

-------DUMBO-------

Dumbo is a neighborhood in Brooklyn where a grip of streetwear brands keep their headquarters. KILO and 10DEEP were the folks that we'd be seeing on this trip, but before heading to the Tenth Division, we stopped off at the workshop of Osamu Koyama.



until that day, i'd only seen a few pieces of jewelry from CompleteTechnique and wasn't quite familiar with their origin, but Phil was kind enough to introduce me to the man behind it all. you kno those silver/gold/platinum Shure-MM47 needles on necklaces you sometimes see people wearing? that's this dood.



he had hella stuff ranging from 45 adaptors, Shure headshells, Ortofons, headphones... even a custom MPC he was working on for Hi-Tek that shined so bright the doods in UGK would have said "you ain't a pimp you's a FEH-REH..... no, no wait... i take that back."



i was very intrigued and continued to inspect his goods until it slowly dawned on me that i obviously couldn't afford anything there so we got out quick so as not to take up more of Osa's time. big up for lettin us come thru cuzzo!

-------10DEEP-------

we then went upstairs to the offices of the Tenth Division AKA 10DEEP. this brand has come up in the last year. there we got a chance to chop it up briefly with ANT, a dood that apparently i had met before but we couldn't figure out exactly where. maybe it was the API Summit... oh well.



10DEEP was the line that unwittingly shouted out the Northwest last fall with their hoodie they dropped that looked like a flannel lumberjack thing for hip hop kids.

they were busy packing for their jump off in Barcelona with Crooks & Castles so we chopped it up for a bit, i broke off ANT with a copy of the new record, and we were out.



-------

much love to all the folks in NYC. peace to Ralph, Sony RED, Lauren Barford, CAUSE, Yamez, the Jesse and the doods at Alife, Asim the friendliest cab driver in Queens, the ladies at SAYA, the Philippine Forum, FATBEATS, KJ Butta (transplant!), the residents of Clinton Hill, all the street teamers who put stickers on trains and NYPD traffic control vehicles (!!), and anyone else i forgot. we'll see you when we get out there again!

Saba



2007-07-06 | Friday

James Vincent DuRuz, 1981 - 2007



Spiritual existence is immortality.  Non-existence therefore is an expression applied to change of form, but this transformation can never be rightly considered annihilation, for the elements of composition are ever present and existent as we have seen in the journey of the atom through successive kingdoms, unimpaired; hence there is no death; life is everlasting. 

abdu'l baha

Though we mourn the loss of a true comrade in the struggle for change, we celebrate his release from the confines of earthly existence and pray that he will continue to assist us from beyond.   It is immensely difficult to find words that adequately express both the sorrow we feel from the separation and the solace we take from knowing he is no longer harassed by the troubles of this world.

 

You may have seen Jimmy from time-to-time around the town fighting crime (as he was often called to do), playing his guitar on the Ave, checking out stacks of cds from the library, or  taking in a show to satisfy his insatiable desire to stay connected to music.  Jimmy had a vision that music would be the driving force in societal change at the global level; his work will undoubtedly be carried on by the folks at massline.

 

A private memorial service will be held tonight at sunset to celebrate Jimmys life through song and dance.   Friends are asked to contribute to the memorial by simply performing an act of service in Jimmys name.  If youve not already done so, please take a moment to visit this site honoring his memory:

 

 http://jamesvincentduruz.virtual-memorials.com/

 

Please leave a comment to let the masses know just how much we appreciate those willing to sacrifice their lives for the betterment of humanity, by any means necessary.

 

Say whattup to Dilla for me, my brother.  Remain.In.Position.
