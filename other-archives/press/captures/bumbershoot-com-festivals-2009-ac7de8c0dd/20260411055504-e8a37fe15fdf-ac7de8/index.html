Overview

Bumbershoot '09

Official Poster Artwork: The New Year

In 2009, Bumbershoot faced one of the most challenging environments in its modern history.

The festival was still operating under the programming and promotional partnership between One Reel and AEG Live, but external conditions made the year difficult. The Great Recession significantly reduced discretionary spending nationwide, affecting attendance and sponsorship across the live events industry.

At the same time, heavy rain during the festival weekend dampened turnout for the outdoor event. Combined with the broader economic downturn, the conditions contributed to financial losses for the festival’s promoters.

In later accounts, AEG described experiencing “significant losses” during this period, highlighting the growing challenge of sustaining a large, multi-day outdoor festival with rising production costs and economic uncertainty.

The difficulties of 2009 illustrated the delicate balance Bumbershoot had always navigated: maintaining an ambitious, multidisciplinary civic festival while adapting to changing economic realities. Even so, the event continued presenting its characteristic mix of music, film, visual arts, performance, and community programming across Seattle Center.

Nearly four decades after its founding, Bumbershoot had weathered economic downturns before. The challenges of 2009 became another chapter in the festival’s long history of adapting to shifting cultural and financial landscapes.
