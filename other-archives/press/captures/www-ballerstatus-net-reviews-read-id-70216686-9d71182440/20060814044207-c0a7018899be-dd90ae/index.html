Blue Scholars
Who: Blue Scholars
Rating: Image not recovered

� by Tyra Greene

If you're tired of the normal formula of today's rap music, explore your neo-soulism through the likes of the Blue Scholars, hip-hop's newest MC and DJ combination. The group formed circa 2002 as a meeting of diverse minds: Geologic, a battle rapper and Sabzi, a former punk drummer and pianist seemed to be an odd fit. But, one listen to their self-titled debut album and you'll admit that the duo is on to something.

Their sound is reminiscent of early '90s East Coast hip-hop, where you didn't have to necessarily rhyme about money, jewelry, and women to get recognition. But, this works for and against the group, due to the fact that the majority of listeners won't know what to do with their blend of poetics over raw turntable sounds. On the contrary, the album still manages to be a pretty solid effort.

Scholars seem to be the perfect name for this group as their main goal is to school listeners in 15 tracks or less. Many songs seem to have a political feel such as "Blue School," an introduction of sorts to Geologic's mind frame and influence as a leader which states "When the revolution comes/ we gon' shoot your ass first." Likewise, "Blink" is a striking tale of the conspiracy that is the United States of America. The lyricists swiftly and efficiently storm about the casualties of war -- both Iraqi civilians and low-income soldiers who have been summoned by a backdoor draft. Songs like these speak testimony to the pain that many listeners can probably relate to while simultaneously speaking truths that may be hard to swallow.

While many of the album's tracks take on similar verbal fire, another noticeable aspect of the Blue Scholars is the production, provided solely by Sabzi. The best way to describe the sound is melodic, combining soulful aspects of his formal instrument training with scratching and old school drumming. But, listeners may find themselves questioning whether or not it is too old school to enjoy. Songs like "Bruise Brothers" is lyrically interesting, but reminds you of the classic musical approach brought to us by the likes of early Rakim, Nas or Pete Rock, who just happen to be some of the group's influences. And with the majority of beats sounding as such, it has potential to put the audience in a mood of what used to be, which may or may not be a good thing.

While most songs don't provide direct uplift (such as "No Rest for the Weary", a realistic view of the black man's struggle), the Blue Scholars provide a consistent view of the ways of the world. The lyrical content is strong and refreshing, while the beats take a trip down memory lane. But, while neither Geologic nor Sabzi's individual skills and contributions can be denied, their new approach to revisiting the past may be too progressive for some. However, perhaps progressive is just what today's listeners need for the soul.
