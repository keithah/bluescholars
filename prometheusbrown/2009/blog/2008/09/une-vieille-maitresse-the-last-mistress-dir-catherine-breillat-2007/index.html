Une vieille maîtresse (The Last Mistress) (dir. Catherine Breillat, 2007)

Posted on September 3, 2008
Filed Under Film Reviews |

Une vieille maîtresse (The Last Mistress, 2007)
dir. Catherine Breillat
114 min.
France

For the first 20 minutes, Une vielle maîtresse (The Last Mistress, 2007) looks and feels like a conventional bourgeois European period flick (the title doesn’t help, either), which is usually just an excuse to make elaborate sets and costumes and not have to cast any brown or black people. But as soon as pre-metro playboy Ryno de Marigny (Fu’ad Ait Attou) begins narrating/confessing the story of his ten-year affair with scowling mistress La Vellini (Asia Argento) on the eve of his marriage to Hermangarde (Roxane Mesquida), writer/director Catherine Breillat unveils something absorbing and political, if soapish.

The film’s many extended soft-core porn scenes never come as distraction or spectacle, but as an indispensable part of the narrative. We see Ryno and Vellini having break-up sex, make-up sex, arguing-while-fucking sex, hatefucking, lovemaking, and even one scene where she rapes him. History moving swiftly through flashbacks, we see him turn from pursuer to pursued; Vellini is no longer the object but the subject of his desire. He becomes the femme fatale and she becomes what we, in modern times, have come to call a stalker.

Patriarchal roles of the aggressive man and submissive woman are blurred, even reversed. Through Breillat’s lens, men are insecure and cowering, yet still masculine, while the women are confident and powerful, yet very much feminine. They still wear their prescribed gender roles in public, but it all goes to shit when it comes to fucking. It’s a direct challenge to traditional cinema’s gender politics, most overtly made in the wedding scene between Ryno and Hildegard. “Man did not come from woman but woman from man,” a priest recites from the bible, “Man was not created for woman but woman for man.” As the priest rambles on, Vellini looks down from the balcony with contempt. She knows that the priests’ words might apply to some women, like Hermangarde, but not to her. It’s one of many, many scenes in which Asia Argento dominates with her eyes alone (damn, she can do it even with her eyes closed). 

Sure, Argento plays a little into her character’s exoticness (she’s half-Italian, half-Spanish), but she never relies on it, and still manages to be sexy with an armpit full of hair, a grimace on her face, and a voice that sounds like she just smoked a blunt. Ryno, on the other hand, is a broken shadow of a man whom I increasingly wanted to see killed off with each passing minute. For provoking something other than indifference, Attou deserves credit, perhaps even more for holding his own alongside someone with Argento’s presence. Their chemistry is so palpable that it survives many outlandish, almost campy moments that usually kill a serious film relationship - like when he forces her to kiss him while they’re on horses or how she manages to come that loudly with neither of them moving.    

Une vieille Maîtresse modestly recasts gender politics in an oft-filmed, overly patriarchal period and style of film, but also plays right into a familiar type of statement that divorces it from other fronts, most recognizably class (and race, although we do see one Black maid in deep focus for two seconds). While many viewers might be comforted by the familiarity of aristocratic Europe for a film setting, I’m frankly fucking tired of it - where are the real people at? I don’t fault Breillat for this - of course, the film’s setting in bourgie 19th century France plays a significant social and moral background to how and why Ryno and Vellini’s relationship came to be, and, in some ways, a critique of it. But if I really wanted to see rich people crying over their problems that much, there’s always MTV or CW.
