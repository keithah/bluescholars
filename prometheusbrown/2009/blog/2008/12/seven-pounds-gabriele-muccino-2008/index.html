Seven Pounds (Gabriele Muccino, 2008)

Posted on December 27, 2008
Filed Under Film Reviews |

Seven Pounds (2008) | dir. Gabriele Muccino | 118 min | USA

Remember that one Fresh Prince of Bel-Air episode? The one where Will took a bullet for Carlton, who gets a gun and is about get his revenge on when Will screams on him to put the gun down? Tears flowing, eyebrows scrunched with righteous anger: the definitive WIll Smith sad Face. We would see That Face again at the end of the episode where he reunites with father briefly before he abandons him again.

Will Smith wears That Face for two straight hours in Seven Pounds, a reprisal of the conflicted noble role last seen in The Pursuit of Happyness (same director), but less hopeful. He’s Ben Thomas, a depressed IRS agent randomly selecting strangers to do something good for, with no explanation why. Or rather, he’s supposed to be Ben Thomas, but come on, we all know who it really is. Even when he’s Muhammad Ali, he’s still Will Smith.

Thus, he is a double-edged sword in every flick he’s in. Somehow, despite the meandering melodrama and frustrating classical-emo score, I couldn’t stop watching. Even as they somehow turned the usually-vibrant Rosario Dawson into cardboard, I kept watching. Why? Again, Will Smith. For those of us who first encountered dude back when he was making records with Jazzy Jeff, he’s That Dude. The familiar face we’ve grown up with and watched go from He’s the DJ, I’m the Rapper to the sitcom to a string of summer blockbusters to galactic baller status. 

He’s the 80s baby hip-hop head’s thespian of choice, and now he’s crossing over into the 60s baby white mom crowd with fluff like this. Moving ever so further away from Denzel and creeping closer to Morgan. Which is not a good look, at least for him. As far as I’m concerned, Will has one pass left. Two, if he can next deliver what he does best: a sci-fi action flick in the vein of I, Robot or I Am Legend. Or even Men in Black. If not, I don’t think I’d mind hearing dude rap again, considering what’s “hot” now.
