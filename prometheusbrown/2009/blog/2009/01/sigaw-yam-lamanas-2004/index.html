Sigaw (Yam Laranas, 2004)

Posted on January 28, 2009
Filed Under Film Reviews, Philippine Cinema |

Sigaw (2004) | dir. Yam Laranas | 112 min | Philippines (Tagalog)

Is there any life left in the Asian horror genre for the American market to (have enough profit incentive to) steal from? Nothing since The Ring or The Grudge has found the same success. The Philippines has made some decent horror (Halimaw Sa Banga, Gerry De Leon’s 50’s joints) but has only recently jumped on this newer, stylized Asian psych-horror bandwagon. Of these flicks, Sigaw (2004) was singled out for an American remake (The Echo) that apparently has been filmed but I haven’t heard much of recently. Did we come to the party too late?

Ideally, Sigaw itself would get the recognition (and criticism) it deserves with or without a remake, but sadly, it doesn’t work that way. So I guess I’ll have to play the role of that guy who rides for the original even if no one’s listening, while inadvertently doing publicity for the remake - a win-win for everybody.  

In the OG film, director Yam Laranas shows a gifted sense of dramatic space in a place with very little of it. Someone’s far - intrigue and suspicion. Someone’s near - caution and suspense. Someone’s unexpectedly close - scream! It’s all a mash of familiar techniques (out-of-focus to focus reveals, high-pitched one-note stabs) but still, it’s done well minus the terrible audio mixing (the artistic achilles heel - and sometimes unintentional stamp of realism - of Philippine cinema).

It’s strength is its genuinely creepy, death-green-tinted Resident Evil (the video game) look of the dilapidated apartment building most of the film is set in. The simplicity of the haunting motif (a domestic abuse/murder) yields to the visual, but, like many Philippine films, keeps an otherwise unrealistic fantasy anchored in a real-life social situation. The twists and reveals are predictable, but Yan’s sharp photography (he was cinematographer for Raymond Red’s Sakay) almost makes the plot’s shortcomings forgivable. Plus, Angel Locsin is in it.

Generally, I’d have reservations about the whitewashed remake, but word that Laranas is also directing The Echo has me interested. You’d think that us ghost-obsessed people would have more to offer in this genre, right? I have an unscientific theory that Filipinos’ oral tradition of ghost stories and supersition are so good and theatric already that we don’t have or want to pay to see/hear them. So I’m rooting for Yam and The Echo, riding for Sigaw (flaws and all), and clinging to the hope that this may help, in whatever way, catalyze the release of more quality Filipino horror films.
