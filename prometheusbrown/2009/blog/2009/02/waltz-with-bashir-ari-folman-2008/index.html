Vals Im Bashir/Waltz With Bashir (Ari Folman, 2008)

Posted on February 5, 2009
Filed Under Film Reviews |

Vals Im Bashir/Waltz With Bashir (2008) | dir. Ari Folman | 86 min | Israel

Speaking of war and occupation, I finally saw Waltz With Bashir - an animated docudrama about Israel Defense Force (IDF) veterans who recall their experiences in the 1982 Sabra and Shatila massacres. Much of the hype surrounding the film celebrates not only its audiovisual technique, but its historical relevance to the current situation in Gaza. It got a limited US release this past month, gaining ground as the front runner for an Academy Award - Best Foreign Film (it already won the Golden Globe).

What would otherwise be a boring series of talking heads reminiscing about the war becomes an alternate reality where vectorized faces and bodies plunge in and out of their days as soldiers. Colors and images swirl and rearrange to illustrate each story viscerally. The extremities of sound and image are heightened to match the extremity of the violence, from a deathly gray battle aftermaths and an apocalyptic yellow dream sequence to a soundtrack that alternates between techno (violence) and Bach (innocence). War is the topic of conversation, memory is the theme of the narrative, and for every minute I was awed by the visuals, i spent two more trying not to think of it as a gimmick (Waking Life, anyone?).

On the other hand, this film left me with the same unsettling feeling I get when I would read first hand accounts of US soldiers writing home during the Philippine-American War. It confirms the ruthlessness inflicted on ordinary people while illustrating the psychological and physical damage inflicted on the soldiers, as well as their thoughts and motivations, many of whom questioned privately the orders they would take. But it happened anyway. Waltz With Bashir spares nothing in presenting the anguish and suffering of Palestinian refugees - especially the final scene that interrupts the animated images with real footage. But it fails to say anything substantial beyond the liberal view that “war makes victims of us all.”
