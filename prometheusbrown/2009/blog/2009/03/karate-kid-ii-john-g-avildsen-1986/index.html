Karate Kid II (John G. Avildsen, 1986)

Posted on March 1, 2009
Filed Under Film Reviews |

 

When Tamlyn Tomita pulled the chopsticks out of her in hair in Karate Kid II (1986), oh boy. The cascading wave of black velvet, the shedding of a longstanding innocence, the reckoned invitation. I’ve loved her and green tea since. Come to think of it, young Tamlyn also bears strange resemblance to someone I know very, very well. I must’ve seen the flick at least ten more times shortly after. None of which has been in the last 8-10 years once I got too grown to pretend it wasn’t a wack movie–half as good as the original, ten times worse with the Asian stereotypes and, tragically, no Cobras. Pat Morita (one step forward) as Mr. Miyagi (two steps back) drops even more gems in this one (”Rule number two: first learn rule number one.”), the Yoda to Ralph Macchio’s Luke. Except this time, I was rooting for the darkside, or, rather, the yellow side, led by Yuji Okumoto, who now runs a Hawaiian/Japanese style Seattle restaurant frequented by couples whose racial dynamic his character in this movie would’ve objected to. 

But there’s one remaining redeeming aspect of this film. Kumiko. And with great joy and disappointment, I find out that Tamlyn was among the attendees of the A Song For Ourselves premiere screening we performed at last night in LA, but I never got a chance to say hello. She heard a brother rap though, and that’s enough for me.
