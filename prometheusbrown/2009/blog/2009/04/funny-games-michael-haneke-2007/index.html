Funny Games (Michael Haneke, 2007)

Posted on April 16, 2009
Filed Under Film Reviews |

Funny Games (2007) wants you to do two things most normal people rarely ever do at the same time: (1) be unsettled by its violence and (2) not take it seriously at all. And it would’ve succeeded in its mission if, instead of it trying so hard to remind us that its actually clowning its genre (even by way of title choice), it just went ahead and did it and left it up to us to decide.

Two obnoxious, whimsical psychopaths take a family hostage and tortures em. Calm weirdo type shit that might be shocking to someone who hasn’t seen American Psycho or A Clockwork Orange. Or worse, Salo. It flaunts its senselessness, urging us to become frustrated for the victims as it becomes apparent that they will not be granted the gratifying triumph we want so badly for them. Poor bastards. 

Austrian director/writer Michael Haneke, who remade his original Austrian flick (1997) of the same name, crafts the thriller with some good suspense though, paying homage to and satirizing the slasher genre. But when cliches are thrown in, the characters can’t help but comment on it, actually saying shit like “it’s like a movie.” If the unsubtlety of the joke weren’t enough, wait until the remote control scene (spoiler alert). Homegirl (Naomi Watts) grabs an idle shotgun and blasts one of her would-be killers. But wait. Other dude grabs a remote control and rewinds the whole scene, grabbing the gun before she can use it. Even Scream, the ultimate self-reflexive horror flick, didn’t go that far.

With their all-white golf outfits and young aristocrat hair, the two killers were designed a little too well to be hated on. They might as well have worn smedium tees printed with “Hate Me” on it. So I couldn’t hate em enough to enjoy this, which also meant that I didn’t care enough for their victims to be jarred. All I felt was contempt and little admiration–not for any of the characters–but for the director. For at least attempting something different in the tired slasher genre. But also for wasting what could’ve easily been a cult classic if it didn’t already act like it was one before the end-credits rolled.

    When I saw the original it blew me away. The feeling that there’s no escape is really smothering–that’s the ultimate scary for me. I walked away from it mesmerized by the idea that the villain could have ultimate control. Even in films that don’t end happily you can still walk away feeling like a voyeur and not one of the victims. That said, I wasn’t a fan of the remake. It felt forced and pretentious. If it’s not an original, and it’s not even better than the original, at least be humble about what you’re trying to do. I love the concept but as far as execution, I would recommend someone check out the original for sure.
