Guest Review: Transformers 2: Revenge of the Fallen (Michael Bay, 2009)

Posted on June 26, 2009
Filed Under Film Reviews |

Transformers 2: Revenge of the Fallen (2009) | dir. Michael Bay | 150 min | USA
Guest contribution by Wes Goodlife (WesGoodlife.com)

Tuesday is usually never a good day to do anything, but at 11pm I found myself with the wife surrounded by young people (I’m getting so old) at the Southcenter Mall waiting to get the IMAX experience for what I’m hoping will be this summer’s blockbuster, Transformers 2: Revenge of The Fallen.

Helmed by the dream team of Michael Bay and Steven Spielberg, Transformers starts off with a battle that sets the tone for the rest of the movie: loud, large, expensive, confusing to watch (All the robots blend together to me), but damnit if it wasn’t cool.  Michael Bay is the Rembrandt of large action films. I can honestly say that I believe no one does better when it comes to Summer blockbusters.

I don’t think that there is any point to dive into the plot, but I can give you this brief rundown: Robots are violent, some talk about ending the world, the best slow-motion shots ever, and then more robot violence. In a nutshell, the film is very entertaining. It was not the blockbuster that I wanted for the Summer, but it was way better than Terminator and X-Men: Origins.

After the film wrapped up, the wife and I got into a deep discussion about two characters in the film that rubbed me the wrong way. I don’t wanna pull the race card or be that dude calling for a boycott of the film, but the twins Mudflap and Skidz (who was voiced by Spongebob Squarepants’ Tom Kenny) really Michael, really? Why in the world would there be a need for hood ass robots with gold teeth to play as comic relief on a minstrel show level?. I hated watching them on screen and the sad part was that they were actually funny and kinda cute, but gotdamnit why the gold teeth?  On the first Transformers I was mad that the “black robot” Jazz was the only one who felt the urge to breakdance while he transformed, and he was the only one of the Autobots to die.  At the end of the first film Jazz was no where in sight, but in this film he’s in the opening scene as one of the main robots blending together and kicking ass, and I’m happy to report: no breakdancing.

The twins aren’t my only gripe about the “social undertones” of this film.  From the woman in the film is dressed skimpy and sexual charged to the other racial stereotypes that are splattered all over this film. The introduction of Megan Fox sees her bent over a bike with little butt cheeks spilling out of her little short-shorts. I’m not complaining, just saying.

When Michael Bay was asked about the situation with Skidz and Mudflap, he replied by saying something along the lines of “I wanted bots with whom the younger audience could really identify.” I don’t think Michael Bay is a racist at all. He is Hollywood, and Hollywood is the most sensitive insensitive place in the world. The twins wouldn’t make me not want to see the film again, but I think I would have liked it more if they weren’t there. At the end of the day, though, I would never love it. The film is just too shallow.

Wes Goodlife is an aspiring film maker and blogger residing in Seattle.  His main focus is on music videos and short films. His work can be seen at www.wesgoodlife.com.
