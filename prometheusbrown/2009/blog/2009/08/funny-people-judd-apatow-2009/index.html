Funny People (Judd Apatow, 2009)

August 13, 2009



Funny People (2009) | dir. & wr. Judd Apatow | 146 min |

Funny People wastes no time establishing the plot. Struggling lonely comic George Harrison (Adam Sandler) finds out he has a rare form of cancer and starts falling into the darkside. He fails at his latest gig but meets his new sidekick Seth Rogen, who he hires as a writer/assistant/emotional connection. He makes a minor comeback as a dying comedian as the story finds a nice balance of drama and humor. Judd Apatow (40 Year Old Virgin, Knocked Up) finds Sandler–his former roommate–as comfortable onscreen as ever, reminding us that he can actually be funny without being a complete d-bag.

Has Judd Apatow finally evolved the “bromance comedy”? For the first half of this long-ass film, yes. The behind-the-scenes look at stand-up comedy is as sharp as you’d expect from a whole crew of folks who came up in that hustle. RZA makes a funny cameo. Sandler and Rogen are great together onscreen, and the whole dying thing is twisted humorously. It would’ve been dope to carry this plot all the way through to the end. But that doesn’t happen. Somewhere along the way, a rekindled old flame derails this happy train. Good for George, bad for the chemistry Sandler and Rogen had earlier, and bad for us. This right here is a straight-up romance comedy drama, and no amount of cock jokes will fool us otherwise. Which is cool, if that’s your thing. Cock jokes and romance. Together.
