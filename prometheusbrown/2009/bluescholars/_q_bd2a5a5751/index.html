The Travels

Sabzi in Calcutta, pt. 1

01.04.08 | 1 Comment

2007 was a big year for us. MassLine tour jumped off, Bayani dropped, travelled hella, RA and i finished the new Common Market record, a bunch of other stuff happened, then more stuff happened after that, and then we finished out the year with 5 days at Neumo’s for The Program.

most working folk in the West, if they are fortunate enough to make a living with just one job, get their weekends, some sick days, and a few holidays off. not quite the case for griots such as yours truly. like Geo said, “what you’re doin as a hobby, we do it for food.” i think the last real break i took from work was almost 3 years ago in ‘05 before The Long March came out.

anyway, long story made slightly less long, i decided to go back to my roots. that’s why i’m writing this blog from Bangalore, Karnataka, India. but Saba, roots? India? explain!

i’m Parsi, you didn’t know? SIKE. (that’s for the 6 people that would get that joke and think it’s funny, and fans of Queen.)

i’d like to go to Iran, but currently as a Baha’i holding a US Passport and assumed Iranian citizenship, it isn’t the best environment. hmm, how close can i get… Iran is in between two other countries, Iraq and Pakistan. as tempting as it would be to visit folks in Pakistan, or maybe join the resistance in Baghdad, i have fam in Seattle that expect me to be let back into the US next month and i can’t really let them down. so, i decided to visit a friend of mine in India to see what he’s up to.

residing in Calcutta, my friend (let’s call him, um, “Paul”) is currently working with local NGOs and Microsoft to develop a radical new form of community based schooling involving DVDs, centralized curriculum digitization, and the local postal service. over a cup of Assam he broke down the simplified version for me in only 2 hours. one of the working titles is “the educational YouTube for rural and urban poor youth.” mmm, sounds interesting. i always enjoy engaging fam and friends in their community work, particularly outside Seattle, to see if there’s anything i can learn and take back to implement into what we got goin on in the Town. but we’ll get to that later…

we’ll be headed to Calcutta in a few days, but until then, it’s Bangalore baby. at a 3000 ft elevation, the air here is crisp, warm… it almost feels like summer in Seattle, but with significantly less white people.

my trip to get here was also interesting.

my itinerary was Tel Aviv –> Kiev –> Delhi –> Banglore. i’d rather not get into the first two because one involved getting hella grilled for my last name, being ordered to remove my outer garments for a search, and sitting in a hospital like cubicle for 30 minutes while security went through all my photos and personal belongings… but somehow, remained notably more polite than TSA.

this will officially be my second iPhone foto jurnelle.

this is the only entertaining photo from Tel Aviv’s Ben Gurion Airport. well, entertaining to me. i think i have a mild dyslexia, cuz everytime i saw this i thought it said “love a bitch.” 30 Shekels says Snoop Dogg doesn’t bank there.

(PS, that joke is very offensive.  i’m sorry)

maybe it was cuz i hadn’t slept for 24 hours, but it appeared that the Kiev waiting room didn’t have much to offer either, besides this ad in the men’s room.

usually, “before & after” shots also include the subject’s change in attitude. the old man really appreciates his new mop, but it appears that the young dood on the upper right thought he was just as much of a player when he was bald.

the flight from Kiev to Delhi was about the same as Seattle to NYC. long enough to be irritating, but short enough that people don’t take you seriously when you complain about how long it was. when i got thru customs in Delhi to begin enjoying my 7 hour layover, the first thing i saw was this.

the picture isn’t that great, so you can’t appreciate how many people were there holding signs for clients and family, and how grimy the airport was… at least when i was there. they appeared to be remodeling it.

next, i went straight to the Nescafe stand and got myself a coffee.

it cost Rs. 10 (ten Rupees), which is about 25 cents. what a ripoff!

i had to find a bus to take me to the domestic-flights section of the Indira Gandhi International Airport. after dodging about 6 different guys who claimed to be the bus driver and pointing at an old Datsun minivan saying “this is the bus,” i realized i still looked like a foreigner. i needed change my clothes so i could convince people that i was from northern Punjab. even then, it only works about 10% of the time, but it’s fun anyway.

eventually i found a real bus with a real driver who took about 34 of us on a 25 minute bus ride to the other side of the airport where they have the domestic flights.

on the back of the bus i met a young guy whose shirt said, in big white trapstar-style block lettering, “YOU LOOK LIKE MY NEXT EX GIRLFRIEND.” i shook my head in disgust at how America pawns off its shitty Hot Topic novelty clothing to the developing world.

for those who don’t know, a lot of companies that can’t pawn off their textile commodities to the American public end up trying to recoup their losses by dumping their remaining stock in overseas markets. this happens frequently. this is why “western fashion” in a lot of countries below the equator is so corny. you’ll be walking around a village and see “new” old NO FEAR tees, or shirts that say “YOU’RE JUST JEALOUS BECAUSE THE VOICES IN MY HEAD DON’T TALK TO YOU” or “SUCK IT,” or championship tees for the losing team because the NBA merchandising company did two different prints before the game like “Seattle SuperSonics 1996.” honestly, there’s a better joke in there, but i’m waiting for Geo or Hari to write it.

at least, i thought all this this until i talked to the young man wearing the shirt. turns out his name was Daniel, a South Asian American from Queens on his first trip to India. Daps1 then jumped out the bushes, read the previous paragraph, and said “that’s racist!”

when we got to the domestic side of the airport, i discovered it was considerably nicer than the international side. i assumed this was intentional. you know, give the shit end of the stick to the colonizer like they did to you for so many years. kudos, India. kudos.

i looked around for some food. first thing i found was this

what you can’t see are the employees watching Yo! MTV Raps on the side of the stand. the last time i had Chinese food in India was a couple years ago… like Tim Armstrong in ‘91, it was absolutely rancid. (badum, chssh!)

not feelin that. so i hit up the cafeteria for a snack. the server recommended a dish, the name of which i can’t remember. i’m pretty sure it was spelled with an “H” following another consonant.

this was a dollar. what a ripoff!

still, it wasn’t hard to give a 20% tip to the kind gentleman that brought me my meal. what was hard was seeing his boss come take it from him as i was leaving.

then, in my sleep deprived delirium, i spent the rest of my layover wandering the airport and taking pictures of stuff that made me laugh.

this is a cellphone charging station with about 12 different plugs for various phone models. not that funny. what was funny was seeing all these people come up to use it and being unable to find one that matched their phone. damn free markets!

this ad sounds like they don’t want the whiskey. like, “yea Johnnie. keep walking, asshole. get the fuck outta here.”

oh good, now i can use the toilet. but wait a minute…

there’s no toilet, no sink… just a dood sleeping on a table. holy shit, it really is a “rest” room.

since when was “coffee” a verb?

the squares aren’t allowed to travel with these guys. the staff is also a bunch of pretentious brand-blockers with 300 pairs of Nikes.

ha! “Easons Greeting” what the hell does that mean? oh wait… nevermind.

here’s me taking a picture of myself in the bathroom mirror. not that funny. however, i find it amusing that now there’s a guy in India who no idea that there’s a photo of him taking a piss at a urinal on the internet. muahahaha.
