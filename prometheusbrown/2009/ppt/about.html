THE PEOPLE POWER TOUR

Two of the West Coast's premier Filipino emcees, Geologic aka Prometheus Brown (of Seattle's Blue Scholars) and San Francisco's Kiwi (by way of Los Angeles), formerly of Native Guns, are teaming up again to rally youth and students nationwide to raise awareness about the worsening social, political and economic conditions in the Philippines under president Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo's (GMA) current administration.

In March 2007, Kiwi and Geologic embarked on the Stop the Killings Tour - a two-week, 8-city tour to raise awareness of the over 800 (now over 900) extra-judicial killings and other human rights violations. The tour successfully raised funds for families of the victims of the Philippine government to testify at the International People's Tribunal at the Hague, Netherlands. On March 26, 2007 the tribunal found president Arroyo's government guilty of crimes against the Philippine people.

On March 14, 2007 the US Senate Committee on Foreign Relations, Sub-Committee on East Asian and Pacific Affairs held a hearing attended by progressive Filipino Americans and families of those kidnapped or killed in the Philippines. At the hearing, Sen. Barbara Boxer commented, “It is important we (Americans) do not have blood on our hands... If we are training the (Philippine) military with our hard-earned tax dollars, our concern about the military cannot be discounted. (The) war on terror cannot be used as an excuse to kill innocent civilians.”

While 2007 has seen a decline in politically-motivated killings (a decline, not a stop), in 2008 political and economic conditions in the Philippines have worsened as the GMA administration is wrought in food and energy crises, corruption scandals (election fraud, corporate nepotism and kickbacks) and increasing US military presence threatening Philippine civilians' lives and livelihood (January 2008 Sulu Massacre, numerous military rape cases). Meanwhile, Filipinos continue to leave their home country to the US and all over the world, facing problems anew in their adopted homeland. A rapidly growing movement comprising all sectors of the Filipino community are mobilizing to oust the corrupt administration, as they have done successfully in the two previous non-violent People Power movements that ousted dictator Ferdinand Marcos (1986) and the corruption-ridden Joseph Estrada (2001).

Both Kiwi and Geo are active Filipino community organizers in their respective cities, drawing connections between the struggles of Filipinos in the US and their compatriots back home. They share the view that art is not only a reflection of the world, but a tool which can shape a more just future society. From the rapidly gentrifying street-corner to the Ivory Tower of academia, Kiwi and Geo bring forth a message of resistance and self-determination in the tradition of pro-people hip-hop music by such artists as Dead Prez, Public Enemy and KRS-One.

Proceeds from the tour will be donated to Bayan-USA, a coalition of progressive Filipino American organizations advocating for social justice and democracy in the Philippines.
