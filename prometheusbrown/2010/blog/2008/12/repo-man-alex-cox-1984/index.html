Repo Man (Alex Cox, 1984)

Posted on December 26, 2008
Filed Under Film Reviews |

Until last night, I had never seen Alex Cox’s 1984 cult classic Repo Man starring Emilio Esteves in a streetwise role as entertaining as it is unconvincing thanks to The Mighty Ducks trilogy. It happened to be on some random cable channel last night, and I was immediately captivated by its blending of dark humor, science fiction and subtle politics in recognizably absurd situations (Cox also later wrote the screenplay for Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas).

The film takes satirical jabs at everything 1980s, set in the most 1980s-defining of American cities: Los Angeles. Conspiracy-theorists, Christian evangelists, LA street gangs, LAPD all get lampooned as Otto (Esteves) reluctantly joins a ragtag bunch of cats who repossess cars for a living. Most of all, darts are thrown at a financial system in crisis and a government too incompetently focused on other shit (like extraterrestrials) to do anything about it.

Come to think of it, it’s not too different from America in 2008, fashion sense included. Which is probably why, for better or for worse, a sequel titled Repo Chick is currently in production, produced by David Lynch and with writer/director Alex Cox apparently involved somehow. Says he:

Cox has previously said that the follow-up will “unfold against the background of the credit crunch and the subprime mortgage crisis in the US, where repossessions of homes, cars and other forms of property is at a new high. The repo business has expanded to everything from boats, houses, aeroplanes, small nations…children.” (SlashFilm.com, December 2008)

Sounds fascinating. Perhaps it will be based on Waldo’s Hawaiian Holiday, a comic-book sequel to Repo Man released this past year.
