Vicky Cristina Barcelona (Woody Allen, 2008)

Posted on December 21, 2008
Filed Under Film Reviews |

 Vicky Cristina Barcelona | dir. Woody Allen | 96 min | USA

I’d say that Vicky Cristina Barcelona is delightful, except that I don’t ever use the word delightful unless I’m being sarcastic. And I’m not as big on sarcasm as Woody Allen, whose films I stopped paying attention to around the same time he hooked up with his adopted Korean daughter not too long ago (it’s never too long ago). I’ve seen most of the stuff between Annie Hall and Celebrity, and I’d have trouble telling you which was what if it weren’t for the actors. 

Or the settings. Through the most picturesque (and wealthy) parts of Barcelona, Vicky (Rebecca Hall) and Cristina (Scarlett Johansson) live it up. They have escaped the fascist state of America, bringing with them their country’s two biggest stereotypes for women. Vicky, about to get married, has her life figured out. But she’s repressed. Cristina is an aspiring uninspired artist, lost and trying to find herself. But always down for an adventure and the sex.

Then along comes charming artist Juan Antonio (Javier Bardem). Very much looking like an opiated Robert Downey Jr., he bags both of em (not at the same time, though he proposes it). It’s all entertaining, superficial fun with lots of sightseeing and Spanish guitar. Right on cue at the halfway mark, Juan Antonio’s ex-wife Maria Elena (Penelope Cruz) brings the crazy. Things get interesting for the next half hour.

Which leaves us, then, with fifteen minutes. The subtle, high-brow joke well runs dry before wrap-it-up time commences. Suddenly, the story shelves Cristina and Maria Elena, and even Juan Antonio, to focus on the least interesting character, Vicky (yes, even less interesting than one played by Scarlett Johansson.), and her squarish husband. He has arrived from the US to remind the audience why Americans are soulless moneychasers. Unlike the passionate, artistic Europeans. 

So what did Vicky do? Frankly, I didn’t care. And that’s the thing with me and Woody.The way I feel about his movies is the way some of his characters feel about each other: I enjoy it while it happens, but with an emotional disconnect and a strange blend of charm and annoyance. Plus, some Filipinos like myself aren’t as easily charmed by Spain as others (again, it’s never too long ago).
