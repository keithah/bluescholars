The Best TV Mini-Series Ever

Posted on January 20, 2009
Filed Under Television |

Watching the inauguration on TV this morning, my four-year old son exclaimed “it’s like a movie!” Not so much said in awe, but almost as if to ask, why? Yes, son, it’s finally here: the long-awaited final episode of The Best TV Mini-Series Ever. A climax and resolution to end a perfectly scripted, photographed, acted and marketed production, a landmark in American, and global, television history. Broadcast live, as it has been for the past year, in glorious HD.

However, this - the final episode - filmed with an astonishing Hollywood blockbuster budget ($150 million), fell short of previous efforts such as the suspenseful and celebratory November 4th episode, or the DNC nomination acceptance speech on the anniversary of the Martin Luther King, Jr. “I Have a Dream” speech (followed by the dramatic entry of a new villain the next day!). Today, a day after Martin Luther King, Jr. day, in front of a huddled mass awaiting a knockout punch, Barack Obama (as Barack Obama) played defense. Instead, he replaced the progressive, populist rhetoric of his campaign with familiar, presidential Bush-isms, asserting a bossness that hints at a possible sequel. He was, however, the highlight of a string of bland, safe speeches that killed whatever celebratory mood the people brought with them. Especially the god-awful poetry. Like most top-notch drama series finales (The Wire, The Sopranos), it seems that the penultimate episode will remain the most memorable, the finale serving more as a convenient bookend.

With most critics glowing over the star actor’s precise, cool year-long performance or his co-star’s costume design (especially Jason Wu’s contribution), the single most impressive presence in this production has, sadly, been overlooked. The American People, without whom all this history-in-the-making would just be… in-the-making. It was a performance  for the ages - well-timed tears and elation with righteous anger and occasional spontaneous dance numbers. You believed that yes, you could, and even made someone like me believe it too. I hope you remain the same way now that the cameras are off.
