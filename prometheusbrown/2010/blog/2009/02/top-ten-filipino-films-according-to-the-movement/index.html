Top Ten Filipino Films, According to The Movement

Posted on February 6, 2009
Filed Under Philippine Cinema |

One of the best and informative Filipino news sources online, Bulatlat.com, asked a cross-section of aktibistas in the Philippines what their top ten local films were. The poll was conducted in 2002 among 37 organizers representing people’s organizations and NGOs.  

Most of the films mentioned match up with many critics top lists, such as Lino Brocka, Ishmael Bernal and Mike De Leon’s martial-law era social critiques (Maynila - pictured above, Himala, Sister Stella L). But it’s interesting, but not surprising, to note how high Orapronobis (Brocka, 1988) - Brocka’s most overtly political film (and which has been banned from screening in the Philippines) - is ranked. It’s considered by many established Filipino critics to be one of his lesser works (I disagree). It also appears that we’ll forgive the limitations of historical epics or melodramas as long as it represents an important national issue, person, or event (the anti-US military base film Minsa’y Isang Gamu-Gamo, The Flor Contemplacion Story, Jose Rizal). I also dug the inclusion of Bayaning 3rd World (De Leon, 2000), described by one respondent as “funny, intelligent with Filipino petty bourgeoisie humor.”

I do wonder about the demographics - age, gender, class background - that delivered this response. Overall conslusion: aktibistas have great taste in Filipino film. So if you want to step your Filipino cinema game up, you know who to hit up. Here’s the rest of the list:

1. Maynila Sa Mga Kuko Ng Liwanag (Lino Brocka, 1975) - my review

2. Orapronobis (Lino Brocka, 1988) - my review

3. Himala (Ishmael Bernal, 1982) - my review

4. Sister Stella L. (Mike De Leon, 1984)

4. Tinimbang Ka Ngunit Kulang (Lino Brocka, 1974)

5. Minsa’y Isang Gamu-Gamo (Lupita Aquino-Kashiwahara, 1976) (tie)

5. Oro Plata Mata (Peque Gallaga, 1980) (tie)

6. The Flor Contemplacion Story (Joel Lamangan, 1995) (tie)

6. Bakit May Kahapon Pa? (Joel Lamangan, 1996) (tie)

6. Tatlong Taong Walang Diyos (Mario O’Hara, 1976) (tie)

7. Jose Rizal (Marilou Diaz-Abaya, 1998)

8. Bayaning 3rd World (Mike De Leon, 2000)

9. Sakay (Raymond Red, 1993)

10. Moral (Marilou Diaz-Abaya, 1982)

10. Manila By Night/City After Dark (Ishmael Bernal, 1980) - my review

10. Ganito Kami Noon, Paano Kayo Ngayon? (Eddie Romero, 1980)

Honorable Mention:
Batch ‘81 (Mike De Leon), Bagets (Maryo J. delos Reyes), Bayan Ko: Kapit sa Patalim (Lino Brocka), Insiang (Lino Brocka), Sakada (Behn Cervantes), Ligaya ang Itawag Mo Sa Akin (Carlitos Siguion-Reyna), Scorpio Nights (Peque Gallaga), Nunal Sa Tubig (Ishmael Bernal), Kisapmata (Mike de Leon), Lucia (Mel Chionglo), Sana Maulit Muli (Olivia Lamansan)

I think it’s time to do another poll, with a different demographic. Same question: what are your top ten Filipino films (if you’ve seen more then ten)?
