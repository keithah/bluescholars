Knowing (Alex Proyas, 2009)

Posted on April 3, 2009
Filed Under Film Reviews |

Knowing (2009) | dir. Alex Proyas | wr. Ryne Douglas Pearson | 121 min | USA

In Knowing (2009), Nicholas Cage plays an MIT professor who deciphers a random series of numbers written 50 years ago as a roadmap for future disasters leading to the apocalypse. Like all prototypical single movie fathers, he is overprotective of his child, watches old videos of his deceased wife, and his house is a mess. Like many prototypical movie men of science, he’s lost his faith (son of a pastor), is angry at God and is ripe for redemption. When his kid comes home with The Numbers, he googles the first sequence “91101″ (really?) and figures the shit out in less than 3 minutes. Cue slow motion shot of glass dropping from his hand to the floor, shattering into pieces like any chance this film had of being taken seriously.

When he’s not playing detective trying to dig up the mystery surrounding the girl who wrote The Numbers, disasters start happening–wherever he happens to be, and with increasing frequency. Everything about this flick’s storytelling is pandering and/or artificial, from the peeling paint in Nic’s rustic Boston home to the overly-haunting Shyamalanish score to the moment halfway through when the sci-fi elements yield to a shameless Jesus and His Rapture story. The directing (Alex Proyas - who helmed the half-aight I, Robot) is adept and standard quality Hollywood action fare, but the screenplay and story is beyond bad–it’s a new low, even by Cage standards (he starts Caging at about the 30-minute mark and never turns back). Worse than that, it’s irresponsible, Judeo-Christian Apocalyptic masturbation looking to profit from your hopelessness in the middle of a depression. Suggesting that this world, fucked as it is, is beyond hope and fixing, and, even worse, that global warming has nothing to do with our own doing and everything to do with a God-ordained sun flare functioning as a reset button for humanity, isn’t just harmless movie fantasy. It’s the type of lie that will fuck us all in the end, except with no silent angels in UFOs with lightbeams in their mouths running around to collect the chosen. Avoid at all costs.
