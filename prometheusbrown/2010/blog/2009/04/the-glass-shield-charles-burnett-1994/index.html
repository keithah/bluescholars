The Glass Shield (Charles Burnett, 1994)

Posted on April 8, 2009
Filed Under Film Reviews |

The Glass Shield (1994) was that movie we saw back in the mid-90s and thought was wack compared to the more outlandish gangbanging shit we hoped it would be. We heard Ice Cube was in it, so we expected Ice Cube shit. But nah. Instead, this flick was about some other guy–a cop (Michael Boatman, who has the most screentime but isn’t even on the poster) –the first and only Black man in his precinct, facing corruption and racism in his new workplace. Ice Cube was cast as a minor but pivotal figure: a fall guy that some crooked cops try to pin a murder on. Eager to get in with his fellow pigs, our guy forges a report to help convict the innocent Cube. Then he has a few conversations with a Jewish 90s feminist-type woman (Lori Petty, excellent) in the department who bursts his “I don’t see oppression” bubble. Suddenly, despite being treated like shit the whole time, he finally realizes that these white cops don’t like him. So he grows a conscience. Does he play along and put a fellow innocent Black man to jail? Or do the right thing whatever the consequence is?

In retrospect, The Glass Shield is a good, but not great, film. Its direction and photography subtle but sharp, story more nuanced, dialogue intelligent but believable. In other words, to those of us still high off The Hughes Brothers and John Singleton, it was boring. But looking back, it’s the simplicity of the story and its daring to keep racism and police corruption (and the connection between the two) in the conversation that makes The Glass Shield stand out from its peers a decade later. Yes, parts of it feel like a slow Law & Order episode. And some of the acting, particularly Cube’s, is on the weak side. But I can’t recall any other Hollywood film in the last 20 years, except perhaps Training Day, that goes in on police brutality and racism on an institutional level like this, and done well. More importantly, written and filmed by a Black man himself. It’s also the best film in Ice Cube’s whole catalog that doesn’t have the word Friday in it.
