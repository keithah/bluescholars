Trailer: Dukot (Desaparecidos)

August 13, 2009



Archived embedded media is not available for offline playback. Open its archived source

Having very mixed feelings about this one, which is described (in the film’s own words) as “a timeless, fearless film on human rights violation but also a love story.” The press release also name-drops Lino Brocka and Mike de Leon. The good intentions are there, as they are in director Joel Lamangan’s other films such as The Flor Contemplacion Story (1995). Less so in ones like Pacquiao: The Movie (2006). It seems that he, more than any other mainstream Philippine director, is the quickest to capitalize on a mass feeling, wrap it in soap-drama packaging with dramatic orchestral score, and sell it back to us with a love story. Which can be entertaining, but also hollowed-out of its substance. Our version of Hollywood. On the other hand, looking at many of the other mainstream Philippine films this year, this actually seems like a good look.

Either way, 2009 has become a coming-of-age year for the movement to expose the Arroyo regime’s rampant human rights violations. We’ve been hollering for years, and now, what was once painted as a leftist propaganda has gone mainstream. Melissa Roxas’s ordeal, which mainstream US media has heavily slept on, finally got a look in the New York Times yesterday. Seems that the truth can no longer be ignored.

Look, one of the kids in the film (at 0:30) is rocking the Sammy the Sunshine shirt our homies Pnoy Apparel brought to the PI and donated last year.
