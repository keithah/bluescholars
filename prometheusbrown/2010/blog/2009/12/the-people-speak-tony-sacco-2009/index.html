The People Speak (Tony Sacco, 2009)

December 13, 2009

My high school US history teacher, Mr. Thorsen, made us read the first chapter of this racist-ass standard textbook. Then, he made us read the first chapter of Howard Zinn’s A People’s History of the United States. Fucked me up and changed my whole world. A confirmation of things I’ve felt but could never articulate or point a finger at. Or maybe heard in a Public Enemy song. The truth about this “great” country, and how it became an empire.

Narrated by Howard Zinn himself in beautiful theater, The People Speak (2009), is a super-condensed documentary version of parts of A People’s History interspersed with famous people reading historical American speeches. DMC reads David Walker. John Legend sings a song. Jasmine Guy makes multiple cameos! Benjamin Bratt, Matt Damon, Viggo Mortensen and other well-known liberal thespians do their thing. It’s not as flashy as that list of personages makes it sound. It’s very much tailored to that “historical” vibe with all those slow-panning black-and-white photographs and sad music. It is, essentially, a well-orcestrated lecture.

Good thing the attempted Fox Television-produced (true story) mini-series A People’s History got derailed. Leave that in book form. Let people learn to read something in bound paper pages every once in a while. Instead, mash it up a little. Just like this. Maybe now,  the door’s open to try even more forms of disseminating this information. Because there’s something about the current form of “historical” documentary that still makes it feel like distant rather than applicable. The People Speak is a worthy attempt at repackaging the things every good progressive should already know into something that might be digested by someone who doesn’t. Especially one being aired on The History Channel, which has been on an Apocalypse documentary binge as of late. And though the segment on Philippine-American war was brief, at least it got mentioned. Not bad for a channel usually obsessed with Hitler and wars. Lupe Fiasco and Michael Ealy reciting Malcolm X. Until at least half the people who have a copy of A People’s History actually reads that shit, a win is a win.
