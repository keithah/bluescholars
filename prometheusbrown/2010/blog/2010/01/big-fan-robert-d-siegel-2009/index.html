Big Fan (Robert D. Siegel, 2009)

January 13th, 2010 | No Comments »

Never thought that the first movie I see starring Patton Oswalt would be this dark. Also didn’t think it would actually be this good. Big Fan (2009) is funny in spurts but mostly depressing has hell. Most stand-up comics dive into film on some slapstick shit, then go dramatic later. Oswalt said fuck that, I’m going straight to dramaville. He plays Paul Aulfiero, a parking attendant and New York Giants fanatic who lives with his nagging moms in Staten Island, has an extended family of sleazeball Italians, and masturbates nightly. The opening scene finds him at work, writing notes for his regular phone-in to the local sports radio station to spar with “Philadelphia Phil.” His mother interrupts his calls. A shot of Paul driving to the stadium has a rosary hanging from the rear view mirror placed prominently in the frame. Football is Paul’s religion, the Giants are his gods.

What emerges isn’t a dark comedy for the sake of being dark, nor a comedy failing to become a drama, but something unexpectedly neorealist-ish. Anything we find funny, such as Paul and his best friend going to Giants stadium on Sunday to watch the game in the parking lot, or his mother berating him, is also tragic. Paul, like Mickey Rourke’s Randy “The Ram” Robinson, is a sad sack of shit, but one we’re forced to try to understand rather than laugh at. After a chance encounter at a gas station with their favorite player, Quantrelle Bishop (“What’s he doing in Staten Island? Maybe he’s looking for The Wu-Tang”), he and his buddy follow him and his entourage to a strip club. He meets his hero, who then beats the shit out of him for following them. His new dilemma, on top of all his old ones: staying loyal to his team while being hounded by a pesky investigator, pressured to file a lawsuit by his lawyer brother and, most of all, getting called out on-air for the beatdown by Philadelphia Phil, who is later revealed to be a fat Michael Rappaport.

That it looks and feels like The Wrestler (Aronofsky, 2008) is no accident–Big Fan writer/director Robert D. Siegel wrote that film (as well as The Onion Movie and a stint as The Onion’s editor-in-chief). Having Michael Simmonds (Goodbye Solo) as your DP doesn’t hurt. Makes the working-class concrete jungle settings both drab and stunning (even the Manhattan scenes), with many close-up shots of Patton Oswalt out-drooping his already-droopy face. There’s definitely some patronizing of the American Sports Fan (an easy target), but it’s no less interesting to watch through a most un-sports-like filter. After a climax that nearly had me bugging out, it all ends with bittersweetly. With blind optimism in the most fucked up of situations–the most important thing that sports, like the religions they’ve supplanted, can offer.
