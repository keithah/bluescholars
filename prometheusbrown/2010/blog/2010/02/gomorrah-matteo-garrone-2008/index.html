Gomorrah (Matteo Garrone, 2008)

February 4th, 2010 | 4 Comments »

I tried watching Gomorrah (hailed by some as “our generation’s Scarface” – even getting a quick Criterion DVD treatment) late one night and fell asleep not long after all those dudes get murked in the tanning salon. Probably 10 minutes in. The second and most recent time I’ve seen it was off Sabzi’s Macbook screen in a hotel in Raleigh during a snowstorm. Typical traveling indie musicians that we are, The Wire was the subject of many discussions on the trip. So that was what was fresh in my mind as I watched Gomorrah while afflicted with cabin fever and hunger. I remembered why I dozed off the first time. It’s that darn disjointed establishing plot device: seemingly random scenes of the everyday lives of disconnected characters whose connections will be very slowly revealed for the rest of the film i.e. Amores Perros, Traffic, Babel, etc. Then I remembered why and how this template, once fascinating to me, started to annoy me.

All because of The Wire. Because of that show, I started believing that two hours can never do justice to an a big ensemble cast of characters whose individual stories deserve their own development, no matter how well done. Things get rushed, connections are forced midway through. I started favoring some stories (the old textile businessman who hires Chinese sweatshop labor), over others (the two punks who fantasize with Uzis about being Scarface but get their ass kicked frequently). I’d gain and lose interest every 10-15 minutes. For a film with as much silence as violence, the characters never went past being caricatures. Just really well-filmed ones. I know the intent is rooted in realism, an attempt to paint a grand panoptic view of society. What ends up happening though, is that everybody’s surface gets scratched and nobody’s armor gets pierced.
