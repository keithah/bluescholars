Cyrus (Jay & Mark Duplass, 2010)

July 4th, 2010 | 1 Comment »

Cyrus (2010) | dir. & wr. Jay and Mark Duplass | 92 min | USA

Sad sack of shit John (John C. Reilly) meets Molly (MILFGOAT[1] Marisa Tomei). Molly lives with her 21-year old son Cyrus (Jonah Hill). They have an abnormally close bond, which puts John and Molly’s relationship to the test. Instant comedy recipe. In other words, who the hell cares? I would’ve passed on this one if it weren’t for two things. One, Marisa Tomei. Two, that Cyrus (2010) was “written” and directed by Jay and Mark Duplass, the duo behind Baghead (2008) known for their low-budget, improvisational aesthetic (see: Mumblecore-wiki).

Apparently these dudes simply write a rough outline of the film’s story, set up each scene, turn on the cameras, and let the actors improvise most of the dialogue. Which is what ultimately gave Cyrus its charm and a feeling that it wasn’t just another comedy cut from the Apatowian bromance cloth. John C. Reilly and Jonah Hill are funnier here than they’ve ever been. They needed to be in order to offset all that Oedipal creepiness happening. Jonah comes through with several quotables like “Don’t fuck my mom… just kidding.” John C. Reilly is perfect as the pushover divorcee. And Marisa Tomei? Yow.

On the other hand, there are a few hundred unnecessary zoom-ins/outs and lots of transitional scenes with un-synced dialogue. I know those are aesthetic norms in the “improvised” and “indie” realm but it just looks forced with Hollywood actors. Small flaws for an otherwise funny, restrained (except the one time we see John C. Reilly’s asscrack) film.

[1] term invented by @heyphilchang
