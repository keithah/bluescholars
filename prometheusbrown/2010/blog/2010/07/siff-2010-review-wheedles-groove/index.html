SIFF 2010 Review: Wheedle’s Groove (Jennifer Maas, 2010)

July 9th, 2010 | No Comments »

I dig how the OG Mr. Supreme, the undisputed DJ with the deepest crates in the whole city, possibly the whole coast, narrates the jump-off point for Wheedle’s Groove (Jennifer Maas, 2010), a documentary about Seattle’s 1960s-70s funk and soul scene. It rightfully positions this community of artists, lost in the mainstream music industry timelline, as the foremothers and forefathers of Seattle’s present music scene. Particularly, logically: hip-hop. Anyone who’s into record-digging and local music history will appreciate seeing Vitamin D side-by-side with his father, musician Herman Brown, reminiscing on the Central District music scene. Original tracks playing in the background, musicians talk story about a vibrant local black music scene fronted by bands like Cold, Bold and Together and A Black And White Affair.

First-time director Jennifer Maas avoids turning the documentary into a reminiscing-fest by splicing the storytelling with commentary from Seattle musicians of all genres and eras. These musicians, rather than becoming cultural artifacts, are thus seen as still-present, and not just metaphorically. Many of them are still top-notch performers. I had the privilege of performing onstage with some of the Wheedle’s Groove musicians on New Years Eve 2005 (little-known Seattle trivia: we released the first Blue Scholars album on Light In The Attic Records, the same label that put out the Wheedle’s Groove compilation that same year). I remember hearing back then that a documentary was going to made, which indicates how much time and work has been put into this film. Now that it’s done, its safe to say it was worth the wait.
