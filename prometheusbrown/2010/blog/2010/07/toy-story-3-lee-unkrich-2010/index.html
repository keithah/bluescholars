Toy Story 3 (Lee Unkrich, 2010)

July 1st, 2010 | 1 Comment »

As I drove to the theater to see Toy Story 3 (2010), the five-year old film critic in the back seat declared, “I wish all movies were 3D.” I nearly shed a tear. The two of us were on our way to South Seattle’s lone indie neighborhood theater to catch it in glorious 2D, with snacks for both of us, for the price of one IMAX 3D ticket. I reasoned my decision to him, not out of some broke-parent shame for taking the cheaper route, but with a stern fatherly opinionation that, sorry son, 3D sucks. We lightweight argued all the way to our seats.

The 2D/3D debate was deaded quickly with the battle/chase sequence that opens Toy Story 3. Fun stuff. It ends abruptly as Andy, The Owner, now 17, enters the room and starts cleaning up one last time before going off to college. The toys get mistakenly thrown out with the trash but escape and decide to move on, stowaways in a donation box to Sunnyside Day Care. There, they’re welcomed by a pink leader bear named Lotso who assures them they’re in a libertarian utopia where the motto is “no owners, no heartbreak.”

But what appears to be socialism is actually front for a fascist police state. They get duped into staying in the riotous toddler room while all the OG toys live comfortably with the bigger kids. When Buzz speaks out, he gets arrested/resetted and Lotso throws the rest in jail. The rest of the film finds the toys waging guerilla warfare to overthrow the fascist Lotso regime, with lots of inside jokes for parents whose childhood toys make cameos and action for the kids. Same ol stuff you saw in the previous two Toy Stories, but with more urgency and finality. Somewhere in there, especially during the climax scenes in the landfill and incinerator, is a critique of conspicuous consumption similar to the one found in Wall-E (2008).

Ah, yes. A perfectly good animated summertime flick undefiled by an unnecessary third dimension. With a lightweight progressive message characteristic of recent Pixar flicks. At least I thought so. But I had to check in with the kid to verify. Did you like it? I asked. “Yes,” he answered, “Toy Story 3 is my favorite movie ever.” And believe me, he never says things like that unless he means it. Good thing, too ’cause if Toy Story 3 sucked, I might’ve lost the 2D/3D argument. To a five-year old film critic.
