REVIEW: Mountain Thief (Gerry Balasta, 2009)

August 8th, 2010 | 3 Comments »

I caught Mountain Thief (2009), a low-budget film about garbage scavengers in the Philippines, on the same day I saw Inception, a $200 million film about blurring the lines between dreams and reality. My cinematic cognitive dissonance was peaking, and I welcomed the return to concrete.

The film begins with a quote describing the Payatas garbage dump in the Philippines as the largest in the world. So big, you can see it from space. After a wide shot of the mountain’s picturesque filth, it opens with frantic, handheld camera movement that tells us this is a 21st century Philippine slum movie. We see Julio and his blind son, Ingo, arriving to find work. He comes upon a spot called Little Hope, meets a girl, and encounters a religious zealot who is convinced that Julio, who is a Muslim, is a devil.

Key moments repeat from different perspectives as we jump from one character to another. There’s a definite City Of God (Fernando Meirelles, 2002) influence here. Like that film, actual townsfolk were recruited to play versions of themselves. Sometimes, a director finds a way to make us overlook flat acting with long takes or sharp editing. Unfortunately the neorealist magic doesn’t work here except for the kid who plays Ingo, who’s blind in real life.

The sequence leading to the open-ended final shot (my second of the day), is the film’s best scene. That whole disjointed plot thing, previously distracting, set up an intense confrontation that earned its unresolved ending. But when the lights came on, my earlier conflict was suddenly replaced by another. On one hand, the film is a social document and is helping draw attention to some real shit. On the other hand, the filmmaker’s sympathetic intentions lacked a clear purpose beyond the making of the film itself.

Director Gerry Balasta was in attendance for the film’s New York Asian American Film Festival screening. So I asked him what he thinks should be done to address the conditions depicted in his film. Something, anything. He replied that he didn’t know. Sadly, that right there might have been the missing ingredient that could’ve helped Mountain Thief become than just another third world slum movie.
