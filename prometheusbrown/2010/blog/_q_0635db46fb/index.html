Burn Hollywood Burn

Posted on March 5, 2008
Filed Under Culture |

Why does it seem like the “worst movie I’ve ever seen in my life” is being released like once a month now? Used to happen once or twice a year. And even then, a movie was so bad that it was clowningly entertaining. It’s official: TV dramas and comedies are currently superior to major studio films. TV used to be the more ADD-friendly shit, and films were more immersive and sophisticated. Seems like it’s the other way around these days.

Which is wack, because a good flick provides a perfect 90-120 minute distraction and an hour of discussion to ease the road weariness during tour. IMO, I’ve delivered some of my best performances after watching a good movie earlier in the day.

I’m a new movie hater. It’s doo-doo baby! (c) Chappelle. Thursday is the new Friday, 30 is the new 20 and TV is the new theater. Quality-wise the shit looks and sounds better than ever, but depth and relevance are in short supply. Not to mention, fewer and fewer movies are told from a working person’s perspective. Everyone is either in another century or in some generic everycity with no poor people. Looks like the best movies ever made will remain so for a very long time.

Jumper

We caught this flick on a six-screen multiplex near Cambridge, MA. Possibly the worst theater we’ve ever been to. If not, then definitely in the bottom three. Our ticket taker was a non-Asian girl named Asia who asked another girl if she cleaned the theater. She said yes, but we walked in and found out that she lied.

Maybe it’s good we saw some garbage like Jumper instead of a dope flick that would’ve been forever tainted by the memory of the shithole we watched it in. Now, I was actually halfway entertained throughout the movie. But only because it reminded me a little of a Heroes episode (which I can dig). Jumper takes a bite out of Nightcrawler’s (from X-men) teleporting ability and blows it to cornball proportions with fight sequences that start in London and end on at the Great Pyramids in Egypt.

More thumbs down on wasting Samuel L. Jackson’s always-welcomed screen presence on a character whose main rival is… young Anakin Skywalker? GTFO!

Vantage Point

Arab terrorists don’t even have a “cause” anymore - just a photo of a family member, an ambiguous hatred of anything American and some dynamite strapped to an extra-medium vest. It’s not important to know why they do it, just that they hate you and it’s only a matter of time.

The film is basically a series of 10-15 minute short films revolving around different characters all witnessing the same event: the terrorist assassination of the President of the United States. The first two “vantage points” were promising: Sigourney Weaver and a live television crew broadcasting the event, and Dennis Quaid as the super secret service agent Jack Bauer knock off. The rest of the stories devolved into a sort of bastard child between “24″ and “Lost.” Twists were predictable, and by the end, the terrorist scare was averted (and all of ‘em dead) and justice prevailed.

Good thing the dudebro at the counter of the Isis Theater was kind enough to charge us normal people prices instead of the inflated Aspen price, and serve me some beers even though he wasn’t allowed to.

There Will Be Blood

Echoing Bao’s sentiments: no Asian, Black and Mexican people in California, circa 1900-1920? Really?

If you can ignore the white sleight of hand (which you can’t if you look like me), you’ll actually find an intriguing story about a self-made capitalist and the changes he goes through from desperation to ruthlessness to (you guessed it) downfall!

At the very least, it made me want to check out the novel “Oil!” by Upton Sinclair, which the movie is based on. So that I can see how watered down they must have made the story, considering Sinclair’s reputation for muckraking on big business and unabashedly socialist politics. I actually liked most of this flick. But it was mid-afternoon and real nice in Denver (which I haven’t seen much of on this trip) and at around the 2:15 mark, I decided leave to go buy some socks and draws before we headed back into the mountains. By the time I came back, the movie was done, and I had apparently missed the part where the blood promised in the title finally arrived.

—–

1/3 aint bad, considering it’s early in the year and all that’s around are last year’s Oscar nominees and the usual round of late-winter garbage. Hopefully later on, I’ll feel the way Mos Def looks on the promo poster for Be Kind Rewind.
