Zoo (Robinson Devor, 2007)

Posted on July 12, 2008
Filed Under Culture, Films |

Zoo (2007)
dir. Robinson Devor
74min

A couple years ago, a man in Enumclaw died from injuries sustained from being fucked by a horse. The local and national mass-media, always short on relevant news and big on shock-value, milked the story dry. When I heard that a movie was going to be made about it, I thought: great, more tasteless bullshit.

But my curiosity was sparked when I heard that the film - titled Zoo, a reference to zoophilia - was in the hands of director/writer Robinson Devor and writer Charles Mudede. The duo’s Police Beat (2005) treated freaks and weirdos humanistically, but never losing their strangeness.

Like Police Beat, Zoo is quiet, slow and paints the Northwest as an enchanted, dark place with secrets in them thar hills. Secrets like a whole crew of people who love animals, and sometimes let the animals make love to them. 

As a statement film that challenges the media’s treatment of the “accident” as a spectacle, Zoo works as a hybrid drama/documentary (as opposed to a docudrama, which includes “talking heads”). Voices narrate a beautifully shot dramatized story with both actors and persons who were involved in the events re-enacting real-life events. Horsefucking is explored rather than exploited, almost too seriously and sympathetically. But if that is Zoo’s intent, it does it well. I almost feel guilty even joking about it anymore. Almost.
