Hellboy II: The Golden Army (Guillermo Del Toro, 2008)

Posted on July 13, 2008
Filed Under Films |

Hellboy II The Golden Army (2008)
dir. Guillermo Del Toro
120 min

Guillermo Del Toro is a beast when it comes to beasts. Hellboy (2004) was good (not great) and the fantasyland of Pan’s Labyrinth (2006) was breathtaking. While not as “deep” as Pan’s Labyrinth, Hellboy II: The Golden Army shits on all his previous work. Now, we can talk all day about the film’s stunning visuals and non-stop action (or, even worse, go the nerdboy route and compare it to the comic), but I want to pose a political question: is Hellboy a sell-out?

As with nearly all comic-book adaptations, a typical plot: a long time ago, elves and other creatures-of-the-night led their indestructible Golden Army in a war against the humans. They kicked our ass so much that they started using us for fertilizer. But the elf king felt so guilty that he brokered a truce. We got to keep the cities, and them - the forest and everything else.

But as we started building shopping malls, chopping down forests and destroying the Earth, the elven Prince Nuada, the OG human hater, couldn’t take it anymore. Flash forward to now, he returns from exile to awaken the Golden Army in order to wage war against us pillaging humans (second question: is the Prince justified?).

But lovable cigar-smoking demon spawn Hellboy (Ron Perlman), who descends from this creature-of-the-dark community, is on our side even though we hate on him and call him ugly when he’s out strolling the city protecting us. And though Prince Nuada tries to lure him (”you’re one of us!”), he isn’t the least bit conflicted about his stance, mostly because he was raised by a good adopted human father (John Hurt) and likes to do human shit like drink beer and have sex with Selma Blair (which we don’t get to see, unfortunately).

Through all the fight scenes (all real filthy), awesome-looking monsters (both massive and miniscule) and well-timed humor, the film’s train stays firmly on the formula track. The “terrorists” are defeated, their cause goes to hell (literally and figuratively), and we live to see another day and drink another Coke.

Does that make Hellboy a sell-out? Well, yeah. Is Prince Nuada justified? Only if there’s little to no other option, and more importantly, if enough creature-people (and not just him) are down for it, and it sure seems like it. Del Toro, just as in Pan’s Labryinth, seems to say that true love justifies anything, even the eventual destruction of the planet. That in a world of endless war, fantasies and love are all we really have and, more importantly, the biggest thing that distinguishes us from them. Because of that, we should be given a chance, irrational beings that we are.

If I were living in the Hellboy world, where actual elf-terrorists were trying to kill us and Satan’s son was our only hope, I would agree with all that. But since I don’t, I couldn’t disagree more.
