The Dalai Lama in Seattle

Posted on April 12, 2008
Filed Under Culture, Politics |

 

The Dalai Lama is in Seattle this weekend preaching compassion. I dig compassion - we all should. However, I can’t really get down with how his message is utilized by media and politicians - to minimize class struggle by centering the world’s ills around individual anger and greed rather than institutional reality. It’s a shame that many surface-level progressives have blindly adopted the Free Tibet campaign without digging deeper and seeing how and why this “movement” came to be. Professor Michael Parenti has written extensively on the subject, and whatever your opinion on the matter, it’d do you some good to get hip.

The well-appropriated and celebrity-cosigned Free Tibet campaign has found a new face this year with a rising tide of anti-China sentiment that focuses on its questionable human rights record. Especially with the 2008 Olympics kicking off. Mainstream media has pounced on this story while our own government continues economic and military foreign policies far worse than any other government’s.

While China no doubt has blood on its hands, it reeks of hypocrisy for any American to spend more time pointing a finger at another country’s human rights record while our own citizens are losing jobs, dying in unjust wars, facing police brutality, working with inadequate health care and countless other problems that hit closer to home. And many have framed this complex issue as an absolute, either-or thing - that you’re either in support of China’s human rights record, or you’re against it.

If you wanna go that route, do you - but if you’re gonna boycott the Olympics, you better also be boycotting any sporting event that plays the Star Spangled Banner. That is, unless you’re not so much in favor of human rights as you are in favor of believing that only other countries are guilty of violating them. If there’s anything His Holiness and I agree upon, it’s that we should look in the mirror first before pointing the finger elsewhere.
