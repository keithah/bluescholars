MUSIC VIDEO: Keegan DeWitt “Hearts Beat Loud” (dir. Matt Jay)

July 25th, 2010

Archived embedded media is not available for offline playback.

Longtime readers of this blog know that I’ve been digging the young homie Matt Jay’s film work for a minute now. “Hears Beat Loud” is a simple video: a group of friends on a nighttime galavant through NYC featuring skateboards, subways, street food vendors, flashlights, the Ace Hotel, photo booths, city lights reflecting off water, all culminating in a beautiful sunrise at the Brooklyn Bridge. In its simplicity are the tiniest moments that really matter which often get overlooked by less attentive filmmakers: a smile from afar, a head resting on a shoulder, the slight clutch of hands. It’s a visual poem that matches the song’s frenetic pace. Frequent foreground obstructions evoke a feeling of being there and not there. Somehow, the viewer is both a voyeur and a participant.

MUSIC: Native Guns “Handcuffs”

July 7th, 2010

Today, the jury in the Johannes Mehserle trial begins deliberations over whether or not he is guilty in the shooting death of Oscar Grant. Everywhere, particularly in Oakland and Los Angeles, people are on edge wondering if justice will be upheld. While we wait for the verdict, my comrades Kiwi and Bambu have reunited to release the first new Native Guns track in four years, “Handcuffs.” In it, they speak on witnessing the 1992 L.A. uprising and urge everyone to take it to the streets, but to channel their anger into organized efforts rather than spontaneous destruction. MUSIC VIDEOS: J. Cole “Who Dat” (& A Brief History of the One-Shot Rap Video)

June 30th, 2010

Archived embedded media is not available for offline playback.

When done right, the one-shot music video is the purest and highest expression of the form (when done wrong, nothing screams “amateur” louder). No artificial effects, no ADD-inducing editing, no unnecessary bullshit. It’s such a fitting video style for a genre of music as straightforward as rap is. A juxtaposition of visual continuity with lyrical randomness. You’d think there’d be hundreds of one-shot rap videos like there are for other genres. Maybe, despite every rappers fantasy (including mine) to do a music video in one take, it’s actually rare to find a director who can pull it off. J. Cole & director BBGun (who also directed Asher Roth’s one-shot video “Muddy Swim Trunks”) do just that, following this cinema-rap tradition with “Who Dat.” Here’s a few of my favorite one-take hip-hop videos. Power Struggle – Remittances Album Release Party

June 2nd, 2010

This right here is my kasama Nomi. He was part of Minneapolis hip-hop group Oddjobs, who toured all over the country in the early 2000s. Before Blue Scholars ever played a show outside the Northwest, before him or I started organizing with Bayan-USA, we’d link up when they rolled through Seattle and he’d school me on the raps and life on the road. Aside from the homies The Pacifics and Native Guns, he was the only other Filipino emcee out there working the independent circuit and putting out albums on a national scale. We wrote a song back in ’04 that never came out, but part of the hook that he wrote I ended up using for the hook to “Morning of America.” (more…)
