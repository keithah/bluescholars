RIP Corazon Aquino (1933-2009)

Posted on August 4, 2009
Filed Under Obituaries |

Since the news hit Saturday, Filipinos worldwide are mourning former Philippine presdient Cory Aquino’s passing. I’ve been to the Philippines twice, as a child–once before 1986 and then again in that year just months after People Power uprising that ousted Marcos. Most things from those trips are blurry, vague memories, except for one thing: I remember seeing yellow everywhere, and people were alot happier the second trip than the first. To this day, lemons remind me of Cory Aquino.

In part out of respect for an elder’s passing, another part an expression of gratefulness for her part in ousting the dictator, most obituaries read like instant hagiography (Time magazine: People Power’s Saint). Philippine film critic Noel Vera’s eloquent eulogy celebrates Cory Aquino’s significance without shying away from her political shortcomings, including her adverse impact on local cinema. Censorship, rather than being relaxed from Marcos’ era, continued and, in some ways, got worse. Lino Brocka’s film, Orapronobis, which exposed the Aquino government’s responsibility for paramilitary death squads, was banned.

She was a boss. She was the boss. Even withstanding six coup attempts. Just recently, she joined the masses of people calling for president Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo to step down amidst corruption scandals and human rights violations. The story of her coming into power was dramatized in the incredibly contrived English-language action-thriller HBO mini-series Edsa: A Dangerous Life. Starring Gary Busey, I shit you not (and director/actor Laurice Guillen as Cory). Here’s to hoping that one day, a more truthful story of Cory Aquino makes it to the screen. One that includes both her triumphs and shortcomings with no taint of sainthood and, most importantly, a fair look at the people who built movement against Marcos and created the conditions for her historic achievement.
