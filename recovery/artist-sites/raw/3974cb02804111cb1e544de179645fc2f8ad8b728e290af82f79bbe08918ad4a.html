It’s Like That and Uh (Hieroglyphics, Yeah)

Posted on February 26, 2008
Filed Under Tour |

Day 4 of a 7 day trip through the Rocky Mountain region with the mighty Hieroglyphics crew. Even though the air is thin and cold and the money is shorter than we hoped, I’m feeling every minute of this. Every show has sold-out. I catch myself in fan-mode at least once a night on this trip. Especially when “93 Til Infinity” or “You Never Know” drops. But beyond being influenced by their music and grind, I’ve been jogging down memory lane on pivotal moments in my upbringing through this independent hip-hop shit, and everywhere I turn, Hiero has been omnipresent. Allow me to talk story for a minute.

1. December 1993 - My First CD purchase……was Del The Funky Homosapien’s No Need For Alarm I got a CD player for Christmas and finally joined the ranks of the Compact Disc purchasing population. For my first digital purchase, I decided to take a chance on this cat I’ve never heard of except for “Mr. Dobalina” and some rumor that he was Ice Cube’s cousin (true, BTW). Was my favorite album that year… until pops confiscated it, disapproving of the photo of Del sparking trees on the back cover.

2. Hieroglyphics @ RCKCNDY (RIP). March 1999.One of the first hip-hop shows I ever attended in Seattle, and still ranks near the top on my short list of most memorable shows. Part of it is nostalgia: the ambient grime of a legendary venue that was to close for good later that year, hearing all the Hiero classics I came up on performed live for the first time. (Sidenote: it was also my first time seeing Tilson, now with The Saturday Knights, and Khingz/Khalil & Neezie Pleaze, then with Maroon Colony, rock the mic). But this was a truly legendary show - it was Hieroglyphics’ second tour back in the Northwest, blazing that independent do-it-yourself tour trail that we’d later find ourselves marching through… with them, no less.

3. Del the Funkee Homosapien @ The Showbox. October 1999RCKCNDY had just shut down and for the time being, every hip-hop show in Seattle was held at 21+ venues. Bad look for a hungry 19-year old heads such as myself and the dude Marc whom I met in the kitchen of the Showbox as we both attempted to sneak in past security. I got in, he didn’t. I eventually sneaked backstage and rolled a fire-filled Swisher for the dude whose second album was my first CD purchase. Marc, on the other hand, was so infuriated about getting kicked out that he wrote an article the next day for the school paper, calling for folks to organize and do something about the lack of all-ages venues. SHOW was formed, through which I met Sabzi, and 9 years later here we are, rocking sold out shows with the crew who put down the blueprint for independent hip-hop worldwide.
