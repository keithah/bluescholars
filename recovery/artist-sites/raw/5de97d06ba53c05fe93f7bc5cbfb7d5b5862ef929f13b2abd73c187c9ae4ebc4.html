Notorious (George Tillman, Jr., 2009)

Posted on March 9, 2009
Filed Under Film Reviews |

Notorious (2009) | dir. George Tillman, Jr. | wr. Cheo Hodari Coker & Reggie Rock Bythweood | 123 min | USA

“I need some Pepsi. And more weed.”

Christopher Wallace aka Biggie Small’s life (and death), on and off wax, has been thoroughly documented and since become part of hip-hop and American pop culture mythology–a persona and legacy perhaps only matched by 2Pac’s. So a Biggie biopic’s worth rests on two things: shedding light on the as-yet unilluminated parts of his life. And, two, providing some context behind, and capturing the energy of, his music.

It gets the latter right. The best parts of Notorious (2009) come and go with the beats and rhymes. On sidewalks, in smoke-filled home studios, onstage–these scenes, featuring Biggie’s actual music matched to newcomer Jamal Woolard’s admirable lip-sync, hint at the man’s larger-than-life presence. However, when the music turned off, Notorious often veered into cable-TV mini-series territory. The parts of the story it chose to tell played out like a bunch of shit we’ve already either seen or heard: his first meeting with Puffy, meeting and fucking Lil’ Kim, the short friendship and long rivalry with 2Pac, meeting Faith, album release parties, crowds yellling “we love you Big Poppa,” etc. Two curious scenes edged on a revelation, then got cut short: a physical confrontation between Big and Kim in the studio and the frantic night 2Pac was shot in NYC en route to meeting Biggie. The dialogue, though often stilted (”You’re gonna be a great father.” “No doubt, no doubt.”), yields to believable drama. The emotion almost creeps up on you in the scenes between a young Biggie and his moms (Voletta Wallace, played by Angela Bassett). Almost–until a forced sappy score fucks it all up. At one point, Puffy yells out “shit, all this soap-opera drama!.” And I thought irony was out.

I ain’t mad, though. I wasn’t expecting a groundbreaking cinematic achievement, but Notorious could’ve done more to challenge or reinterpret myths about Big than prop up the ones we already know about. 12 years after the man was gunned down in LA on this very date, March 9, his murder (as well as Pac’s) still remains unsolved. I guess Notorious, if anything, helps us not forget that. But if you really want to see Big’s best biopic, sit back, light a blunt, close your eyes and play his 1994 debut album Ready to Die from beginning to end.
