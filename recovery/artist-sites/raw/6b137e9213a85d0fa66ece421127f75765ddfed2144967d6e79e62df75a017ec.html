The Great Debaters (Denzel Washington, 2007) and Resolved (Greg Whiteley, 2008)

Posted on July 7, 2008
Filed Under Film Reviews |

The Great Debaters (2007)
dir. Denzel Washington
114 min

Resolved (2008)
dir. Greg Whitely
90 min

Suddenly, debate has become a popular topic for film, perhaps not coincidentally in the thick of a historical presidential election year. I recently caught two features - a Hollywood drama and an HBO documentary - that attempt to paint competitive debate as an empowering, transformative and even potentially revolutionary space.



Directed by (!) and starring Denzel Washington, The Great Debaters follows a familiar formula: a rag-tag bunch of (black) kids learn discipline from an influential mentor/coach/father-figure and overcome great odds (white people) to become champions. Set in the South during the Great Depression, the racial tension is palpable from the first scene.

On the surface, it’s pretty much Coach Carter or Glory Road with arguments instead of jumpshots. What distinguishes The Great Debaters from these feel-good pieces of shit is that it dares highlight Melvin B. Tolson’s (Washington) socialist politics - he’s a teacher/debate coach by day and a labor union organizer (and possible CP-USA member?) by night. He teaches his students words like “revolution,” recites black liberation poetry and sees racism as a byproduct of capitalism.  

In the final scene, a historical debate between Wiley College and Harvard, James Farmer Jr. eloquently defends both nonviolence and the right to resist through armed struggle, saying: “I have a right, a duty to resist, with violence or with civil disobedience. You should pray I choose the latter.” As one of the few big-studio films that acknowledges class contradictions, The Great Debaters is the well-dressed closet revolutionary at a bourgeois cocktail party. 

While The Great Debaters ends with smiles and celebration (what else did you expect?), Resolved is more interested in presenting the contradictions (racial, cultural, economic) of present-day high school debate competitions. Director Greg Whiteley contrasts two teams consisting of two white students from an affluent Texas suburb and two working-class black students from Long Beach. 

The differences between the two teams couldn’t be any more distinct - their only commonality is their penchant for winning. The white kids have mastered the art of debate as filibuster: speed-speaking nearly-incoherent arguments and cramming information in an attempt to overwhelm opposition with more hubris and sophistry than principle. The black kids refuse to debate this way. Putting Paolo Freire’s theories of education into action, they debate topics while debating the nature of debate itself as inherently flawed as exclusive and racist - and laying philosophical grounds for why it is so. Fucking brilliant. 

While most people’s view of debates resembles the eloquent, genteel style shown in The Great Debaters or boring-ass politician debates on TV, these kids go in. Emotion, while always kept in check by the argument, is put on blast and confrontations are frequent. Catching the mostly white and nerdy competition off guard, we see classic (I mean, classsssic) footage of privileged white kids backpedaling from the Long Beach team’s real talk. Without spoiling anything, I’ll just say that there is no happy ending in Resolved like the one we get in The Great Debaters. 

Big-up to Denzel Washington and Greg Whiteley for giving competitive debate the shine it deserves, especially when class contradictions come into play and all sophistry goes out the window. Had I seen films like these in high school, I might have tried it myself. Hopefully, more young cats from underrepresented and underpriviliged communities (like my young dude Dakota Camacho!) will be inspired to take this route and show these crackers what’s up.
