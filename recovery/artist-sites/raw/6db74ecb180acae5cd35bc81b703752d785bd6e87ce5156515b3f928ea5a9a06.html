Sounds Of A New Hope: Live Video Remix (Los Angeles, 8/27)

August 11th, 2010 | No Comments »

When the homie Kiwi came back from the Philippines in summer of  2007, he showed me a grip of amazing video footage of him rapping with urban poor youth, freestyling and recording in the slum, meeting up with Filipino hip-hop legends Francis M (RIP) and Gloc-9, and several jarring testimonies of barrio life from the youth themselves. It was confirmation of what I’ve been hearing about the PI for a minute – that more and more city kids are expressing themselves through hip-hop music. It wasn’t always that way, and there’s still a long way to go.

Even more salient to the story is how this cultural phenomenon intersects with the National Democratic movement of the Philippines: Kiwi’s music came out of his experience organizing in the movement while most of the kids he linked up with are either members of or are down with organizations like Anakbayan, which originated in the Philippines but also now has chapters in several U.S. cities. The video came via Anakbayan L.A. member/filmmaker/DJ Eric Tandoc (DJ E.T.), who cut together the PI shots with archival and interview footage with Kiwi, to create the documentary Sounds of a New Hope. The DVD dropped late last year in partnership with Sine Patriyotiko (SIPAT) as a fundraiser for Typhoon Ondoy (and is available here).

For all in the Los Angeles area, you can catch a FREE live “remix” screening of Sounds of a New Hope on August 27 at the Japanese American Cultural and Community Center plaza. In addition to catching a quality documentary, it’d be interesting to see how a filmmaker/DJ merges both disciplines with technology to explore new ways of presenting cinema, manipulating the video at will while making transitions between live performances. Really wish I could be there.
