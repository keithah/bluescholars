Jon Augustavo: A Re-introduction

August 20th, 2010 | 2 Comments »

Like I’ve been saying all year, there’s a lil’ rap music video renaissance going on in Seattle these days. In just the first half of 2010 alone, Jon Augustavo (aka Jon Jon, aka @jonjonaye) has dropped 2-3 music videos a month, each one showing growth from the last. He’s done joints for J. Pinder, Sol, Wizdom, Eighty4 Fly, and Roderic to name a few – all finding the rapper in their most natural element. It’s lifestyle realism, in a similar vein as other Seattle hip-hop music video directors (Zia Mohajerjasbi, Stephan Gray), but with slightly more polish and a vibe that echoes the classic YO! MTV RAPS era of hip-hop videos. Jon Jon is also the fastest interview responder ever:

How’d you get started in filmmaking?
I’m a simple guy, no legendary story of me watching a film and it grabbing my soul or me meeting Stanley Kubrick. My dad bought a camera and an iMac when I was 13 years old. I picked the thing up and started making sports highlight tapes and these crazy bush jumping tapes of me and my friends. And here I stand.

There’s a definite cinematic approach to your vids, one that focuses on capturing the musicians in their most natural element rather than something staged. What filmmakers have influenced your work?
When I approach these videos I don’t know if I really look at other filmmakers for a lot of inspiration, but I definitely have my favorites who I’m sure I mimic unconsciously. Filmmakers like Gus Van Sant (Finding Forrester), Tony Scott (Man on Fire), Matthew Vaughn (Layer Cake) are my favorites — I really appreciate all of there styles and having no real formal training, I think I’m more into their styles because of my fine art background. Capturing musicians in their comfort zones is key in my eye, whether that be them rapping in front of a corner store; where their homey recently died or just drinking tea. I want people to think they are who they present themselves to be.

What equipment do you work with and how do you prepare for the videos? How much of it is your vision, and how much of it is the musician’s?
I’ve worked with just about every style of camera available. From traditional film cameras to little handy cameras. At the moment though, I am on a DSLR kick and am using the Canon 7D when I shoot myself and my right hand man Phil Conover uses the Canon 5D when he is the Director of Photography. For almost every video I’ve done, the basic idea of the video is most definitely the musician’s vision, which I am okay with because I understand the blood sweat and tears they put into their own music and don’t want to create something they don’t believe in or feel is a facade. However, once I’m given the track and the idea, I’m given the ball and I run with it.

List 3 music videos (from any genre, any era) that you dig.
in no particular order:
Wax – California
30 Seconds to Mars – Kings and Queens
Kanye West – Diamonds From Sierra Leone

What music, besides that of the musicians you work with, are in your current rotation?
Drake, Theophilus London, Oasis, The Killers, Washed Out, MGMT, Donnis, Tinchy Stryder, Bashy, Corneille, Dave Coresh, Example, 30 Seconds to Mars, Blakroc, Kid Cudi, Far East Movement, Justin Nozuka, Johnny Polygon, Aloe Blacc, Deadmau5 … I could go on forever. I pretty much listen to music at any moment possible.

Are there any musicians out there you’d like to work with?
Freddie Gibbs, Mr. Hudson, MGMT, Bat For Lashes and Bell Biv Devoe.

What current and upcoming projects are you working on?
Working on The Physics “I Just Wanna Beat” video, just shot Lace Cadence’s video for “Moonlight” and am also working on J.Pinder’s first single off his upcoming album. Oh yeah and Scribes “Roll My Way.”
