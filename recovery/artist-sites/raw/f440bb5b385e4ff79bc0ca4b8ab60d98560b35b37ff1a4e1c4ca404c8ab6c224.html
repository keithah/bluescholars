110 YEARS STRONG

Posted on February 4, 2009
Filed Under Video |

February 4, 1899. The first shots fired in the Philippine-American War. It would be the first US imperialist invasion and occupation fought with two new weapons. One was the Colt 45. The other, film. The Library of Congress has a small archive of propaganda reels which were shown to the American public, who were just seeing moving pictures for the first time.

The American Mutuscope and Biograph Company sent their spanking new cameras to the islands to film these three reels: 25th Infantry, Aguinaldo’s Navy, and An Historic Feat. Notice the smug hubris of titling a clip of some wooden boats on a river “Aguinaldo’s Navy.” 

Folks like Thomas Edison and them couldn’t come up with real footage, so they staged their own such as this clip: Filipinos Retreat From the Trenches (played by Black actors).

Cameras in the hands of our colonizers, we may have been the first casualties of film.

110 years later, that once-exclusive weapon is being used to resist these sort of things. One of the biggest historical tragedies of the Philippine-American War isn’t just the casualties or the political outcomes, but the fact that it’s largely unacknowledged, a non-event. The fact that these films exist speaks the exact opposite.
