Blue Scholars Impress at Academix 2011
By biconews On 2 Apr, 2011 At 09:01 PM | Categorized As Arts, Bryn Mawr | With 0 Comments

Ani Chen
Staff Writer 

Bi-College concertgoers expecting a conventional hip hop concert might have been surprised at the Blue Scholars concert that was held in Founders Hall on April first. Seattle-based Blue Scholars brought down the house with their unconventional lyrics and infectious beats late into the night. Their musical vision is clear with lyrics like “Sunsets in the south land/Right next to the mountains/And a light hits the Puget Sound/Bright for a minute then its time for the clouds.”

Formed at the University of Washington in 2002, Blue Scholars know how to market to college students. George Quibuyen and Saba Mohajerjasbi, better known as Geologic and Sabzi, are rising talents in the genre of 21st century rap. Their music addresses issues from immigration law to those more Seattle-centric. A few silences in “The Ave” were filled in by the Bi-Co’s proud Pacific Northwest community.

While Blue Scholars are certainly better known on the West Coast, they quickly established a rapport with their audience. Geologic thanked Haverford coordinators for the warm welcome of Rice Krispies Treats before beginning their set, which spanned an eclectic corpus.

Haverford College did well in remaining loyal to their original vision for Academix. The crowd was certainly not disappointed with this year’s event, which brought Blue Scholars to the Quaker stage. The symposium held its inaugural event in 2008, when it dared to ask, “What’s the connection between social justice and hip hop?” Earlier in the evening, it took this question to a panel of scholars: Haverford’s Professor Jesse Shipley, UPenn’s John Jackson and Haverford alum Wilifredo Gomez. These scholars were soon followed by Blue Scholars, Philadelphia’s DJ Jazzy Jeff and M1.

As “dudes who nourish their bodies with Vietnamese coffee, Ethiopian injera and steaming bowls of pho,” Blue Scholars blends social justice issues with their true-life experiences. “Here’s to the hope that the beats that I’m rockin’/Is reachin’ your coffee,” rapped Geologic. While Blue Scholars aim to provide a fantastic concert experience, their secondary goal is to provide thought provoking lyrics. Indeed, their words stick to the insides of ears. DJ Sabzi and Geologic take care to remind their audience not to forget about homelessness and drug addiction. By incorporating social justice with a corresponding polyphony, they demonstrate hip hop’s real potential to effect change in the wider world.
