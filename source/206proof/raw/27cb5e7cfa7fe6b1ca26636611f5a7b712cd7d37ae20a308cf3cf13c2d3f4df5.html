    from Seattle Times in '93 after the trial after the shooting after the concert. damn, 50 shots?!

    Man Acquitted Of Shooting At Detectives

    SEATTLE

    A King County Superior Court jury has acquitted a Bremerton man accused of shooting at two Seattle Police detectives during a melee outside the Paramount Theatre last December.

    After hearing nine days of testimony, the jury took only 90 minutes to find Kevin D. Jackson not guilty.

    The detectives testified that Jackson was leaning outside a slowly moving car shooting at them. The officers returned fire, hitting the 16-year-old driver in Jackson's vehicle.

    Authorities estimate roughly 50 bullets were fired from various locations outside the auditorium at 9th Avenue and Pine Street shortly after the rap concert featuring Ice Cube ended.

    Jackson, 21, said he fired into the air while ducking below the level of the car window.

    He said he was trying to protect a friend who was being beat up by a group of concert-goers.

    Defense attorney Antonio Salazar said Jackson could not have been leaning out of the car, or else officers who fired eight times from close range would have hit himThe Hustla

    Lench Mob was there. I'm right:



    Three Shot, Others Hurt In Seattle Theater Melee
    By Margaret Miller

    A fight that broke out during a rap concert last night spilled into the streets of downtown Seattle where it escalated in a shower of gunfire and screams.

    At least three people were shot outside the Paramount Theatre about 10 p.m., and several more were injured by bottle and knife-wielding men some witnesses identified as gang members.

    Later, as police led away at least two suspects, onlookers standing in a street strewn with broken glass and gun casings said they heard as many as 30 shots.

    Sixteen-year-old Demarcus Lautigue of Seattle witnessed the melee from its starting point near the Paramount stage. Trouble began, he said, as soon as the house lights dimmed when headliner Ice Cube took the stage after a performance by Da Lench Mob.

    "A bunch of them started throwing gang (hand) signs and then one guy got punched in the mouth," said Lautigue, explaining that the tension simmered and then boiled over after the concert. "It just spilled straight into the street."

    When the street fight broke out, Richard Monaco, 44, was a block away, watching from the Cloud Room atop the Camlin Hotel. Monaco said he heard shouts and at least four shots, then watched as a man was set upon and beaten by several men. Then, he said, the real violence began.

    "A whole bunch of guys just piled in two Lincoln town cars parked back to back here in front of the hotel," said Monaco, of Kent. "Then they fanned out and I saw guns pulled up to all the

    windows - front and back - and they just started shooting at anything."

    Monaco said the street was packed with concert goers. When the shots began, people dove for cover between parked cars until the gunmen raced up 9th Ave. toward the freeway.

    Another witness, Darren Cromeenes, 21, said he was standing opposite the Paramount when the cars raced by, strafing the sidewalk with gunfire. Pointing to a large smear of blood on the sidewalk, he said he was standing next to a man who was shot in the stomach.

    "He was right here and I saw him go down," said Cromeenes, who had not attended the concert. "Then another shot went past us and hit this window. It could have been me."

    At Harborview Medical Center, three shooting victims were still being evaluated late last night. All three were reportedly in satisfactory condition. Hospital officials would not disclose names or descriptions of injuries until evaluations were completed.

    check out this one, the next big concert after cube. digable.

    Positive Hip-Hop? It's Digable Planets
    By Alison Pember

    Six years ago, Ishmael Butler was playing basketball at Seattle's Garfield High School. Today he's on the road with his hip-hop group Digable Planets, touring nationally to support the gold album, "Reachin' (A New Refutation of Time and Space)."

    Butler, who uses the name Butterfly these days, has been on tour for a little more than three weeks, selling out 800- to 1,500-seat venues every night. Digable Planets plays at the Oz on Tuesday.

    On stage, the group, which includes co-rappers Mary Ann "Ladybug" Vieira and Craig "Doodlebug" Irving, performs all the material from their record with help from an upright bass player, drummer, alto saxophonist, trumpeter and DJ. The show also features different versions of some of the album's tracks, along with what Butler called "a couple of surprises."

    Digable Planets presents a sharp contrast to Ice Cube, the last major hip-hop performer to visit Seattle. Ice Cube's show ended in a melee outside the Paramount Theatre last December.

    Hip-hop has been characterized by the mass media as aggressive and hard-edged, as artists like Ice Cube, Public Enemy and Ice-T thrust the anger of the streets onto mainstream America. But hip-hop has a variety of styles, tones and voices, as does any musical genre.

    Digable Planets presents a refreshingly positive aspect of African-American culture, yet maintains a critical eye.

    "First and foremost we're rap, hip-hop music, which pulls lyrically and musically from all areas of African-American expression - from jazz to funk to the language that goes into the lyrics. It's like a reflection of not only history, but a reflection of contemporary stuff, both politically and socially," Butler said.

    Butler grew up in Seattle's Central District and majored in civil engineering at the University of Massachusetts. He said he channels his life experiences through his music to give audiences a novel message.

    "A human invented the concept of time," explained Butler. "Therefore, each human can reinvent for themselves. So basically you're free of any type of constrictions. You operate in your own time. You do things that you want to do, when you want to do them. Life is yours to make whatever you want to happen, as long as you realize your boundaries."

    Groove Garden, a hip-hop dance-oriented duo from New York, consisting of DJ Atsushi and vocalist Ambersunshower, opens Tuesday's show.
