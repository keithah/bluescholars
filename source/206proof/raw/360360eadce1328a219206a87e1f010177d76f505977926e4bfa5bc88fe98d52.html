    I <3 Lisa Dank, for chrissakes she bought me a hot dog once, how can I not? Here are my conflicted thoughts on the subject, in no particular order:

    1) Whether its art or not, an artist should be mindful of what they are doing, or at the very least the consequences of their actions. If you know me, you know that my dream is to one day swing a microphone with such reckless abandon that it knocks my hypeman out. I don't do it though, because even the beer-smelling open mic mics I get to use aren't mine...and I have respect for the venue and their equipment. EVEN the venues that have "screwed me over." At the end of the day, I attempt to be as professional as I can possibly be because my business is a direct reflection of me. If I don't take it seriously, I am not taking myself seriously.

    2) Hinted at in #1, but if I broke something as a result of wilin' out on stage, I would expect to pay for it personally.

    3) UNLESS of course there was a contract (like the one that appears to have been signed) removing my liability for doing so. Then I wouldn't EXPECT to pay for it, but even if it was insured, that would mean SOMEBODY is responsible for paying for it.

    4) $300 is god damned chump change to OneReel, and this is a shady way of dealing with an artist. Pay what you promised, then fucking bill me if you want your money so bad.
