    "Is Mad Rad still relevant [to the Seattle hip hop conversation]?" yes, I think so. In playing shows, they still have the ability to push other performers in the rap scene to elevate their live sets, and Smoov continues to innovate in the production game. I don't think much has changed for the Rad in these respects since White Gold, which I think is what Matson is saying: while they can still rock the hell out of a show, Smoov's still dope, etc., their shtick isn't as fresh and shocking as it once was, and they have struggled to grab a new foothold (here and beyond). Is that accurate?

    I don't think they really need this new foothold, only really to embrace their strengths as a party ignition service and turn as many people on to it as possible, through touring, videos, whatev. I think they've tried to change the focus of their deal from the live show to the recordings a bit, but overall, it still sounds like they simply want to have fun and make everyone who listens to their music--whether at a show or at home--dance and get stupid at parties.

    That said, I like "Epiphany", "Qu'est-ce Que C'est", and the instro on "Jungle Cat". I also like when they sing.
