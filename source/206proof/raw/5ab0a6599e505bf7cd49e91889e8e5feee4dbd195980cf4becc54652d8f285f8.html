    That KOMO shit got me heated too, but I shouldn't have been surprised.

    I really want to underscore how much TalkSick appreciated all of you in the Seattle and Northwest hip hop community. Even if the dude didn't like your music or performance, he always respected the effort and the people. TalkSick was dedicated to our scene, I mean he really loved it and made great efforts to attend various events from all elements of hip hop culture. TalkSick attended community center jams all over the region, mostly every local rap show and performed hours and hours of community service/organizing through the 206 Zulu chapter. My first impression of TalkSick from our Zulu experience is that he always showed up when people needed support. When volunteers were called for in our organizing meetings, TalkSick was usually the first person to volunteer. His dedication took a toll on his health for sure, but I take great inspiration from his passion and love for his culture. I mean dude didn't have a car and yet he never let that be an excuse. Usually first on the scene and the last brother to leave the spot. I think it's great that so many of us happily kept an eye out for my man, making sure he wasn't stranded on the other side of town. I know some of my best memories took place on the drive home with my Ahki TalkSick.

    On another note, if you can, please bring a small donation to the memorial today so we can send our brother TalkSick home the right way.Big ZoBeaconSkillz
