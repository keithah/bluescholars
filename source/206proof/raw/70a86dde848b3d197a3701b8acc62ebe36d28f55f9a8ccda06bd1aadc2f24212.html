    Ahki James TalkSick Sullivan was a proud member of the 206 Zulu chapter and a true lover of hip hop culture. Many of us had the privilege of choppin' it up with TalkSick and we all came to know him as a living hip hop historian. Blessed with a great sense of humor, refreshing candor and an unforgettable smile, TalkSick was a fixture at most Northwest hip hop events. TalkSick lived life big and was known to party like there was no tomorrow. Kool G Rap was his favorite emcee although TalkSick had a special love for hip hop from the Bay and of course Seattle. I would always get a kick out of introducing TalkSick to the town legends as my man would light up with excitement often proceeding to prove his knowledge of most any hip hop topic. If you were a hip hop recording artist or deejay, TalkSick knew your catalogue better than anybody. He was also an avid archiver of hip hop music and videos and was my personal source for any hard to find joints from years past. His blog,War of the Words is a true testament to his passion and appreciation for hip hop artists of all types.

    TalkSick's passing is a huge blow to our 206 Zulu chapter and the Seattle hip hop community because the man was a champion and defender of hip hop culture. His style was abrasive to some but his heart was huge which is why he gave so much of his time to community service through the 206 Zulu chapter.

    We members of the Universal Zulu Nation traditionally greet each other by saying Peace Ahki or Peace Malika! Out of love and respect we return the greeting by saying Ahki Peace! Through this greeting and embrace, we know we have expressed our love and appreciation for each other, just in case it is our last visit in this world.

    So with that explanation and a heavy heart I say.....

    Ahki Peace brother TalkSick, Ahki Peace!Big ZoBeaconSkillz
