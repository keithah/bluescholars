    Blue Scholars and Common Market are headed to Denver during the Democratic National Convention, joining other groups like Rage Against the Machine, The Coup, M-1 and Flobots, to take part in the historic progressive demonstrations during the DNC. All the info is below (More shows will be announced soon) .

    Sunday, August 24:
    End the Occupations/End the War March & Rally 9am - 2pm
    West Steps of the Denver State Capitol Building to the Pepsi Center

    Bands: David Rovic - State Capitol Steps, kicking of the rally
    Dead Prez - State Capitol Steps, prior to the march
    Blue Scholars & Common Market - Concert at State Capitol, after the march
    Jim Page - State Capitol Steps, during the rally

    Speakers: Ida Audeh - Palestinian Refugee, Kathleen Cleaver - Black Panthers, Ward Churchill - Long-time Author, Activist, and Scholar, Chairman Fred Hampton Jr. - Prisoners of Conscience Committee, Larry Hales - World Worker's Party, Larry Holmes - Troops Out Now Coalition, Ron Kovic - anti-war activist, veteran and author of Born On The Fourth of July, Cynthia Mckinney - Green Party United States Presidential Candidate

    ______________________________________________________________________________________________

    Tuesday, August 26th
    Tent State Music Festival
    http://tentstate.org/
    City of Ceurnavaca Park, 20th St & Little Raven(across from the Skate Park)

    Blue Scholars 9 - 10 pm
    Common Market 8:30 - 9pm
    P Nuckle 8 - 8:30 pm
    David Rovics 7:30 - 8 pm
    DJ Russh 7 - 7:30 pm
    Kombat 6:30 - 7 pm

    In a sea of tents, Tent State University will be erected in City Park, Denver. Tent State will serve as an alternative university, teaching the tactics and strategies necessary to force an end to this war, hosting guest 'professors' such as Ralph Nader and Cynthia McKinney, and hold the Town Halls used to allow everyone the ability to democratically decide the days direction.

    Tent State will serve as the music venue for the first radical music festival, the Tent State Music Festival, hosting artists such as Jello Biafra, The Coup, and some very special guests. It will host an unending list of activities and installations, such as the WARGAMES Anti-War Fashion show which aims to highlight the consequences of war on women, and Backbone Campaign's "Funeral for the Past" installation.

    Most importantly, Tent State will serve as basecamp for activists and organizations to strategize, stage, and execute actions from throughout the week. It will also house first aid tents by the Colorado Street Medics, drop off points for social media, and other tools.

    Tent State University at the DNC is part of the Alliance for Real Democracy which includes local and national groups as diverse as United for Peace and Justice, Iraq Veterans Against the War, Indigenous Support Network, local SDS chapters, World Can't Wait, CODEPINK, and others.

    A few things to consider:

    First, the Democratic and Republican parties differ only depending on what issues you're looking at and where you're looking from. If you live in Palestine, East Timor, or the Philippines for instance, you wouldn't think there was a difference between the D's or R's policies, because when it comes to US foreign policy in these countries, both the D's and the R's have equally poor policies that are harmful to a lot of people.

    Second, I believe that come 2009, the Dems will be in control of he White House, the Senate and the House, and they need to be reminded that there are people ready to hold their feet to the fire on progressive policies.

    I don't speak for Geo, Sabzi or RA. I personally will be voting for Obama and feel he is a far better choice than McCain on most issues. But I think he is far from great, and I also think the Dems have a ton of problems. The Dems control the House and Senate right now, and when Obama is in office we will see only marginally better policy. When it comes to the environment, foreign policy, trade, education, and many other issues, the Dems need to be pushed to do better. And the DNC is the start of that.

    Is this pseudo struggle? I don't think so. I've been involved with music/community organizing/ politics since the 80's, and I have seen the music community led by artists make a huge difference. We all have our own ways to make the world around us a better place. It's easy to sit back on forums and criticize how one group chooses to do that, far easier than it is to do it yourselves. The question isn't so much if being a part of demonstrations at the DNC is the right way or not, it's more about what you're doing.

    I won't pretend that in itself these shows will change the world, that would be naive. But nor is this all any of these people do. I do think it is a great chance to lend our voices to many others encouraging the Dems to pay attention to some serious issues they need to do better on. And it is a great chance to witness what I'm sure will be an historic event.

    And yes, there will be similar actions at the RNC.
