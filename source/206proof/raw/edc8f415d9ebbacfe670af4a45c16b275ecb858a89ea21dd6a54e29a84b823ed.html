    Originally I had the TLM103 going through the Avalon 737 mic pre & compressor, which was OK, but then I bought the Sony C800G and Neve 1073DPA. I retired the Neumann, rolled with the Sony and never looked back. I also only use the Avalon 737 for the compressor now, which is what the "Babyface mod" is about, it increases the attack time. It's pretty slow on the old stock model, but the mod comes standard now (black face 737). You just have to replace a capacitor anyways, pretty simple. So my chain at this point is Sony C800G > Neve 1073 mic pre > Avalon 737 compressor > Apogee Rosetta 200 > DAW all via Mogami Gold cables. That mic, pre & compressor combo is also known as the "Dr. Dre chain" cuz that's what he uses on 50 Cent and most Aftermath artists. I would really like to get a Tube Tech CL-1B tho. Replace the Avalon compressor in my chain with the Tube Tech and that's what they use to record Mariah Carey. I also want a Universal Audio LA 2A, two Weiss DS1's, a Manley Massive Passive, etc! I told you I'm a gear slut.

    As for cables, I went and bought like 200ft of Belden cable & Neutrik connectors from Westlake Electrical Supply (across the street from downtown Guitar Center) and just build them as needed. Most of my stuff is wired with Mogami Gold, especially the vocal chain, but everything else is the homemade Belden. I'm not hip to the Audio Technica cable.

    You do mastering, eh? I worked with Barry Corliss over at Master Works for about 5yrs, did about 15 albums with him, he's definitely the best mastering engineer in the NW and I consider him my mentor. These days tho, I'll give him a run for his money! lol. He's got some really nice analog hardware and I wouldn't be as good as I am if it wasn't for him lacing me up. Mastering is an art and I often find it amusing when people say they "master" but don't have a clue about the technical aspects or any guidance/experience. A lot of people think it's just "making stuff louder" but there's a lot more to it than that. I don't even know how cats can call themselves mastering something when they don't use any meters, don't understand RMS levels or have weak monitors. And if you can't mix, then you definitely can't master. You know who Bob Katz is? Bernie Grundman? If you don't, you should do some research.

    Yep, I have everything worth having software-wise. I should probably be hooking YOU up! I also have a fairly extensive sound efx collection, around 90 CD's worth. All top of the line shit. I've made some pretty dope movie sounding skits. Cats are like..."What movie did you sample that stuff from?" And I'm like..."I did all that from scratch!" The shit takes HOURS tho...and HELLA tracks. Only way you could do it on ProTools is if you have HD. I'm NOT a ProTools user BTW. I have it, but rarely work in it.

    I love shop talking with cats, it's just most of them aren't serious enough about their crafts to get into the deep technical aspects or beyond their Mbox...lol. Or they have pride issues and think they don't need advice. Shit, I'm still learning. I talk to pros online all the time. It's a perpetual learning process.Musical Madman - www.myspace.com/d_sane

    Focus on perfecting mixing first, cuz if don't know where things belong in a mix, how are you going to adjust them on a master? Trying to master without being able to mix is like trying to race a car and not knowing how to drive. How bout your monitors? Room? Converters? All these things are going to influence the mix n master too. Also, every time you change something in your audio chain, you're going to have to readjust to the new sound. You'll see as you upgrade.

    I say it all the time tho, a class/book/video is NOT going to give you a pair of ears. You gotta just have an ear for this shit. Sure you can learn the lingo, technical aspects, etc...but you still gotta rely on the most important tool and that's your ears. You can take a class on how to play basketball, but that doesn't mean you're gonna play like Kobe Bryant. Education is helpful, but ultimately you need TALENT to excel.
