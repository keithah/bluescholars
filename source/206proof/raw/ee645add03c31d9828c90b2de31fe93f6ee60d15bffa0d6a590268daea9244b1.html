    but back to the subject, the hipster thing is actually interesting, because it goes beyond what our anonymous friend was talking about.

    the common misunderstanding is that hipsters are people who simply wanna be down with something that the "mainstream" isn't up on, or are folks that put up a front like they know about something they really don't. this may or may not be the case with many hipsters. so in a sense, although i doubt anybody really understands the analogies and unfounded assumptions about me in the Parrot's post, he was right with this part.

    quote me if i've contradicted myself, but this is not, and never was, the issue i take with the hipster ass hipsters. so please disregard the weak personal attack counter-argument above. like i said before, the true meaning of hipster has been overlooked.

    i should say that our good buddy Tiles was on the right track, but he would have been better to use the link above the one he chose. that is, since we're using Wikipedia definitions:

    Hipster, as used in the 1940s, referred to aficionados of jazz, in particular a form of modern jazz called bebop, which became popular around 1940. The hipster adopted the lifestyle of the jazz musician, including some or all of the following: manner of dress, slang terminology, use of reefer and other drugs, relaxed attitude, sarcastic humor, self-imposed poverty, and relaxed sexual codes. The term eventually described many members of the Beat Generation.

    then hipster went on to colloquially mean guys in tight jeans and ironic tshirts, blah blah blah. note that one of the key points in the above definition is "self-imposed poverty." the true origin of any cultural expression comes from the working people that are forced to create in order to survive. historically, artists, actors, musicians, and the like were all poor working folks that were regarded as worthless human beings while their art was appropriated and used by the rulers and the wealthy class.

    artists create. the more that artist is integrated with a community of people, the more relevant that art is to the said community. i don't mean to presume that we come correct with our art all the time, but we try.

    the "hipster" is a member of the first string of consumers to pick up on this art. sometimes considered the "tastemakers" of the rest of the consumers--one of the most impressive deceptions of "average" people--they are the individuals who aren't engaged in the creation and dissemination of artistic product. they are the folks who have no real relationship with, or understanding of, the labor it takes to do this continually within a community. if they do labor artistically, it is, at best, a hobby. the crime, as i implied earlier, is in the way these people consider themselves the "keepers of the culture" when, in fact, all their culture is borrowed and co-opted from the people who actually create it.

    a concrete example would be an overwhelming majority of journalists.

    they have no basis criticizing art for the rest of the people, because they don't have a real relationship with the people. if they did, they would never hate.

    and i certainly don't attack plain individuals for being hipsters. if somebody takes it as a "dis," i'd just encourage them to recognize that their opinion is purely aesthetic, subjective, and individualistic.

    and whoever calls me a hipster would be right. that is, if i assumed some kind of authority over what's the hotshit in these things that i don't know much about, which is something i strive not to do. there's a lot of kids that apparently listen to us and i don't wanna stand in the way of them connecting with an artist that may speak to them positively. that's why it's nothing but love and constructive criticism for all the hard working artists in the town!
