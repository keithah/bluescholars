Angela Markado (Lino Brocka, 1980)

Posted on April 26, 2009
Filed Under Film Reviews, Philippine Cinema |

Angela Markado (1980) | dir. Lino Brocka, wr. Jose “Pete” Lacaba | 99 min | Philippines (Tagalog)

A waitress, Angela Del Mar (Hilda Koronel), struggles to support her sick mother. She gets abducted and is raped for five days by five men who tattoo their names on her back (hence the title–Angela, the Marked One) and attempt to sell her to a brothel. During a raid, she escapes and finds her mother has passed away. Tracking down her rapists one by one, she seeks vengeance for her brutal, violent violation.

The dude Oggs Cruz observed in his review that Angela Markado and Kill Bill (Quentin Tarantino, 2003), both female-victim vengeance stories, both took cues from Francois Truffaut’s The Bride Wore Black (1968), which I’ve yet to see. As much as I hate to say it, I enjoyed Kill Bill, cultural appropriation on steroids that it was. And as much as I hestitate to say it, I didn’t enjoy Angela Markado at all.

 

Nonetheless, it’s a superior film. I suspect that director Lino Brocka and writer Jose Lacaba intended no enjoyment in watching this film, even when victim gets some get back through bloodshed. After a mundane, standard melodrama set-up that mirrors Brocka’s previous urban-poor realist work, we’re subjected to witnessing Angela’s rape in a long, discomforting scene. Without spoiling the ending (it’s no spoiler if you’re familiar with Brocka’s work though), I’ll just say that though justice is only half-served, the victim/victimizer righteous/evil lines get blurred, and no convenient resolution comes. 

In Brocka’s Manila, violence is violence. In Tarantino’s wherever, violence is a giddy spectacle. It’s far more easier to laugh than to empathize. It’s far more difficult to portray than to mock. Both films may have bit hard from another. But Brocka chewed and spit the shit back out while Tarantino done asked for seconds and thirds, drizzled it with hot sauce, swallowed, shit it out and ate it again. And that’s the tragedy and triumph of Angela Markado, filmed with the amount of money it took to film 30 seconds of Kill Bill, committed to a realism devoid of irony. Reminding us that rape, as experienced by countless Filipinas such as the recent “Nicole” Subic case, is not entertainment, and revenge is, more often than not, a fantasy.

Oh, I heard they might do a remake of this film.
