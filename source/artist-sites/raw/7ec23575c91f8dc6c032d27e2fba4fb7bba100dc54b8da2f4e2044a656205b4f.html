All the Real Girls (David Gordon Green, 2003)

September 5, 2009

All the Real Girls (2003) | dir. David Gordon Green | wr. Paul Scheider & David Gordon Green | 108 min | USA

Everything is staged in a David Gordon Green film. Beautifully. His characters and settings might be naturalistic but he runs their stories through a poetic filter, speaking lines worthy of a bad poetry reading, photographed with an auteur’s precision. Sprinkled conservatively with lowbrow humor, occasional melodrama and a raspy indie-folk soundtrack. I was floored by his debut feature, George Washington (2000), and have been entertained but unimpressed by his recent turn towards Apatowism (Pineapple Express, 2008) but somehow, I missed his 2003 gem All the Real Girls.

Simple plot: Paul (Paul Schneider), the town player, falls for his best friend Tip’s (Shea Whigham) virgin sister, Noel (Zooey Deschanel). Tip starts beefing with Paul, Paul falls in love, Noel complicates things. Lovestruck (or is he?), Paul confides in a cast of cute supporting players: his equally emotionally distraught father, his dad’s adopted Asian daughter Feng Shui (yeah, i know), Noel’s little brother who has down’s syndrome. Its all set in a rural North Carolina town where many cinema-friendly props (such as a broken piano amongst a pile of junk in a lush meadow) and settings (the cotton mill Noel works at, countless rivers, lakes and mountains) happen to be.

Paul Schneider and Zooey Deschanel’s honest, quiet and often (intentionally?) awkward performances are magnetic. At least until a little more than halfway through, when the playfulness and uncertainty of their relationship suddenly turns into conflict and inevitability. The great supporting cast gets pushed to the side. Which is a shame, especially when a hilarious dude called Bust-Ass (Danny McBride, in his first acting role) was just about to steal the show. Had I seen this film when it first came out, I would’ve stood up, pointed at Bust-Ass and said, this guy needs his own show! And now he does, playing Kenny “Fuckin” Powers on HBO’s Eastbound & Down, which David Gordon Green also directs. Which is a dope show. But I’d personally love to see more David Gordon Green do his thing on the big screen more often.
