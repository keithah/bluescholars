MUSIC VIDEOS: J. Cole “Who Dat” (& A Brief History of the One-Shot Rap Video)

June 30th, 2010 | 6 Comments »

When done right, the one-shot music video is the purest and highest expression of the form (when done wrong, nothing screams “amateur” louder). No artificial effects, no ADD-inducing editing, no unnecessary bullshit. It’s such a fitting video style for a genre of music as straightforward as rap is. A juxtaposition of visual continuity with lyrical randomness. You’d think there’d be hundreds of one-shot rap videos like there are for other genres. Maybe, despite every rappers fantasy (including mine) to do a music video in one take, it’s actually rare to find a director who can pull it off. J. Cole & director BBGun (who also directed Asher Roth’s one-shot video “Muddy Swim Trunks”) do just that, following this cinema-rap tradition with “Who Dat.” Here’s a few of my favorite one-take hip-hop videos.

Xzibit – What You See Is What You Get dir. Gregory Dark

Nevermind the fact that this is actually several shots edited together to give the illusion of uninterrupted continuity. If you remember the first time seeing this on MTV and BET back in ’98 then you remember how it blew away every other rap video airing at the time. Director Gregory Dark, a former porn director dubbed the “Martin Scorsese of Erotic Thriller,” did the damn thing here. I guarantee you that every director of every one-shot rap video since ’98 has seen this and went “I’ma do that too.”

Denizen Kane – Holding Up The Wall dir. Jason Mateo

A grand statement made by the simplest of means, framed effectively without the “look I was filmed in one take!” pretension present in most one-take videos and, most of all, leaving the viewer with the lasting image of an Oscar Grant protest poster.

Shabazz Palaces – Bellhaven Meridian dir. Khalil Joseph

The crown of em all. There’s no rappers rapping into the camera in this one. Just a slow stroll through Watts, filmed in a black and white stylistic homage to Charles Burnett’s Killer of Sheep (1977), one of my favorite American films.

Check out Complex Mag’s list of one-take rap videos (even though more than half of em weren’t filmed in one take). And if you’re up to it, check out Russian Ark (2002), a 99-minute movie filmed in one take.
