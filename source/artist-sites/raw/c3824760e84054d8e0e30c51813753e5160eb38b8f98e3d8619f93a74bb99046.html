Persepolis (Vincent Paronnaud & Marjane Satrapi, 2007)

Posted on June 27, 2009
Filed Under Iranian Cinema |

Persepolis (2007) | dir. Vincent Paronnaud & Marjane Satrapi | 93 min | France

The most educational and entertaining film/video about Iranian society I’ve ever seen is a cartoon. No, I’m not trying to trivialize the film or its subject matter in the least. I’m merely reflecting on the irony. That despite all the “realism” about Iran that we Western audiences have been exposed to, whether it be mainstream news outlets or, to a lesser extent, films by Iranian directors (most notably Abbas Kiarostami, whose work I love and admire), an art form traditionally looked upon as fantasy, or more condescendingly, “for children” is the medium through which illustrator/writer/director Marjane Satrapi does what no other Iranian artist has done or is currently doing.

Persepolis, based on her two best-selling autobiographical graphic novels, is as much an indictment of the Iranian revolution of 1979 as it is a lament for the the leftist movement that worked tirelessly to oust the Shah only to have power co-opted by rightist Islamic fundamentalists who turned on them. The film does an admirable job of translating the simple, black and white aesthetic of the comics to the screen, though it loses some of the literary nuances, most especially the little glimpses of Marxist humor embedded in the narration. As much personal as it is political (only through artifice can you really separate the two), Persepolis is engaging, bold, even humorous in its attempt to explain history through the lens of an eccentric child of revolutionaries. With all the shit going down in Iran these days, it would benefit many Americans, especially those who have jumped on the “Democracy in Iran!” bandwagon, to put some work into finding out what they’re actually protesting. Persepolis would be a good starting point.
