Z (Costa-Gavras, 1969)

December 12th, 2008 | No Comments »

Z (1969) | dir. Costa-Gavras | 122min | France (French w/ English subs)

Today marks day 7 of youth/student riots in Greece following the murder of 15-year old Alexis Grigoropoulos by Athens police. The wave of incredible photos coming from Athens evokes Z (1969), Greek filmmaker Costa-Gavras’s political thriller inspired by the murder of left-wing leader Gregoris Lambrakis, set against the backdrop of massive street protests and popular struggle against a totalitarian regime. 

A thinly-veiled fictional story, Z is set in an unnamed country (but obviously Greece), where chaos has claimed the city following the assassination of a popular political leader. An fence-riding magistrate (Jean-Louis Trintignant) probes for information in a maze of uncooperative government officials and cover-ups. The crime-investigation narrative is standard fare but the screenplay, based on Vasilis Vasilikos’s novel, makes excellent use of the conventional template with intriguing exchanges, suspenseful pacing and dark humor satirizing right-wing bureaucracy. The film opens with a disclaimer: “Any resemblance to real events, to persons living or dead, is not accidental. It is DELIBERATE.”

I’ll write more on this later. In short, go watch it.
