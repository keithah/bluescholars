The t-shirt design illustrates the ‘Sole Clap.’ It is taken from a specimen illustration drawn by one of Captain James Cook’s notes of his first visit to Pepelo Islands. The ‘Sole Clap’ is a traditional male war dance of the Sosisi tribe – located on the southern islands of Pepelo. The ‘clap’ is the act of slapping the hands together and on various parts of the body while hopping on one leg at a time. It is said that the movements look similar to that of a headless chicken. It is usually performed around the village fire just hours before battle. Some anthropologists have argued that it was initially an act of slapping the mosquitoes off their bodies. Others have argued that this is not true because females have not yet been seen performing the dance.


Iosefatu Sua has been studying the people, culture, and environment of the Pepelo Islands for over a decade. It is a tight group of small tropical islands located deep in the South Pacific seas located south of Hawai’i and north of the Marquesas. According to ancient legend, this island was believed to be the home of the polynesian gods. It is an island that has never been seen from an aircraft. To find it is to sail by canoe or a ship with sails. Sailors believe the sound of motored modern day ships are too loud and cover up the cries of the Manu Ata Ata – the guardian birds of the islands. Legend says the first peoples of the island were drawn to the beautiful mating calls of these birds and discovered the vast group of islands while searching for Palagi - The Great White Whale.

 Sua resides in Seattle and describes his style as "street influenced fine art that deals with culture, identity and expressionism." Raised with roots in Samoan, New Zealand and American culture, he is currently focusing on the idea of taking creation myths from the Pacific Islands, Asia and other non-Western cultures to inspire new myths, create and expand on them.
