The Hip Hop Word Count: Blue Scholars Approved

January 31, 2011

We wanted to let everyone know about this proposed project, The Hip Hop Word Count: A Searchable Rap Almanac, that takes being a hip-hop head to brainiac status. This guy Tahir Hemphill is the mastermind behind the project, which he describes thusly:

How can analyzing lyrics teach us about our culture?

The Hip-Hop Word Count locks in a time and geographic location for every metaphor, simile, cultural reference, phrase, meme and socio-political idea used in the corpus of Hip-Hop.

The Hip-Hop Word Count then converts this data into explorable visualisations which help us to comprehend this vast set of cultural data.

This data can be used to chart the migration of ideas and builds a geography of language and is the engine for a teaching curriculum.

As students, we learn that cultures are remembered and live on through how they’re documented, be it oral tradition, historical record, or otherwise. Hip-hop as a culture has its own documentation: its lyricism. Often times, academic takes on hip-hop forgo its own self-expression and context, with a anthropological, subjective and theory-heavy approach that zaps the authenticity out of the account. This is the genius of this project: a top-level, thorough and accessible platform to let rap lyrics– and hip-hop– speak for themselves, with infinite possibilities as to how it’s applied.

As of this writing, the project needs only $2,000 more to become reality. If you got the guap, consider donating to this project. And you might even get a cool, tasty Rap Chocolate out of it.

MLK Day (Mon, 1/17) Workshop: End Police Violence, Deportation and Suvelliance Now!

January 13, 2011

Four seconds. That’s how long it took for SPD Officer Ian Birk to gun down John T. Williams in broad daylight last August 30, as evidenced in this dashboard video that was ordered released by a judge last month. This week, the inquest into this shooting began at the King County Courthouse. Since I’ve posted this video on Facebook and Twitter and even put out this open call to the mayor, we’ve been getting hit up by folks asking how they can take action.

First and foremost: stay posted on all the updates surrounding this case on our local news outlets, particularly independent sources such as The Stranger. Second, there’s been a resurgence of community forums and workshops addressing police violence. Next Monday morning on Martin Luther King, Jr. Day, prior to the annual MLK rally and march at noon, several workshops will be held at Garfield High School. We encourage folks to hit up End Police Violence, Deportation and Suvelliance Now!, conducted by Lynn Domingo in room 217.

For more info on this workshops and MLK Day events, go here.

Life & Debt & Fresh-Ass Kicks

December 9, 2010



Thanks to the good people at IHeartBlueScholars (we see you!), we just got hip to these customized quilted Nike Blazers inspired by our cut “Life & Debt.”  Mister Danny P, the mastermind behind these here kicks, explains:

“Life and Debt” is one of my favorite tracks from Blue Scholars. I listened to this song EVERYDAY for two years when I was away at college. Although it was a love song, the melody put my soul at peace during my rough two years at college, so I wanted a pair that incorporated the words “Life” and “Debt.”

Check out more work by FalseQuest, whose designs across the board are clean and subtly subversive.

And a shoutout to the documentary Life & Debt (Stephanie Black, 2001), which examines Jamaica’s neocolonial economic quandary at the mercy of international lending. Watch it if you haven’t yet, and go fight globalization by supporting local artisans and getting your kicks customized using our song titles.

(Yes, we know Nike is a globalization-benefitting multinational corporation. And so is the supplier of the computer you’re reading this blog post on.)
