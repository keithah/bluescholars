N30: A Personal History by Geo

- bluescholars Cinemetropolis, Misc

I celebrate November 30th for hella reasons.

First and foremost, everywhere Filipinos and real friends of Filipinos are, you’ll find people celebrating the anniversary of revolutionary leader Andres Bonifacio’s birthday (1863). For this reason, on this day in 1964, the national democratic youth organization Kabataang Makabayan was founded at the University of the Philippines, relaunching a movement that saw itself as a continuation of Bonifacio’s unfinished revolution to get the Philippines free from foreign domination.

In 1999, the first day of the World Trade Organization Minesterial Conference drew 50,000 protesters to downtown Seattle – an experience I witnessed firsthand and rapped about on our song 50K Deep. It was the first protest I ever attended, and none have matched that feeling since. So, for me, the “Battle in Seattle” is like the Illmatic of protests. An epic beginning to an era where the follow-ups paled in comparison in the short term but permanently changed the game in the long run.

In 2002, Anakbayan Seattle, the first overseas chapter of the Philippines-based youth and student organization, was founded. Still riding off that WTO high, jarred by the increasingly hostile post-9/11 political climate, and beginning to see the threads between our homeland and our exiled communities, I joined AB206.

Blue Scholars was founded that same year a few months earlier. Alternating between mobilizations and meetings on one hand and making music on the other, I soon realized that I wasn’t just in some local organization, but part of a mass of people all working towards the same goal. And that there were others doing similar work, such as my brothers Kiwi and Bambu. In fact, Blue Scholars and Native Guns played their first Seattle show together at AB206′s first anniversary show on November 30, 2004. You can hear N30 all over this track:

So November 30 is where it all intersects for me: the movement, the music, the fire–literally. We played a show at Holy Names Academy on November 30, 2007 and the speakers caught on fire, the first and only time that’s ever happened.

Now, it’s November 30, 2010 and it’s only fitting that the final day of writing and recording for our next album, Cinemetropolis, lands on this very day. It wasn’t planned this way but weather, work and scheduling made it happen. Or maybe I subconsciously wanted to complete recording on a day of reflection and waited til today. Whatever the case, I’m ready now. N30 for life.
