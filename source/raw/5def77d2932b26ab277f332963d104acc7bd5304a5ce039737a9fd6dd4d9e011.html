7 Questions with Gordon Voidwell

March 18, 2010

Nomenclature has been a huge theme in the life of Will Johnson, Bronx native also known as Gordon Voidwell

Gordon was Will’s middle name (after his absent father) but at the age of ten changed it to Shawn (which his mother originally wanted). In his adult years, he reclaimed the name Gordon after reuniting with his father who was once a jazz guitarist in the ’70s and ’80s.

Often compared to the likes of Prince and George Clinton, Voidwell’s sound is meant to be experimental and undefinable. But those who know what’s up hear his references – funk, boogie, pop, R&B. In short, music that you can feel if you’re alive.

Catch Voidwell at our DOUBLE FEATURE, and be sure to check back in a few days for a Gordon Voidwell remix – Sabzi laces the track with some newness! Stay tuned.

1. What’s the last movie you watched? High Fidelity (2000)

2. A short review? Couple of white dude music snobs being super heteronormative in their approach to “love” and life. In fairness, they play some good music. In the end, they all go onto create Pitchfork Media.

3. What did you think the year 2010 was going to be like 20 years ago? Never envisioned it. As a buddhist nihilist, I think of nothing beyond the present moment.

4. How did you meet Blue Scholars? J-Date…

5. Describe your stage performance in three words. THIS SHIT KNOCKS!

6. If you had to pick a film genre to classify your music, what would it be? Feminist Porn.

7. Protagonist or Antagonist? more like pro-choice or pro-life?

NEW MUSIC: Paul Valéry

March 16, 2010

“The trouble with our times is that the future is not what it used to be.” – Paul Valéry

Remember when The Future was dope? We were swerving on hoverboards in a neon-colored techno-utopia, planet hopping in spaceships, and dressing like Jesus but with pointy sunglasses.

Now, the future is a post-apocalyptic zombieland, the aftermath of some worldwide cataclysm we have yet to experience.  Some cling to the old vision of the future. Others have bought into the new one hollering all that 2012 bullsh*t.

Meanwhile miss Future herself screams at us to leave her alone so that she can party like it’s 1999, again.  And we hear her. But first, we had to eulogize her.  Last time we saw her, she was stumbling drunk on the street humming an Owl City tune that she insisted was a Postal Service song.  She didn’t believe us when we told her, and haven’t seen her since.

In her honor, we sampled that tune, and wrote this song in dedication to the once and future Future.  We miss you, but we can never go back to what we had.

7 Questions with DV One

March 15, 2010

DV One:  Veteran of the music scene in Seattle, starting as an emcee then learning to DJ and mix on his own, he’s hosted KEXP’s program Rap Attack from 1994-99, toured alongside De La Soul, Hi-Tek, and Just Blaze, and continues to do his thing today.

Catch him at the DOUBLE FEATURE on both nights – March 26th & 27th – and don’t forget to download one of his mixes here, or you can go straight to this heater, “dedicated to pimp music.”

1. What’s the last movie you watched? Waking Life (2001)

2. A short review? This movie is a thinkers movie. I always get something new out of it every time I watch it.

3. What did you think the year 2010 was going to be like 20 years ago? In 2000 I thought all computer data, watches, and technology would go haywire.  In 2012 it’ll be all over.  I thought by 2010 we’d at least have the [little] backpack jet propelled individual flying device.

4. How did you meet Blue Scholars? Ohhhh you mean how did the Blue Scholars meet me? They met me individually before they were a group. I think I met Sabzi at a DJ battle, and Geo when he was a poet for Isangmahal.

5. Describe your stage performance in three words. IN FUCKING SANE… syke you probably can’t use that, so thoroughly mind blowing

6. If you had to pick a film genre to classify your music, what would it be? Anime.

7. Protagonist or Antagonist? both.

7 Questions with Macklemore

March 12, 2010

If you’ve been following these posts for the past week, you might have noticed we’re presenting a section titled ’7 Questions with Blue Scholars’ to introduce our close friends and opening lineup for our Seattle DOUBLE FEATURE event.

We understand how boring it can be to read an interview that touches upon a musician’s bio, how long they have been an emcee, and the generic/over-used “what inspires you?” question.  What we really want to do is shine light on process; the intangible parts in between that drive us from beginning to end, versus analyzing the final product.

And with that we present Macklemore: Seattle-based emcee and spiritually connected being that is ever-present while balancing music and life’s dealings. Although, it wasn’t always this easy.  From his sophomore LP The Language of My World, to VS (his latest collaboration with Ryan Lewis), the contrasting colors of subject and lyrical evolution are nothing short of inspiring.

In this order: check The Town music video, center yourself, and prepare for his performance on Saturday, March 27th at The Showbox in Seattle.

1. What’s the last movie you watched? Movies take too long.  Nowadays I’m watching Deadwood.

2. A short review? Deadwood is a show that I watch when the rest of the world is watching Lost.  It takes place sometime in-between pilgrims and gang bangers.

3. What did you think the year 2010 was going to be like 20 years ago? 20 years ago I was 7.  The only thing “Future” I was thinking about [was] this one fine vanilla beauty named Meredith that was in the first grade with me, Bo Jackson shoes and 4 square.

4. How did you meet Blue Scholars? I first met Geo probably around 2003-ish.  I had a bootleg copy of their first album a couple months before it came out, so I was familiar with the music before I really knew them as people.  Their CD release party was at the Vesper Lounge, and there couldn’t have been more then 75 people there.  I’m pretty sure I had met Geo before that, but I was smoking weed professionally back then and my memory sucks.  I had long hair, and that is somewhat embarrassing.  When my hair gets long it is curly.  Respect.

I don’t remember when I met Saba officially, but he does have old MySpace emails I sent him using blackmailable hip-hip phrases like “we should collab” and “come on man, hit me back!”

5. Describe your stage performance in three words. Honest, Engaging, Passionate.  But put those in caps, so it should read something like HONEST, ENGAGING, PASSIONATE.

6. If you had to pick a film genre to classify your music, what would it be? Documentary.

7. Protagonist or Antagonist? I just rap.  A little of both I suppose.
