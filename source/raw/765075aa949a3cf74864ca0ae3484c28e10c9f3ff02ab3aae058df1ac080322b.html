7 Questions with Common Market

March 11, 2010

RA Scion of Common Market is a seasoned emcee.  Born in Louisville, Kentucky, moved internationally to Zambia and Greece, and then finally settled down in the Pacific Northwest,  RA has been at this game steadily, releasing LP’s since 2005 while given opportunities to tour with artists such as KRS-One.

With “abstract imagery, straight-forward slogans and…an unmistakable delivery”, there’s no need for us to convince you that Common Market is one that you won’t want to miss.

As one of our special guests for our upcoming DOUBLE FEATURE at The Showbox on March 26 & 27 (did you purchase your tickets yet?), Common Market was willing to share a few thoughts on the film ‘Belly’, their energetic stage performances and advocacy of polyandry.

Keep both eyes and ears open for a new project, titled ‘Victor Shade’, alter ego for RA Scion.

1. What’s the last movie you watched? Belly (1998)

2. A short review? It’s kinda like Coming To America in reverse – Queens get the money! Hype Williams does his thing here, and I don’t think Kurt Loder’s contribution to the film’s credibility as first-rate cinema could ever be overstated.  Must see.

3. What did you think the year 2010 was going to be like 20 years ago? I thought it would be significantly warmer than this.

4. How did you meet Blue Scholars? Dave Meinert’s Queen Anne Beer Summit, ca. 2005.

5. Describe your stage performance in three words. Out. Of. Breath.

6. If you had to pick a film genre to classify your music, what would it be? Animated Short.

7. Protagonist or Antagonist? I think women should have the right to wed many husbands, so whichever one that is, that’s what I am.

7 Questions with The Physics

March 5, 2010

Performing on the second night of our ‘DOUBLE FEATURE‘ event is The Physics, made up of Seattle’s South End natives Thig Natural, Just “Justo” D’Amato, and Monk Wordsmith.  As their music touches on subjects of everyday value — such as a 9-to-5, relationships, and living a comfortable life — The Physics give us some insight into their stage performance and film genre of choice.

1. What’s the last movie you watched?
Justo: Eagle vs Shark (2007)
Thig: 500 Days of Summer (2009)

2. A short review?
Eagle vs Shark: Kind of reminded me of Napoleon Dynamite in terms of the feel and comedic angle.  It was a good movie, funny and cute.
500 Days of Summer: boy meets girl. girl breaks boys heart. boy tries to get over girl. dope movie.

3. What did you think the year 2010 was going to be like 20 years ago? Never thought about it, it seemed so distant and imaginary back then.

4. How did you meet Blue Scholars? They bought CD’s from us via myspace in 2007, I was pumped when that happened!  Then we met in person at a show in Olympia shortly thereafter I think. Funny thing is, [we] have so many mutual friends, it’s crazy we didn’t meet sooner.

5. Describe your stage performance in three words. Engaging, personal, fun.

6. If you had to pick a film genre to classify your music, what would it be? Rom-Com.

7. Protagonist or Antagonist? Both.

*Photo (from left to right): Monk Wordsmith, Just “Justo” D’Amato, and Thig Natural. Photo by Canh Solo.

7 Questions with Bambu

March 3, 2010

We’re a little over three weeks away from our Seattle ‘DOUBLE FEATURE‘ event — back-to-back shows on March 26 & 27, 2010 — and we can barely sit still.  In light of this cinematic music spectacle, we thought we should give our opening acts a proper introduction with a section titled ’7 Questions with Blue Scholars’. We asked 7 questions, and they answered.

Aside from being a father, MC (not to be confused with Father MC), and community organizer, we’re excited to share the stage with our good friend Bambu for the first night of our DOUBLE FEATURE,  Friday March 26th at The Showbox at The Market.

He shares his view on the film ‘Ip Man’, reminisces about first meeting Blue Scholars, and expresses surprise about Obama being president in 2010.

And while you’re at it, don’t forget to check the new music video ‘Slow Down’ by Bambu featuring Prometheus Brown.

1. What’s the last movie you watched? Ip Man, 2008

2. A short review? I really enjoyed the fight scenes. They went for a little historical commentary with the occupation of China by the Japanese, but that got lost after the compassionate Japanese General showed mercy to the main character in the end. But, watch it for the fight scenes!

3. What did you think the year 2010 was going to be like 20 years ago? I thought we would be somewhere between Back To The Future 2 and Blade Runner. Definitely didn’t think America would have a black president.

4. How did you meet Blue Scholars? The first time I performed in Seattle for NWFASA, back in 2003, I met Geologic (who was killing the scene as a solo artist already), and soon after, I met Sabzi (who still wore button-ups to sleep) when we all performed in Florida together with 9th Wonder (inside joke). My two favorite guys to travel with!

5. Describe your stage performance in three words. Philippines Versus China.

6. If you had to pick a film genre to classify your music, what would it be? Docu-Comedy!

7. Protagonist or Antagonist? Depends on who’s watching the show…

Olympic Speedskater J.R. Celski & Blue Scholars Have A Lot In Common

February 24, 2010

The Seattle Times published a story about the bronze-medal athlete, exposing J.R. Celski’s love for underground Seattle music and his hope of spreading the word via his Twitter account.

“I really would like to see it put on the map, and one of the ways that’s going to happen is if people talk about it.”

Dare we say a Blue Scholars x Celski collab? It’s not impossible.

FADER has a guy that wrote something cool

February 23, 2010

(i’m not sure who this is a picture of)

sometimes, we talk about hip-hop.  i mean, it makes sense considering… you know.

usually, a discussion about hip-hop ends up attracting a whole lot of irritating questions about defining hip-hop, keeping it real, mainstream vs. underground, etc etc like flies on poo.

somebody named Peter Macia recently wrote something for FADER.  i’m not sure if it’s supposed to be a book review of David Shield’s Reality Hunger, or if talking about that book is a vehicle for him to just share his opinion.  either way, it’s worth a look-see.

in his editorial, Peter isn’t fist-in-the-air about golden-age hip-hop, nor does he really try to defend whatever people attempt (and fail) to call mainstream rap these days either.  instead, he kinda just does this and asks everybody on all sides to cool it with the deb(h)ate that goes nowhere.  i like that.

“No one should ever be called a faggot for listening to Gucci Mane rather than Young Jeezy, that doesn’t even make sense. In fact, let’s just stop calling people faggot in general. Herb is funnier anyway, and I say that having several members of my family named Herbert, none of whom are herbs.”

80′s (and 90′s) Babies (Not So) Secret Show

February 21, 2010

our friend Jordan hit us up a little while ago about doing a mini shindig as a fundraiser for Haiti.  when he used the words “themed house party” and “basement in the U-District” to describe what he had in mind, we said “yes please!”

it was Friday night at Theta Chi.  the house was packed and we raised a lot of money.  the line up included Macklemore, Brothers From Another, Antbeezy, and INGLISH.

take note of the mutli-vintage nature of my jacket.

i must say that was arguably one of the most fun and energetic shows i’ve had the privilege of being a part of.  the space was small, the crowd was super hype, and we debuted a bunch of new material.  i even played some solo joints.

keep an ear open to the literal, digital, and proverbial streets this year because we plan to do more unpublicized themed house party type events in dank basements.  the next one may be in Renton, Kent, Shoreline, South End… hmm…
